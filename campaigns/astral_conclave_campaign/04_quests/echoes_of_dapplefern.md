# Echoes of Dapplefern

## Summary

The Shadow Fangs destroyed Moonpaw's village and sold its captives into different networks. Their leaders are gone, but one debt from the raid remains unfinished: an ancient fey hunter called [[The Thorn-Crowned Stag]] was promised a family of exceptional Tabaxi quarry in exchange for hiding the raiders' tracks through the Verdant Expanse.

Moonpaw escaped the raid. His wife and child were delivered as the Stag's trophies, alive and trapped in the Stag's fey domain, the **Gloamfold Chase**. At the time, the Stag did not know Moonpaw existed; it considered them two interesting prizes among many.

The Stag learned of Moonpaw only recently, when Tabaxi survivors he freed from the Shadow Fangs reached Dapplefern and told the village how he had saved them. The Stag overheard every story told inside its own realm. It now suspects that Moonpaw is the runner worth waiting for, but it does not take stories at face value. It wants proof before it offers the First Race.

The hidden [[Dapplefern Refuge]] is already trapped inside the Stag's forest-realm by the same old agreement. It can become an ally, but Chief [[Sable Reedwhisper]] will not ask anyone to risk a race until the party understands that victory frees the entire village while failure leaves everyone trapped.

## Objective

Win the First Race to free Dapplefern from the Stag's forest-realm, prove that Moonpaw's wife and child are alive in the Gloamfold Chase, and earn Dapplefern's trust without treating its people as rewards.

## GM Run Material

Use [The First Race](echoes_of_dapplefern_first_race.md) for the full hunt: declared rules, clocks, terrain zones, hazards, capture rules, and encounter options.

## Key NPCs

- **Moonpaw:** The Tabaxi player character. His wife and child are the Stag's living trophies; their emotions, memories, and eventual reunion remain player-led.
- **The fey player character:** They have heard of immortal fey shapeshifters like the Stag: beings too powerful to defeat directly, who endure by turning boredom into games, stories, and bargains. They can interpret the Hunt's customs and negotiate as an equal participant, but do not decide their court, patron, or obligations without the player's input.
- [[Sable Reedwhisper]]: Dapplefern's chief, who protects the refuge before considering any alliance.
- [[The Thorn-Crowned Stag]]: Ancient fey hunter and holder of the unpaid debt.
- **The family members:** Alive in the Gloamfold Chase, but unable to leave until the debt is resolved. Their condition, wishes, and response to reunion need player approval.

## Locations

- [[Dapplefern Refuge]]: The hidden Tabaxi refuge in the Veilroot Basin.
- **The Hunt Clearing:** A moonlit arch of living roots at the edge of the basin, where the Stag declares its races and victory opens a true path out of the realm.
- **The Gloamfold Chase:** A fey echo of the destroyed Tabaxi village and its surrounding forest, preserved as an endless hunt rather than a prison with ordinary walls.
- **The Fang Cairn:** A ruined Shadow Fang waystation where the original bargain was witnessed and where its surviving record can be found.

## The Unpaid Hunt

### What the Shadow Fangs Promised

Years ago, a Fang captain asked the Thorn-Crowned Stag for hidden paths through the Verdant Expanse. The Stag granted them in exchange for a precise payment: a family of swift Tabaxi quarry, their memories of home, and the promise that one escaped runner would eventually return to complete the hunt. Moonpaw's village was one such raid.

Moonpaw's wife and child were delivered, but Moonpaw was not. The Stag considered their capture complete until the surviving Tabaxi told Dapplefern about Moonpaw. It could not find him before then: it is not all-knowing, and the old bargain carried no name or trail for him. Now it has heard enough to be interested, but interest is not recognition. Fey bargains care about the shape of a promise, not moral fairness.

### Why the Stag Has Not Taken Everyone

The Stag is an immensely powerful shapeshifter, but it is constrained by the agreement and by fey law:

- It can hunt only a person who is **named, marked, invited, or follows its trail**. It cannot simply march through Dapplefern and seize everyone.
- The Stag's forest-realm loops every exit back into itself. It may shape paths, weather, and signs inside the realm, but it must recognize a formally won race and open a true exit.
- The Stag may send signs beyond the roots: white-antler tracks, velvet moths, a familiar voice calling from the mist, or a recovered token from someone lost.
- It may not hunt Dapplefern indiscriminately. It needs an eligible quarry and a declared course; otherwise, it has no sport and no claim.

This makes the threat visible and fair. Dapplefern survives through watchfulness, rules, and shared labor, but cannot escape until someone wins the Stag's game.

### How the Stag Hunts

The Stag does not use soldiers or cages. It creates a story people can be lured into completing:

- A survivor hears a missing loved one call from beyond the roots.
- A lost family token appears on a trail that did not exist yesterday.
- Velvet moths settle on a marked person's bedroll and form an arrow at dawn.
- A quarry sees a perfect path through the forest, but only while alone.

The target is not forcibly teleported. They must cross a marked boundary, speak an offered name, accept the token, or follow the trail three times. A character who knows the rules can resist, interrupt, or deliberately use the lure to enter the Chase on their own terms.

## Quest Flow

### Act I: Sylas's Feather

When Moonpaw considers a rapid move toward [[Aetheris Island]], Sylas's owl leaves a Tabaxi token wrapped in a dark green feather. The feather points not north, but toward the Veilroot Basin. Sylas may say:

> "The storm is not the first thing hunting your companion. Follow the debt before it learns your names."

Sylas does not explain the contract or command the party to go. He provides a warning and a direction; the choice remains theirs.

### Act II: Dapplefern's Closed Door

Sylas's owl leads the party through a hidden fey crossing into the Veilroot Basin. Freed Tabaxi may vouch for them, but Sable grants only temporary shelter. The village is in a tense new-moon watch after a child found a white-antler track inside a decoy camp. The sign appeared only after Moonpaw entered the Basin, making it clear that the Stag has finally found the runner it lost. The party soon discovers that its own route back is gone.

Sable refuses help with Aetheris for three reasons:

- The refuge cannot risk scouts while every route out remains under the Stag's control.
- The party's arrival may have brought the one runner the Stag has waited years to hunt.
- The Tabaxi character's family is not proof that anyone else should risk their family.

Sable asks the party to help with one immediate act of protection: identify a Hunt mark, safely return a missing scout, or stop a frightened resident from following a false call. This earns a conversation, not an alliance.

### Act III: Learn the Rules of the Hunt

The party needs the original terms before it can stop the Stag. The Fang Cairn contains a physical remnant of the pact: a thorn-bound ledger page, a horn fragment, or a witness-stone that remembers the spoken bargain.

Use a three-part investigation:

| Lead | What it reveals | Complication |
|---|---|---|
| Dapplefern elders | The refuge's threshold laws and the marks that precede a claim | Some elders prefer hiding forever to confronting the Stag. |
| The Fang Cairn | The exact payment, the original raid route, and the missing final clause | The ruins shift at dusk, placing intruders on a Hunt trail. |
| A captured Hunt sign | How a moth, token, or antler print carries a name into the Gloamfold | Touching it carelessly marks the investigator. |

The fey player character can contribute without being made the solution. They may recognize hospitality language, identify an invitation disguised as a threat, ask an old favor for a clue, or safely address a lesser Hunt spirit. Ask the player which of these fits their fey connection.

### Act IV: The Witness Run

Before the Stag accepts Moonpaw as the runner it has heard about, it sets a short objective battle called the **Witness Run**. This is not the First Race and cannot free Dapplefern. The Stag wants to see whether Moonpaw and his companions can make a difficult situation into a good story.

### The Penning of Bramblemaw

The Stag releases **Bramblemaw**, a huge fey forest predator of bark, matted fur, and curved root-antlers. Bramblemaw is dangerous but not evil; it is a denizen playing its role in the Stag's test. The party may kill it or subdue it; the Stag wants to see which kind of ending they choose.

The Stag's instruction is simple: **"Bring it home, or end its run. Only do not bore me."**

#### Map Setup

Build a roughly `120 ft. x 120 ft.` forest clearing. The extra distance matters: Bramblemaw should be able to threaten a bell-ringer without immediately reaching the Hunt Ring, and the party should have room to use cover, create diversions, and set up forced movement.

- Put **Bramblemaw** at the center on a raised mossy mound.
- Put the **Hunt Ring** against the north edge: a `20 ft.`-diameter circle of white roots with one open living-wood gate.
- Put the **Moon-Bell** against the south edge: a heavy bronze bell on a fallen log.
- Run a shallow, winding creek east to west through the middle third of the map; it is difficult terrain and offers partial cover from a low bank.
- Place two large fallen trunks in the northwest and southeast quadrants, thorn patches in the northeast and southwest quadrants, and three climbable root-knolls along the east side.
- Place the party in the southern third between the Moon-Bell and Bramblemaw. They can see the objective, but must cross roughly 60 feet of contested terrain to reach either the mound or the Hunt Ring.

#### Two Valid Resolutions

##### Kill Bramblemaw

- Bramblemaw is a real combatant. Reducing it to `0` hit points kills it; it does not reform during the encounter.
- The Stag accepts a hard-won kill as proof that the party is dangerous enough to interest it.
- This path is direct, but it earns no special favor. The Stag calls it a short story with a true ending.

##### Subdue Bramblemaw

- The **Moon-Bell** and **Hunt Ring** are the nonlethal tools placed visibly on the map.
- A creature adjacent to the Moon-Bell can use an action to ring it. Bramblemaw must move toward the most recent bell-ringer by the safest direct route on its next turn.
- If the bell-ringer is inside the Hunt Ring, Bramblemaw charges into the ring if it can.
- Once Bramblemaw is fully inside, a character adjacent to the gate can use an action to close it. The gate stays shut only if Bramblemaw is not conscious enough to immediately break it open; the party may need to restrain, distract, slow, or briefly incapacitate it.
- Forced movement, grapples, illusions, terrain, bait, and clever spells all count. The party does not need to reduce Bramblemaw to 0 hit points.
- A successfully subdued Bramblemaw settles, shakes leaves from its coat, and curls inside the ring. The Stag is more entertained by this solution and grants the party one truthful clue about the First Race.

#### Bramblemaw's Behavior

- It charges the bell-ringer, knocks targets aside, and tears through light cover.
- It avoids the Hunt Ring until the bell lures it there.
- It does not focus fire on unconscious characters or attempt to kill. It wants the contest to stay exciting.
- At initiative count 20, choose one forest reaction: roots rise as half cover, a trunk rolls 10 feet, a thorn patch spreads, or the creek floods a low square.

#### What the Stag Is Testing

The Stag is not asking whether Moonpaw is the fastest person in the clearing. It watches whether the party communicates, uses the terrain, takes risks for one another, and turns a predator's strength against it.

The fey player character recognizes this as a classic fey test: the Stag has made the goal concrete, declared the pieces, and expects the party to change the shape of the game rather than win through damage alone. They can warn the party that the bell and ring are the real objective.

#### Success and Failure

When Bramblemaw is killed or the gate closes around it, the Stag laughs—not cruelly—and admits that the survivor stories may have understated Moonpaw. It then offers the First Race.

If the party fails, Bramblemaw pins the Moon-Bell under a root and the Stag calls the test unfinished. No one is captured. Let the party retry after changing the terrain, asking a Dapplefern resident for advice, or negotiating one clarification of the rules. Failure costs time and pride, not the village.

### Act V: The First Race

At moonrise, the Stag turns the Hunt Clearing into the starting line and folds the forest into the Gloamfold Chase. The Gloamfold is an echo of the ruined village caught at perpetual dusk. It is beautiful, wrong, and full of paths that return to the same loss.

The Stag offers the **First Race**. It will free Dapplefern and open every path out of the forest-realm if Moonpaw and the party can carry a living ember from the ruined-village echo to the Dawnroot Bell before dawn. In return, the Stag hunts them in a deliberately limited mortal form. It declares the rules aloud before witnesses:

- It hunts at the party's level and does not use its godlike power, teleportation, or automatic charms.
- It may pursue, misdirect, and capture, but cannot kill a racer.
- The party wins by bringing the ember and every willing racer to the Dawnroot Bell before dawn.
- The Stag wins only if the ember is extinguished, willingly surrendered, or every racer is captured.

If captured, a racer is held in a temporary trophy pendant until the race is resolved; this is a setback the remaining party members can reverse, not a campaign-ending removal.

The fey player character can insist that these terms be exact, witnessed, and binding. They know the Stag's mortal race form is a voluntary limitation, not a weakness to exploit outside the declared game. They can catch a false courtesy, demand a definition of "dawn," or name a neutral fey witness, but they are not required to offer payment or solve the race alone.

During the race, Moonpaw can see and speak briefly with his wife and child. They are held by **three locks** woven into the unfinished hunt:

1. **The Name:** The Stag holds the true-name record or the memory of home that anchors each captive to the Chase.
2. **The Trail:** The family cannot leave while the route that delivered them remains part of the Hunt.
3. **The Price:** Someone must satisfy, transfer, expose, or void the original bargain before the domain recognizes them as free.

The player decides the emotional content of the scene. Make clear that winning the First Race frees Dapplefern, not the family. The Stag sees the family as the prize of a far greater hunt in its true home.

### Act VI: The Stag's Truce

If the party wins, the Stag is genuinely delighted. It acknowledges the race as worthy, releases Dapplefern from the forest-realm, and opens every trapped path back into the Verdant Expanse. It does not release Moonpaw's wife and child. Instead, it offers an honest future bargain: when the Astral Conclave crisis is over, Moonpaw may come to the Stag's true fey home and run the **Final Hunt** for them.

The Stag gives the party proof that the family lives: a dream-path, a fragment of the child's pendant, or a threshold meeting. It must keep that promise because the First Race was witnessed.

If the party loses, the Stag does not destroy Dapplefern or kill anyone. It declares the race unfinished, returns the temporary pendants at the next moonrise, and demands a rematch under a new complication. Failure costs time, confidence, or a temporary mark, but it does not remove agency or make the village disposable.

## Family Status After the Arc

At the end of this quest, Moonpaw's wife and child remain trapped in the Gloamfold Chase. This is intentional: it confirms that they are alive and gives Moonpaw an ongoing reason to remain with the party without making his family a hostage for the GM.

The Final Hunt occurs only after the Astral Conclave arc has been resolved. It takes place in the Stag's true fey home, where it can set a grander but still declared and fair race. The three locks become the structure of that later rescue:

- The Name is held in a fey archive or carried by a surviving Fang witness.
- The Trail passes through a dangerous crossing the party must map and safely close.
- The Price requires the party to outwit the Stag in its own homeland, not merely overpower it.

The family can communicate through dreams, tokens, or brief threshold meetings. They cannot be used by the Stag to issue orders to the player character.

## Dapplefern's Choice and Aetheris Support

Once the First Race is won, Dapplefern holds a council. Sable asks: **"If we show you the hidden way, what will you do when the hunter follows it home?"**

The refuge is free to say no, offer limited aid, or commit on its own terms. Possible support includes:

- A concealed Storm Harbor approach and a safe extraction shoreline
- Forged cargo identities based on old Fang codes
- Tabaxi scouts who observe routes but do not enter the fortress
- A single fey forest-path that bypasses an outer patrol, if the party earns it through the council's free choice


This help is not payment for saving Dapplefern. It is the village's free decision after seeing whether the party protects people without demanding ownership of the result.

## Obstacles

- A marked Dapplefern resident may be tempted to follow the Stag's imitation of a loved one's voice.
- Some refugees believe the safest answer is to banish all outsiders, including the party.
- The Fang Cairn's pact evidence is dangerous to study and can mark careless characters.
- The Stag offers accurate, cruelly selective truths to divide the party.
- Time pressure rises with each new moon, but no one should be taken without a telegraphed mark and a chance to intervene.

## Outcomes

- **Freed refuge:** Winning the First Race releases every Dapplefern resident from the forest-realm and restores true paths out.
- **Known living family:** Moonpaw learns his wife and child are alive and can begin preparing for the Final Hunt.
- **Fey alliance:** The fey player character gains respect, a contact, or a contested favor through their choices—not an automatic power increase.
- **Chosen Aetheris support:** Dapplefern freely provides a specific advantage for the island heist.
- **Unfinished debt:** One or more locks remain, creating a future quest without blocking the campaign's forward momentum.

## GM Guardrails

- The Shadow Fangs are the historical cause, not the adventure's active villains.
- The Astral Conclave should remain a later strategic threat, not the secret author of this fey arc.
- The Stag follows comprehensible rules. Never use it to take a character arbitrarily.
- The fey player has meaningful leverage but is not responsible for solving the quest or paying its cost.
- Moonpaw's wife and child are alive, but their emotions, relationship with him, and eventual choice of where to live remain player-led.
- Do not make the family permanently inaccessible solely to keep Moonpaw in the party. The Final Hunt must remain an achievable future promise, not a cage.

## Open Questions

- What are Moonpaw's wife and child's names, and what reunion tone does the player prefer?
- What type of fey connection does the fey player character have, and which Hunt custom would they recognize?
- Does the player want the Thorn-Crowned Stag redeemable, purely predatory, or tragically bound to the same old bargain?
- What form should the First Race take at the table: a chase through hazards, a relay, a survival race, or a hunt turned back on the Stag?
- What form of Dapplefern support would excite the table most?
