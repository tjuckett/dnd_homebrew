# Dapplefern Village Guide

## At a Glance

**Dapplefern Refuge** is a small Tabaxi survival village of roughly 45 residents in the [[Veilroot Basin]], an old-growth forest realm controlled by [[The Thorn-Crowned Stag]]. It resembles a place deep in the [[Verdant Expanse]]—towering ironwood, strangler fig, and fern trees knot their crowns into an almost unbroken roof—but it is no longer wholly part of the Material Plane. Rain and morning mist rarely reach the basin floor directly, and every path out folds back into the forest.

No one in Dapplefern owns a shop in the ordinary sense. Food, repairs, medicines, lookout duty, child care, and trail maintenance are shared work. People trade favors, time, and scarce personal keepsakes, not coins. A visitor is fed first, questioned second, and never shown the whole village at once.

## Where the Village Is

The Veilroot Basin is a bowl-shaped ravine hidden between two flooded ridges inside the Stag's Gloamfold forest-realm. Its location is protected by terrain and deliberate design:

- **Forest floor:** hanging ferns, ankle-deep runoff, and false game trails conceal every entrance.
- **Middle canopy:** homes, walkways, food drying racks, and lookout nests sit among huge interwoven roots 30 to 70 feet above the floor.
- **Root hollows:** protected spaces for the infirm, young children, food stores, the council, and evacuation tunnels.
- **Water exits:** narrow channels under the roots lead to two hidden creeks. Only trained villagers can use them without being swept into sinkholes.

The village never gives a visitor a complete route. Scouts guide newcomers through three decoy sites before allowing them to see the basin. That secrecy once protected Dapplefern from raiders; it cannot free them from the realm. Any resident or visitor who tries to leave finds the path looping back to a different part of the forest. The Stag allows no true exit until someone beats it in the First Race.

## Village Layout

Use this as a compact point-crawl map. Each area is within a few minutes of the next by village paths; ground travel is slower and exposes visitors to watchers.

| Area | What it looks like | Play use |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Fern-Mouth** | A low, flooded approach choked with broad ferns and fallen trunks. | First contact, decoy trails, and scout challenge. |
| **2. Rootspan** | Three living-root bridges meet at a high central platform. | Village gathering place, news board, and shared meals. |
| **3. Sable's Hollow** | A dry chamber inside the oldest buttress root, lit by glow-moss jars. | Council meetings, formal guests, and the chief's worktable. |
| **4. Raincatch Terrace** | Leaf funnels and clay cisterns collect clean water above the canopy mist. | Water rationing, repairs, and a quiet social space. |
| **5. Common Larder** | Smoked fish, dried fruit, mushroom shelves, grain jars, and communal cooking pits. | Supplies, food pressure, and proof that everyone contributes. |
| **6. Mender's Shelf** | A platform crowded with needles, leather scraps, medicine bundles, and salvaged tools. | Healing, gear repair, and crafting. |
| **7. Nestwalks** | Small woven homes and hammocks reached by narrow canopy paths. | Private life, family scenes, and chase terrain. |
| **8. Watcher Nests** | Camouflaged perches at the basin rim, linked by bell-cord and bird calls. | Alarms, reconnaissance, and the first sign of the Hunt. |
| **9. Hunt Clearing** | A moon-pale arch of living roots on the eastern basin edge. | The Stag manifests here to declare a race; after victory it becomes Dapplefern's first true exit. |
| **10. Underroot Ways** | Low, wet evacuation tunnels with three hidden exits. | Escape route, rescue scene, or last refuge during an attack. |

## Life in Dapplefern

### How the Village Survives

- Hunters rotate in pairs and leave no repeated trail.
- Fish traps and edible fern gardens reduce the need to travel far.
- Every able resident takes a watch shift, repairs a bridge, teaches a child, gathers food, or tends someone who cannot work.
- Personal rooms are small. Common places are carefully kept because they are where the village stays a village.
- New arrivals receive a temporary woven wrist cord. It is not a restraint; it tells scouts whom they are responsible for guiding safely.

### Useful Goods and Services

These are communal resources, not commercial storefronts.

| Resource | Steward | What the party can receive or contribute |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared table** | Pella Fernwhisk | Meals, local news, and an opportunity to help with food preparation. |
| **Mender's Shelf** | Toma Rainstitch | Repairs, herbal poultices, climbing cord, camouflaged wraps, and requests for metal tools. |
| **Trail knowledge** | Rill Underbough | Safe routes, patrol reports, and a hard test before any escort is offered. |
| **Memory hearth** | Elder Nessa Brightmoss | Survivor testimony, village history, and the rules of the Hunt. |
| **Night watch** | Kiri Quickstep | Alarms, decoy trail craft, and warnings of Hunt signs. |

## Key Residents

### [[Sable Reedwhisper]], Chief

Sable is not a monarch. The chief settles urgent decisions, speaks for Dapplefern to outsiders, and carries the burden of ordering an evacuation. Sable's private fear is becoming so cautious that the refuge slowly dies in hiding.

### Elder Nessa Brightmoss, Memory Keeper

An elderly silver-tabby Tabaxi who keeps spoken histories, children’s names, and the hospitality rules that protect the basin. Nessa remembers survivors arriving from several raids, but never claims to know Moonpaw's family unless the player approves it. She can identify a Hunt sign from its smell: rain on cold iron and crushed mint.

### Rill Underbough, Scout Captain

A lean black-furred Tabaxi with a torn ear and a dry sense of humor. Rill runs the watchers and distrusts anyone who walks loudly, talks about armies, or asks for a map. Rill wants proof the party can leave no trail before offering any Aetheris help.

### Pella Fernwhisk, Table Keeper

An older orange-and-white Tabaxi who coordinates food, shifts, and practical care. Pella does not let dramatic guests skip chores. She knows every resident's allergies, griefs, and favorite meal, and notices immediately when someone is avoiding the common table.

### Toma Rainstitch, Mender and Herbalist

A young adult Tabaxi with green-dyed whisker beads and a brace of mismatched tools. Toma repairs armor, stitches climbing gloves, tends injuries, and treats the glow-moss blight near the Underroot Ways. Toma is fascinated by adventuring gear but terrified of leaving the basin.

### Kiri Quickstep, Young Watcher

An eager teenage Tabaxi who discovered the latest white-antler track. Kiri wants to prove that Dapplefern should fight instead of hide and is vulnerable to the Stag's flattering attention. Use Kiri as the first resident the party can save from a Hunt lure.

### Returning Survivors

The Tabaxi the party already freed are not automatically residents or leaders. They may be welcomed as kin, wary visitors, or people deciding whether Dapplefern is safe enough to stay. Their testimony gets the party a meal and an audience, not instant trust.

## The Chief's Introduction to the Stag

After the party proves it can respect the refuge—by helping at a watch, preventing Kiri from following a lure, or surrendering a suspicious token—Sable brings them to Sable's Hollow. Read or paraphrase:

> "The thing in these woods is not a beast, and it is not a god. It is worse than either when you forget the difference. It calls itself the Thorn-Crowned Stag. It does not take whoever it wants. It takes whoever gives it a story worth following. Your friend has been in its story for a long time."

Sable then explains that survivors told stories of Moonpaw when they reached Dapplefern: how he freed them from the Shadow Fangs, how he kept moving when others could not, and why they believe he would come for people he had never met. The Stag heard those stories. It wants to see if the runner in front of it deserves them before it offers the First Race, which can free every resident of Dapplefern from the forest-realm.

Before Sable continues, give the fey player character a private or table-facing recognition: this is the kind of immortal fey story even fey avoid. They know a direct fight is not a real option. Such beings sometimes wear a weak form for a game, but attacking the form does not make the ancient thing behind it vulnerable. The fey character can warn the party: **"We can survive its rules. We cannot survive its attention without rules."**

## How the Stag Appears

The Stag does not need to be summoned. This is its realm. Once Moonpaw reaches Dapplefern, the white-antler tracks appear at the Hunt Clearing and the Stag arrives at moonrise to announce that the escaped runner has finally returned.

The party can ask for terms, challenge unclear language, or refuse to start immediately. It cannot force the race to begin without declaring its course and prize. But it also will not open an exit by negotiation alone: a victory is the only story it finds worth accepting.

The fey player character can insist that every term be repeated in plain language and witnessed by Elder Nessa or another neutral spirit.

## The Thorn-Crowned Stag: First Impression and Voice

It first appears as a tall stag made of wet black bark, moonlit muscle, and layered fern-shadow. A crown of living thorns grows between its antlers; dark-amber pendants hang from the branches and contain the faint silhouettes of living trophies. This is only one shape. Before it is seen, it may have been the stranger on a trail, the raven in the roots, the child's voice in the rain, or a white wolf watching from the reeds.

In its chosen mortal hunt form, it looks less impossibly large and carries old scars, mud, and the fatigue of a real runner. That form can be challenged, delayed, and outmaneuvered during the race. The ancient shapeshifter behind it cannot be defeated by force.

Its voice is low, amused, and musical in an unsettling way: like wind passing through hollow wood, with a second quieter voice repeating the last word of every sentence. It never raises its voice. It laughs softly when someone surprises it, and it calls danger "a good turn in the path."

Useful lines:

> "Moonpaw. At last, the trail remembers its runner."

> "Do not mistake restraint for kindness. I restrain myself because a chase without hope is only carrying meat."

> "Run well, and I will remember you kindly. Run cleverly, and I may remember you forever."

## Running Dapplefern

- Make the community useful, stubborn, and varied. Do not make every resident grateful, traumatized, or eager for adventure.
- Let players contribute before asking for aid: mend a bridge, share food, stand watch, listen to testimony, or respect a boundary.
- Dapplefern does not become an Aetheris staging ground. If it helps later, it provides information, scouts, or an extraction route chosen by its council.
- The Stag's authority shapes the whole forest-realm. No path from Dapplefern leads out until the First Race is won.
