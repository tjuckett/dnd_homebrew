# Elemental Factions

## Overview

The elementals are not a single people with a single answer to the damage done by mortals, war-era planar sealing, and the failing balance of the Material Plane. Their central disagreement is not whether the world has been harmed. It is whether mortals can be part of repairing it.

These factions include earth, air, fire, and water elementals, their allied spirits, and elemental-touched people. Membership varies by individual and community; none of the factions speaks for every elemental.

## The Reclamation Covenant

**Common name:** the Reclaimers

The politically dominant elemental faction believes the Material Plane must be forcibly returned to balance after centuries of mortal extraction, war, industry, and interference with the elemental planes.

### Beliefs

- Mortals broke the balance and cannot be trusted to repair it while retaining unchecked power.
- Elemental presence on the Material Plane is a necessary corrective force, not an invasion.
- Mortal expansion, high-energy industry, and access to planar technology must be stopped or strictly constrained.
- Some members advocate driving mortals from damaged regions; extremist members argue that mortal civilization must be broken so the world can recover.

### Methods

- Occupy or deny access to sites of ecological and magical damage.
- Sabotage extractive infrastructure and powerful planar machinery.
- Demand restrictions on settlement, mining, and industrial growth.
- Use elemental force as leverage when diplomacy fails.

### The Forbidden Lands Deployment

The elemental forces in the [[Forbidden Lands]] were originally sent to the humanoid continent by the Reclamation Covenant. Their assignment was to keep mortal powers in check: deny expansion into damaged regions, restrain extraction and planar experimentation, and make the cost of further ecological harm impossible to ignore.

That deployment has become a major fault line within the Covenant. Many of the elementals stationed there have lived alongside mortals, witnessed both cruelty and courage, and seen communities harmed by the same planetary instability they were sent to contain. A growing number have abandoned the Reclaimer mission and joined or cooperated with the [[The Living Bridge Accord]].

This split gives the Forbidden Lands a different political texture from distant elemental leadership: old Reclaimer orders still matter, but personal experience has made simple anti-mortal doctrine much harder to sustain.

### Internal Divide

The Covenant is not automatically genocidal. Its hardliners use the language of necessary cleansing; its moderates seek containment, reparations, and binding limits on mortal advancement. The difference matters in play.

### Relationship To The Others

- Views the [[The Returning Tide]] as naive for believing open portals alone will heal the damage.
- Views the [[The Living Bridge Accord]] as dangerously willing to dilute elemental interests in exchange for mortal approval.

## The Returning Tide

**Common name:** the Returners

The Returning Tide believes the sealed elemental planes are causing a slow, systemic collapse in both worlds. The longer the planes remain cut off, the more the Material Plane loses the flows that sustain water, air, fire, earth, magic, and life—an unfolding crisis comparable to climate change.

### Beliefs

- Planar sealing was an emergency measure, not a permanent solution.
- The elemental portals must be reopened and stabilized, not merely broken open.
- Many elementals should be able to return home, while safe and limited passage remains possible afterward.
- A repaired balance requires responsible transit and restoration, not domination by either mortals or elementals.

### Methods

- Protect surviving portal sites, tuning knowledge, and elemental stabilizers.
- Pursue diplomacy with mortals willing to acknowledge the ecological cost of the seals.
- Resist both Conclave extermination plans and Reclaimer attacks that would make restoration impossible.
- Preserve communities displaced or damaged by planar collapse.

### Stonehearth

[[Stonehearth]] is aligned with the Returning Tide. Its leaders see the damage from the seals as a long-term wound to the world and favor reopening the portals safely rather than conquering the Material Plane.

**Eldara Stonewise** is the established high chieftain and primary leader of Stonehearth. She is a respected Returning Tide voice: deliberate, cautious, and willing to work with trustworthy mortals, but unwilling to risk another uncontrolled planar catastrophe.

### Relationship To The Others

- Regards the Reclamation Covenant's coercion as likely to restart the cycle of war.
- Shares the Living Bridge Accord's belief that mortals can be allies, while remaining more cautious about permanent integration on the Material Plane.

## The Living Bridge Accord

**Common name:** the Bridgeborn

The Living Bridge Accord seeks durable coexistence between elementals and mortals on the Material Plane. It believes neither exile nor domination will restore the world; people with ties to both worlds must be able to build trust, families, and institutions across that divide.

### Beliefs

- Mortals and elementals can live together if both sides reject extraction, conquest, and inherited hatred.
- Genasi are living proof that the divide is not absolute and can become diplomats, guides, and community members rather than tools or symbols.
- The first genasi communities were fostered through elemental rites and consensual mortal-elemental bonds; the Accord protects their autonomy and rejects any attempt to treat them as a program or resource.
- The portals must eventually be reopened safely, because coexistence cannot be sustained while elemental life and the Material Plane are being starved of one another.

### Methods

- Build sanctuary communities, trade relationships, and mixed mortal-elemental alliances.
- Send envoys and small parties to find allies, protect genasi, and expose threats to elemental life.
- Oppose the Astral Conclave's elemental harvesting and its use of genasi resonance as control technology.
- Offer mediation between mortal settlements, elemental communities, and the Returning Tide.

### Connection To Session 11

The new earth-genasi character's traveling party can be affiliated with the Living Bridge Accord, or can be independent travelers acting on the same ideals. They came from the elemental continent seeking the earth elemental village and the party opposing the Conclave, but did not know the party's identities or location. The Conclave captured them and exploited their mixed resonance in its incomplete elemental-control research.

### Growth In The Forbidden Lands

Many elementals in the Forbidden Lands began as Reclaimer agents sent to keep mortals from repeating the damage that destabilized the world. Living beside mortal communities has changed some of them. They have seen why accountability matters, but also why collective punishment and permanent hostility cannot create balance. The Living Bridge Accord now has a growing local base among these former enforcers.

### Relationship To The Others

- The Reclamation Covenant often calls the Bridgeborn compromised or naive.
- The Returning Tide is a natural ally on portal restoration, though the Accord wants a lasting shared future on the Material Plane rather than a simple return home.

## At-A-Glance Conflict

| Faction | Core answer | Portal position | Mortal relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reclamation Covenant** | Force a reset of the Material Plane's balance | Treats portals as strategic tools and risks | Mortals must be constrained; extremists seek to break their civilization |
| **Returning Tide** | Repair the severed planetary system | Reopen and stabilize the portals | Cautious cooperation is possible |
| **Living Bridge Accord** | Build a shared future across the divide | Reopen safely and maintain responsible passage | Coexistence and mutual institutions are the goal |

## GM Guardrails

- Do not present any faction as biologically or morally uniform.
- The Reclamation Covenant's anger has a real basis in planetary harm, even when its coercive answers are dangerous.
- The Returning Tide's restoration plan has risks; opening portals carelessly could repeat past disasters.
- The Living Bridge Accord must not make genasi responsible for resolving everyone else's conflict.
- The Astral Conclave is distinct from all three factions: it exploits elemental life and seeks control or extermination, not balance.
