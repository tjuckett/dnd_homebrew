## The Two Routes
### Route One: Force The Portal Confrontation
- The party abandons deeper investigation and descends through the spire to stop the Conclave at the portal.
- This is the route the players are most likely to choose because every delay means more citizens are taken.
- The emotional logic is strong and should be honored.
- A frontal assault against the entire controlled populace, spire security, and prepared Conclave forces is close to suicide.
- The populace is simultaneously:
  - an enemy force under command,
  - a moving wall that protects the portal,
  - a source of reinforcements,
  - and a mass of innocent hostages the party will hesitate to harm.
- The Conclave understands this and places its actual soldiers behind citizen lines.
- The encounter should therefore be an objective battle rather than a requirement to defeat everyone present.

#### Portal Assault Objectives
- Carry the Local Directive Projector to the portal and deploy its locked Emergency Dormancy Protocol.
- Protect the projector while the controlled populace outside its radius attempts to destroy it.
- Capture the Conclave phase key from its handler and use it to strip the portal's denial protection.
- Hold a control dais for `3` rounds while Stony or another technician interrupts the transfer.
- Use the wrist devices to close the portal once the `Null-Key Shroud` is disabled.
- Rescue a named group already at the threshold before the portal cycles again.

#### Why It Is Nearly Suicidal
- Killing controlled citizens makes the party's rescue effort meaningless.
- Nonlethal attacks consume actions while the Conclave continues processing captives.
- Area damage is extraordinarily dangerous in a packed intake chamber.
- The crowd can grapple, shove, restrain, identify, and exhaust the party without needing high-damage attacks.
- Conclave troops can fire from behind citizens or retreat through the portal.
- If the party becomes pinned, the portal can be used to take them as part of the harvest.

#### Keep It Playable
- Do not run hundreds of citizens as individual combatants.
- Treat the controlled populace as a battlefield hazard with clear zones:
  - **Pressing Crowd:** difficult terrain; entering or starting a turn there risks grapple or forced movement.
  - **Command Surge:** on a clock step, the crowd advances and closes one safe lane.
  - **Hostage Screen:** ranged attacks through a dense citizen zone risk hitting a civilian on a miss.
  - **Moment Of Selfhood:** disrupting a relay causes one crowd zone to stop acting for a round.
- Give the party visible mechanisms they can affect so the solution is not simply surviving impossible numbers.
- Foreshadow the danger before initiative:
  - show Conclave troops positioned behind citizen ranks,
  - show the anchors spread across the chamber,
  - and tell the party plainly that fighting the crowd head-on will bury them.

#### Direct-Route Outcomes
- **Clean success:** The portal closes, the waiting populace is freed from the immediate funnel, and the Conclave loses its escape route. Captives already taken remain missing.
- **Costly success:** The party disables the portal but suffers citizen casualties, loses access to the fleeing Conclave, or damages a vital part of the spire.
- **Partial success:** The party interrupts intake and rescues the current group, but the Conclave relocates or reopens the transfer through a secondary anchor.
- **Failure without automatic death:** The party is overwhelmed, forced through the portal, or rescued by a sudden break in the control field. This moves the campaign to the portal destination rather than ending it.

### Route Two: Break Control Before Descending
- The party uses its extraordinary position behind the throne room to reach the Continuity Audit Chamber, memory vault, or Lattice signal-analysis interfaces first.
- They accept that more citizens may cross the portal while they search for leverage.
- Possible leverage:
  - discover and detach the Local Directive Projector,
  - authenticate its factory-locked Emergency Dormancy Protocol,
  - preview the best deployment point,
  - open sealed service paths that bypass the crowd,
  - wake Veyrix or loyal Heliox systems against the Conclave,
  - or learn how to defeat the `Null-Key Shroud` before reaching the portal.
- This route turns the portal fight from nearly suicidal into a difficult but winnable confrontation.
- Its moral cost is time. Every failed check or extended scene allows another intake group to cross.

#### Control-Break Objectives
- Learn that the projector can supersede the fivefold signal inside a limited radius.
- Learn that its write-sealed command makes affected citizens and controlled Heliox release anyone they hold and shut down in place.
- Choose a deployment point that catches the largest dangerous group without exposing the device too early.
- Recruit Veyrix, Nemeris-4, or loyal spire security.
- Obtain an internal route directly to the portal-control balcony.

#### Control-Route Outcomes
- **Clean success:** The projector reaches the portal intact and shuts down the unwilling defenders at the chosen objective.
- **Costly success:** The party clears one group, but another intake cycle completes during the delay.
- **Partial success:** The projector works with reduced range or limited charge, forcing more dangerous placement.
- **Failure:** The Conclave detects the attempt, accelerates the portal, and the party must take Route One under worse conditions.

## Recommended Decision Presentation
- Let the party see both choices clearly from the audit chamber or an overlooking spire gallery.
- Show a live count of citizens approaching and crossing the portal.
- Show that the control room contains a portable local command system capable of overriding the fivefold signal at close range.
- Make the tradeoff explicit:
  - descend now and fight impossible numbers before more citizens are taken,
  - or spend precious time learning and authenticating a locked safety tool that can shut down controlled Heliox near the portal.
- Do not hide the danger of the direct assault behind vague narration.
- Do not make the control route a consequence-free correct answer.
- The real choice is lives lost now versus a better chance of saving everyone still outside the portal.

## The Local Directive Projector
### Core Concept
- The party finds an inactive device in the control room behind the throne room.
- Clear table name: `Local Directive Projector`.
- Conclave technical name: `Proximal Priority Engine`.
- It is not the source of the citywide control signal and does not contradict the party's discovery that the Central Spire contains no control emitter.
- It is a portable emergency override designed to impose one factory-locked dormancy command on controlled constructs within a limited radius.
- The five remote devices continue controlling everyone outside the field.
- Anyone caught by the deployed field receives a latched shutdown directive that persists for `10 minutes`.
- The device does not restore free will. It temporarily replaces a hostile command with compulsory dormancy.
- Its directive memory is write-sealed in hardware. Aetherbind wrist devices can authenticate deployment but cannot change the command, its targets, or its permitted behavior.

### Why The Conclave Built It
- The Conclave anticipated that the fivefold network might become unstable during final extraction.
- They needed a way to make a dangerous work zone inert if:
  - one remote device failed,
  - controlled citizens panicked,
  - portal machinery became unstable,
  - or mutually contradictory civic commands threatened to tear controlled Heliox apart.
- The Conclave deliberately write-sealed the safety order because a programmable priority engine would let anyone with stolen authority order an entire crowd to kill personnel or destroy infrastructure.
- It is normally kept inaccessible during extraction because deploying it would shut down the Conclave's controlled laborers and Heliox guards.
- The party reaches the device before the Conclave can deploy it because their infiltration remained undetected.

### Physical Description
- A waist-high brass-and-violet engine mounted in a locking cradle.
- Three folded emitter vanes surround a dark central crystal.
- Five thin needles continuously adjust toward the locations of the remote control devices.
- A detachable power core gives it limited portable operation.
- The base can fold into carrying handles or lock onto a small maintenance cart.
- Its console displays a hollow ring representing a `60-foot` field.
- Citizen silhouettes caught by that ring change color, release held objects, lower themselves into stable positions, and go dark.

### Basic Capabilities
- Radius: `60 feet` while stationary and fully deployed.
- Radius: `20 feet` while being carried or pushed.
- Portable charge: approximately `10 minutes` of travel or `8` rounds at full combat output.
- The field affects constructs already receptive to the fivefold control network.
- It does not affect ordinary organic creatures unless they carry compatible control hardware.
- The directive is fixed and cannot be edited through the console or an Aetherbind wrist device.
- An Aetherbind wrist device can authenticate the release latch even though it cannot close the hardened portal.
- The field does not affect loyal Astral Conclave personnel or any true Heliox volunteer who is not connected to the civic-control network.
- A deployed pulse latches the shutdown command for `10 minutes`; moving an inert subject outside the radius does not restore the fivefold command during that interval.

## How To Make The Solution Legible To The Players
### Do Not Gate The Core Function Behind A Roll
- The players automatically understand that this is a local command override once they inspect the active console.
- Checks reveal risks, advanced uses, and hidden Conclave work; failure should not conceal the only viable tool.
- Use three separate clues so the table does not depend on interpreting one technical description correctly.

### Clue One: The Interface Says What It Does
- When powered, the console displays:

```text
PROXIMAL PRIORITY ENGINE
LOCAL COVERAGE: 60 FEET
SUBJECT FILTER: NETWORK-RECEPTIVE CONSTRUCTS
DIRECTIVE: EMERGENCY DORMANCY / WRITE-SEALED
BEHAVIOR: RELEASE / DISARM / SHUT DOWN IN PLACE
DIRECTIVE LATCH: 10 MINUTES
STATUS: AWAITING DEPLOYMENT AUTHORITY
```

- When a party member places an Aetherbind wrist device near the console, it displays:

```text
DEPLOYMENT AUTHORITY ACCEPTED.
WARNING: DIRECTIVE MEMORY CANNOT BE MODIFIED.
```

- The display shows a construct silhouette entering the ring, releasing its weapon, and going dark where it stands.
- This communicates the intended tactic visually without an NPC solving it for them.

### Clue Two: A Recorded Field Test
- The console retains a short Conclave test simulation.
- In the recording:
  - controlled citizens march toward a destination marker,
  - a technician activates the projector,
  - citizens entering its radius stop marching,
  - release held creatures, tools, and weapons,
  - brace themselves safely,
  - and shut down in place.
- A technician drags one inert subject beyond the test field; it remains shut down.
- End the recording with:
  - `Emergency dormancy latched. Distributed civic command quarantined for ten minutes.`
- This proves both the opportunity and its limitation.

### Clue Three: The Conclave's Intended Emergency Order
- A prepared but unsent directive remains in memory:

```text
EMERGENCY DORMANCY PROTOCOL:
CEASE ALL ASSIGNED TASKS.
RELEASE ALL RESTRAINED CREATURES.
SET DOWN HELD OBJECTS AND WEAPONS WITHOUT DAMAGING THEM.
DISENGAGE MOTIVE, TOOL, AND COMMUNICATION SYSTEMS.
ASSUME A STABLE POSTURE AND ENTER SAFE DORMANCY IN YOUR CURRENT SPACE.
DO NOT MOVE, ATTACK, RESTRAIN, OPERATE, REPAIR, OR RESUME UNTIL THE SAFETY INTERVAL ENDS.
```

- The command forbids attacking Conclave personnel and also forbids attacking anchors or control stations.
- The party gains inert ground on which to fight and sabotage, but not an unwilling army.

### Optional Live Demonstration
- Use this only if the group still does not connect the clues.
- One defeated heavy guard is disabled but repairable rather than dead, or a controlled maintenance construct approaches the room.
- When it crosses the low-power test field, its current directive visibly changes.
- It stops trying to report the intrusion and says:
  - `Emergency dormancy accepted. Releasing restraints. Shutting down.`
- Its lights go dark immediately and remain dark when it is moved beyond the test radius.
- This is an in-world demonstration, not an NPC telling the players what decision to make.

### NPC Prompt If They Still Stall

  - `It cannot free them and it cannot make them fight for us. The command is burned into the core: release, disarm, shut down. Put it down near those guards and every controlled Heliox in range goes inert.`
- The meaningful player decision is where and when to deploy the limited field, not how to exploit command wording.

## Locked Emergency Dormancy Directive
- Canonical command:
  - `Cease all assigned tasks. Release all restrained creatures. Set down held objects and weapons without damaging them. Disengage motive, tool, and communication systems. Assume a stable posture and enter safe dormancy in your current space. Do not move, attack, restrain, operate, repair, communicate, or resume until the safety interval ends.`
- The directive is burned into a read-only crystal lattice and cryptographically signed by the original Heliox safety architecture.
- It cannot interpret `attack the anchor`, `seize the station`, `protect us`, or any new instruction because there is no writable command field.
- Tampering with the lattice destroys the projector's priority signature before it creates a programmable version.
- Player agency comes from deployment location, timing, carrying versus stationary radius, and whether they risk moving the device after the initial pulse.

## The Moral Pressure
- Citizens and controlled Heliox caught by the projector release what they hold and shut down in place.
- Citizens outside remain under the fivefold command to enter the portal and eliminate interference.
- Inert Heliox may become cover, block narrow paths, or lie beside dangerous machinery, but they never assist either side.
- Loyal Conclave troops may attack around the inert citizens or try to drag them beyond the battle while fighting.
- The party must shut down the portal before the projector's charge expires and the `10-minute` dormancy latch releases.
- The device solves the tactical impossibility by removing unwilling combatants, not by turning them into weapons.
- Make clear that the party is borrowing the Conclave's weapon, not curing anyone.

## Portal Encounter Using The Projector
### Battlefield Setup
- The portal occupies one end of a broad intake floor.
- The `Null-Key Shroud` projector is mounted beside or above the portal frame.
- Three physical portal anchors stabilize the opening.
- The portal requires any `2` anchors to remain active; the third is a redundant failsafe.
- Disabling the first anchor transfers its load to the other two.
- Disabling a second anchor breaks the anchor network's protective quorum and drops the `Null-Key Shroud`.
- The portal remains open but exposed, allowing an Aetherbind wrist device to issue the actual closure command.
- A Conclave handler carries the rotating phase key.
- Controlled citizens enter from two or three intake lanes.
- The party brings the Local Directive Projector down a service lift, freight passage, or sovereign-access route.

### Activation
- Deploying the projector requires one action.
- Authenticating the write-sealed safety directive requires another action unless completed in the Audit Chamber.
- Once active, all receptive constructs and citizens within `60 feet`, or entering that field later, receive the Emergency Dormancy Protocol.
- An affected creature immediately releases all grapples and restraints, ends concentration, loses its reaction, and becomes incapacitated with speed `0`.
- Remove it from initiative. It remains inert for `10 minutes` even if moved outside the field.
- They remember both periods afterward unless the control network later burns those memories.

### Device Statistics
- AC `18`.
- `50` hit points.
- Resistance to nonmagical bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage.
- Immunity to poison and psychic damage.
- A character adjacent to it can use an action and a successful DC `15` Tinker's Tools check to restore `2d10 + 5` hit points once per encounter.
- At `0` hit points, the field collapses and catches no new subjects. Creatures already affected remain under the dormancy latch for the rest of its `10-minute` duration.

### Portal Shutdown Sequence
- The wrist devices still cannot close the portal while the `Null-Key Shroud` is active.
- The portal is not emitting the control signal, but its Shroud supplies the common timing reference that keeps the five distant signals perfectly aligned at the intake floor.
- Closing the portal removes that timing reference and causes the five signals to drift out of phase locally for at least `1 minute`.
- During that interval, the civilians at the portal are no longer under a coherent combat directive, giving the party time to stop the fighting and evacuate.
- The five external devices remain active and may eventually resynchronize, so closing the portal does not permanently free Heliox.
- The Shroud depends on at least `2` functioning anchors to rotate and validate its denial keys.
- Once any `2` anchors are offline, the Shroud fails and the wrist devices can close the portal.
- Give the party two viable ways to take those anchors offline.

#### Control-Station Shutdown
1. Reach any `2` of the `3` anchor-control stations on Level `3`.
2. Use the handler's phase key or succeed on a DC `17` Arcana, Investigation, or Tinker's Tools check at each station.
3. Place the first anchor into standby. The other two take its load and the portal remains protected.
4. Place the second anchor into standby. The Shroud loses quorum and the party's wrist devices receive `EMERGENCY CLOSURE AVAILABLE`.
5. Use an Aetherbind wrist device and hold the connection for one full round to close the portal safely.

#### Physical-Anchor Shutdown
1. Reach any `2` of the `3` portal anchors.
2. Disable or destroy the first anchor using damage, tools, or appropriate magic. The portal remains stable as the other two assume its load.
3. Disable or destroy a second anchor. With only one remaining, the Shroud fails and the portal becomes vulnerable to the wrist devices.
4. Use an Aetherbind wrist device and hold the connection for one full round to close the portal.
- Physically destroying anchors is faster but makes the portal unstable during that final round.
- If the wrist-device connection is interrupted, the exposed portal surges and risks harming creatures in transit.
- The third anchor does not need to be touched once two are offline.

#### Common Final Step
- Both routes end the same way:
  - any `2` anchors are offline,
  - the `Null-Key Shroud` drops,
  - a wrist device connects to the real portal instead of the decoy layer,
  - and a party member spends one uninterrupted round completing emergency closure.
- The Conclave can try to reactivate a station, repair an anchor, break the wrist-device connection, or flee before closure completes.
- Full contingency rules and encounter plans for all six interaction locations are in [Central Spire Portal-Anchor Encounters](../01_world/locations/asmar/heliox_prime/portal_anchor_encounters.md).

## Time And Casualty Pressure
### Eight-Round Projector Clock
- `Round 1`: The field activates. Controlled Heliox in range release restraints and shut down immediately.
- `Round 2`: The Conclave identifies the override and shifts loyal troops toward the projector.
- `Round 3`: Conclave spellcasters attack around the inert Heliox. Begin the casualty clock if disabled citizens are exposed to area effects or portal hazards.
- `Round 4`: Conclave troops switch to ranged attacks against the projector and its defenders.
- `Round 5`: The portable core overheats. The field contracts to `45 feet` unless a character vents it with an action.
- `Round 6`: Veyr-Kas authorizes lethal force and accelerates the portal intake.
- `Round 7`: The field contracts to `30 feet`; previously affected citizens remain inert, but fewer new arrivals are caught.
- `Round 8`: The portable core fails unless someone expends a powerful charge, spell slot, or suitable magic item to sustain it for one additional round.

### Casualty Clock
- Use a visible `0` to `6` track rather than resolving civilian attacks individually.
- Starting at round `3`, advance it once per round while loyal defenders use area attacks around inert citizens or uncontrolled arrivals trample the shutdown bodies.
- Additional advances:
  - uses damaging area effects through the crowd,
  - allows forced movement or portal hazards to catch a shutdown group,
  - or lets the Conclave redirect an uncontrolled intake lane across the inert citizens.
- Reduce or prevent an advance when the party:
  - separates civilians and loyal defenders with terrain or walls,
  - uses healing or nonlethal crowd control,
  - or blocks an uncontrolled intake lane from reaching the shutdown citizens.
- Suggested meaning:
  - `0`: confusion, no confirmed deaths,
  - `1-2`: injuries and isolated deaths,
  - `3-4`: a serious civilian clash,
  - `5`: dozens are in immediate danger,
  - `6`: the boundary becomes a massacre unless the portal or projector is shut down immediately.

## Give Every Character A Job
- **Artificer/Stony:** authenticate, deploy, or repair the projector and perform the technical portal shutdown.
- **Construct PC:** access sovereign routes, recognize command priority, or keep the control room from locking out the device.
- **Rogue/Barbarian:** steal the handler's phase key or sabotage the Shroud console.
- **Monk:** cross the crowd, reach anchors, and interrupt ranged attackers targeting the projector.
- **Fighter:** hold the boundary against heavy constructs without cutting through controlled civilians.
- **Druid:** separate civilian groups, restrain attackers, create cover, heal casualties, or block intake lanes.
- Do not require these exact assignments; they simply ensure the encounter has useful work beyond dealing damage.

## Ways The Plan Can Go Wrong Without Ending The Campaign
- The projector is destroyed and the party must retreat through the portal or a service route.
- The projector is deployed too early and fails to catch the heaviest controlled defenders.
- The Conclave steals the projector or blocks its emitter vanes before deployment.
- Veyr-Kas sends uncontrolled reinforcements to drag the projector away from the largest dormant group.
- The portal closes, but the party loses its destination and cannot immediately pursue those already taken.
- The party saves the remaining populace but cannot prevent loyal troops from killing some evacuees in the crossfire.
- The party takes the projector through the portal, turning it into survival equipment for the next location.

## Strong Visual Beats
- As the device activates, five violet control rhythms are answered by a single sharp amber pulse.
- The first citizen touched by the pulse releases a tool, lowers into a stable posture, and goes dark.
- Another controlled citizen crosses the boundary to attack and shuts down mid-stride without striking.
- Inert constructs remain scattered around the projector while those outside the field continue advancing.
- Every few rounds, the field visibly contracts; previously affected citizens remain dormant while new arrivals get closer before shutting down.
- When the portal closes, the fivefold command loses synchronization, but dormant citizens remain safely offline until the ten-minute latch expires.

## Recommended Reveal Line
`The machine cannot free anyone. But for sixty feet and a handful of minutes, it can make the violence go quiet.`

## Recommended Explanation For The Control At The Spire
### The Fivefold Convergence
- No sixth emitter exists in the Central Spire.
- The five surviving control devices are positioned so their fields overlap across Heliox.
- The Conclave has changed their phase and timing so all five signals reinforce each other at the portal intake.
- Away from the spire, a citizen may receive one or two command layers.
- At the spire, the citizen receives all five at once:
  - motor compulsion,
  - emotional suppression,
  - route fixation,
  - identity dampening,
  - and denial of self-preservation.
- This is why the populace can be marched calmly into an obviously dangerous portal.
- Destroying one device would weaken one layer but would not end the control.
- Destroying all five is impossible within the harvest clock.
- The vulnerable point is not an emitter at the portal. It is the precision required to keep five distant signals synchronized.

## Why The Wrist Devices Cannot Stop This Portal
### The Null-Key Shroud
- The Conclave has studied how the party uses its Aetherbind wrist devices against portal infrastructure.
- They recovered transmission logs, observed prior interference, or extracted the devices' handshake pattern from compromised relays.
- This portal is wrapped in a new countermeasure called the `Null-Key Shroud`.
- The Shroud protects portal authorization and routing; it does not emit the population-control signal.
- The Shroud does not merely change the password.
- It recognizes the wrist devices' family of command signatures and diverts them into a false control layer.
- To the user, the wrist device appears to connect and issue a closure or block command.
- The real portal never receives that command.
- The portal then rotates its phase key through the five remote devices, preventing the wrist device from maintaining a valid connection long enough to force an override.

### What The Wrist Devices Can Still Do
- Read fragments of destination traffic.
- Detect the five rotating phase signatures.
- Reveal that their closure commands are being accepted by a decoy layer.
- Authenticate deployment of the Local Directive Projector's locked safety protocol.
- Identify the physical phase key carried by the Conclave portal handler.
- They are therefore still useful without functioning as a one-button solution.

### Ways To Defeat The Null-Key Shroud
- Capture the handler's physical phase key.
- Destroy the local Shroud projector attached to the portal frame.
- Force all five remote devices out of synchronization for a few seconds.
- Use the construct PC's sovereign resonance to make the portal treat the party's command as older than the denial protocol.
- Physically damage enough portal anchors that the Shroud loses its protected routing layer.
- None of these can be done safely from elsewhere in the spire using only a wrist-device command.

### Evidence The Party Can Find
- Logs showing that their wrist-device signatures have been specifically blacklisted.
- A test record labeled `AETHERBIND DENIAL - FIELD REVISION 5`.
- Failed closure requests copied into a harmless simulation while the real portal remains stable.
- Veyr-Kas instructing technicians to `let them believe the door heard them.`
- Five rapidly rotating authorization marks, each matching one surviving control device.

## The Memory Vault
### Atmosphere
- A deep cylindrical archive where memory prisms hang in darkness like frozen stars.
- Records are stored as witnessed experiences rather than written reports.
- Opening a record briefly overlays another construct's sensory memory onto the viewer.
- The vault recognizes authority but also checks continuity of self, creating a problem for the construct PC.

### What The Party Can Learn
- The construct PC once held, or was entrusted with, the Sovereign of Accord's executive resonance.
- They departed Asmar on a mission connected to the weakening ley flow, the [[Elemental Crucible]], or an external command threat.
- At some point, they concluded that their authority could be hijacked and turned against the people it was meant to protect.
- Their memory loss was deliberate at least in part.
- They did more than erase information:
  - they burned out obedience receptors,
  - broke continuity keys,
  - scrambled command routes,
  - and forced themself to rebuild as a person without an accepted master.
- The wipe disabled them and reduced their accumulated capabilities so drastically that they effectively began again from level `0`.
- Their damaged architecture may now be uniquely resistant to the Conclave's control layer.

### Memory Fragments
Use two or three, not all of them.

- The PC watches through their former eyes as two city delegations demand opposite orders. Their past self refuses to choose a winner and says, `Accord imposed by command is only conquest with cleaner language.`
- The PC seals a chamber while an unseen voice repeats an authorization phrase that is beginning to override their movements.
- The PC records a final diagnostic:
  - `Memory deletion is insufficient.`
  - `Authority is the vector.`
  - `If I remain legible to command, I remain usable.`
- The PC voluntarily lowers themself into a severance frame while another construct begs them to find a different way.
- The last intact image is of Asmar receding as the PC leaves on a mission they no longer remember.

### Do Not Answer Yet
- Who supplied the command phrase that frightened the old Sovereign?
- Whether the PC personally was the Sovereign, carried the Sovereign's delegated authority, or contains a surviving resonance imprint.
- The full purpose of the mission away from Asmar.
- Whether someone helped perform the memory wipe.
- These can remain hooks rather than being settled in one archive scene.

## Root Refusal
- `Null Sovereign Pattern` is the archive's technical term.
- `Root Refusal` is the memorable conversational name.
- It is not simple immunity and should not function as blanket protection from every enchantment or control effect.
- It is a scarred internal architecture that rejects recognized external command hierarchy.
- The Conclave graft may detect the PC as:
  - `UNASSIGNED`,
  - `ROOT MISSING`,
  - `EXECUTIVE CHANNEL SEALED`,
  - or `SUBJECT OUTSIDE CADENCE`.
- The pattern could eventually be adapted to protect Heliox's citizens from command-network control.
- A small copy might shield a room, street, or limited group.
- A citywide version may require the PC to become the living template.

## The Construct PC's Possible Exit Runway
- The prior brainstorming called for a satisfying exit within `2` to `3` sessions.
- Session 09 should reveal the possibility and its cost, not force the decision immediately.
- The strongest version remains:
  - the PC once saved themself by becoming unreachable to command,
  - and may now save Heliox by teaching its citizens the same refusal.
- Avoid presenting sacrifice as the only acceptable answer.
- Partial alternatives should be real:
  - isolate one district,
  - cleanse one synchronization anchor,
  - evacuate targeted citizens,
  - build a local dampener,
  - expose the graft so Heliox can dismantle it slowly,
  - or have the PC leave the adventuring party alive to pursue the cure.
- If the player embraces sacrifice later, a citywide broadcast could consume, erase, or permanently anchor the PC inside the Lattice.
- If the player does not, their exit can be a voluntary guardianship of the memory vault or a long-term mission to free the compromised network.

## Preservation Intake And Harvest Protocol
- The population funnel is already active when Session 09 begins.
- The Conclave has converted legitimate preservation routes and spire access procedures into a mass-intake operation.
- Public language:
  - emergency stabilization,
  - contamination screening,
  - mandatory dormancy review,
  - or preservation intake.
- Hidden purpose:
  - isolate citizens,
  - funnel them into the spire,
  - send them through the open portal,
  - and potentially remove power cores, strip useful memory or command components, or process them at the portal's destination.
- The party's belief that this is a harvest-and-withdrawal operation is well founded, but the exact fate of those who cross remains unconfirmed.
- Good dispatch lines:
  - `Preservation intake is now mandatory. Viable cores will be secured for continuity of purpose.`
  - `Unauthorized actors are to be restrained, dismantled, or delivered for review.`
  - `Civic assets will proceed to designated recovery stations. Resistance wastes the future.`
- Veyrix should be horrified by the repurposing because it transforms his doctrine of survival into a machine for liquidation.

## The Open Portal
### What The Party Knows
- It is open at or beneath the base of the spire.
- Heliox citizens are being funneled toward it.
- The Conclave appears to be cutting its losses and withdrawing.
- The party suspects the citizens themselves have become the Conclave's final harvest.

### Questions Session 09 Can Answer

- Are citizens transported alive, dismantled during transit, or delivered to an extraction facility?
- Is the portal powered by Heliox's stored crystal energy, citizen cores, or an Astral Heart mechanism?
- How many citizens have already crossed?
- Can the portal be safely closed while people are in transit?

### Possible Destinations
- A Conclave extraction vessel or mobile staging facility preparing to leave Asmar.

### Portal Complications

- The party's wrist devices can read stored destinations or intercepted traffic, but the `Null-Key Shroud` diverts their closure commands into a false control layer.

- Redirecting it could evacuate the remaining populace somewhere safe, but only if the party secures a stable destination.
- The Conclave can collapse the portal remotely once it realizes the spire has been breached.
- The portal doubles as the local command uplink, so severing one system may disable the other.
