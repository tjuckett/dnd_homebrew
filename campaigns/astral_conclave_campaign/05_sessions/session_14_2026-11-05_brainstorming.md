# Session 14 Brainstorming - The Wintervale Assault

> **GM preparation, not established canon.** Wintervale's actual defenses, civilian situation, leadership, and layout remain unknown until the party encounters or scouts them. Use this document to make the assault playable without locking in facts the players have not learned.

## What Is Known at the Table

- [[Wintervale]] is under [[Astral Conclave]] control.
- It has portal infrastructure comparable to the circle at the ruined mountain outpost.
- The party has **three weeks** until the Convergence.
- [[Stonehearth]] is sending **twenty earth elementals**.
- Warforged aid is possible, but it is not yet committed.
- The party's minimum objective is to take the town without treating its inhabitants as enemy combatants.

## Current Direction: Raid the Docked Airship

This is the active Session 14 plan and **supersedes the townwide-liberation plan below** unless the airship raid spills into a broader battle. The party intends to strike while the Conclave airship is docked. The immediate objective is to neutralize, seize, or destroy the airship and its dock—not to clear every street in Wintervale.

**Celdric** is already inside Wintervale as the party's scout. He will report what he finds before the attack and participate once it begins. His scouting should make the opening feel earned, but should not solve the encounter for the players.

### Raid Objectives

Present these as simultaneous objectives, then let the party choose their priority:

1. **Keep the airship from launching.** Secure its moorings, lift crystals, helm, or dock-side fuel/power link.
2. **Prevent a catastrophic response.** Stop the Conclave from using the ship's weapons against Wintervale, launching with captives, or triggering a self-destruct / portal escape.
3. **Take the command deck or capture its commander.** This is the best route to flight plans, Conclave orders, and the next target.
4. **Control the dock.** The earth elementals can deny streets and reinforcements while the party boards the vessel.

The mission succeeds if the ship cannot leave and the party gains a meaningful asset: the vessel itself, its records, its prisoners/cargo, or its commander. Sinking it is a valid last resort, but should cost them intelligence and mobility.

### Celdric's Scout Briefing

Before combat, give Celdric **four confirmed facts** and **one uncertainty**. Choose or roll from these; do not reveal every defense.

| Scout finding | What it changes |
|---|---|
| The ship has four visible mooring chains, but only two carry its weight while docked. | Destroying, jamming, or controlling two specific chains prevents an immediate launch. |
| The dock's arcane crane is also the ship's shore-power link. | Sabotaging it denies a weapon or speed boost, but risks a dramatic drop or power surge. |
| Cargo is being loaded from a locked warehouse. | There may be prisoners, Conclave equipment, or a reason the vessel has delayed departure. |
| A relay beacon on the dock contacts the portal network. | Disabling it delays reinforcements and cuts a potential bomb-delivery route. |
| The captain has a short-range emergency transit charm. | The commander is a capture challenge, not merely a hit-point challenge. |
| The ship's lower hold is lightly guarded but has narrow maintenance access from beneath the pier. | A stealth boarding party can begin below deck. |
| Aerial scouts circle on a predictable route. | The party gains a narrow attack window if it acts before the next pass. |

**Useful uncertainty:** Celdric cannot tell whether the most heavily guarded crate is a weapon, a prisoner transport, or a decoy. This gives the party a real choice without hiding the entire situation.

### Battlefield: Wintervale Air Dock

Use the Wintervale map as the overland layout. The **ocean lies north and west**, the regular docks sit on the **northwestern waterfront**, and the larger air dock stands on the **north shore immediately beside them**. Build a **150-foot by 120-foot** tactical battle map around the north-side air dock. The airship is docked over or just beyond the waterfront and low enough to board from the gantry, but high enough that falling remains dangerous.

```text
                   NORTH / WEST: ocean and regular docks
  [A] regular docks -- [B] Northshore Transit Warehouse -- [C] relay beacon
        |                    |                     |
  [D] waterfront yard -- [E] airdock cranes / gantry -- [F] guard post
                                      ||
                            [G] mooring chains and gangplank
                                      ||
                     [H] DOCKED AIRSHIP: upper deck / helm
                                      ||
                     [I] lower hold / engine / maintenance hatch
                    SOUTH / EAST: Wintervale streets and reinforcements
```

| Zone | Function | Encounter use |
|---|---|---|
| A. Regular docks | Secondary waterfront access and escape route | Small piers, fishing boats, slippery planks, and low cover |
| B. Northshore Transit Warehouse | Locked dockside warehouse holding the Planar Anchor before loading | Stone walls, a wide crane door, stacked crates, and a hazardous machine objective |
| C. Relay beacon | Alarm and portal-network signal objective | Elevated beacon platform; disabling it delays reinforcements |
| D. Waterfront yard | Open approach between docks and town | Carts, cargo piles, and crane shadows provide cover |
| E. Airdock cranes / gantry | Main boarding route and shore-power point | High ground; controls access to the airship |
| F. Guard post | Conclave alarm and first defense point | Clear lines toward the gantry and streets into town |
| G. Moorings | Launch-control objective | Four chain stations; use two as meaningful launch locks |
| H. Docked airship | Command, weapons, and helm | Railings, open sky, and forced movement matter |
| I. Lower hold | Engine, cargo, and stealth route | Tight interior objective; fastest route to stop launch from within |

### Run the Twenty Earth Elementals as the Dock Screen

Do not send the elementals onto a wooden gantry or into the airship. Give them the terrain work and the perimeter fight.

| Team | Five-elemental job | Result while they hold it |
|---|---|---|
| Road Team | Block the south/east streets leading from town | Town reinforcements do not enter the dock map |
| Yard Team | Secure D and protect the warehouse | The party can examine cargo or rescue captives without being surrounded |
| Beacon Team | Isolate C and support disabling the relay | Portal / signal reinforcements are delayed |
| Reserve Team | Respond to the party's call | Can brace the crane, stop a collapse, breach the guard post, or cover a retreat |

Use one group initiative per team. Each team chooses one major task on its turn: hold, block, escort, breach, or stabilize. This keeps the battle quick and makes their aid visible.

### Three Clocks to Put in View

Start clocks only when the alarm sounds or the party goes loud. Advance one clock at the end of each round unless the party spends actions to address it.

| Clock | 0 | 3 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Launch** | Ship is docked | Gangplank retracts; engine rises | Ship breaks free unless two launch locks are controlled |
| **Reinforcements** | Beacon is quiet | Garrison alerted | Portal team or town guards reach the dock |
| **Evidence / Cargo** | Cargo secure | Technicians begin purge or jettison | Critical cargo, records, or prisoners are lost / endangered |

The party needs only to manage the clocks it cares about. If it chooses the commander over cargo, let that loss create a later lead rather than declaring the raid a failure.

### Enemy Plan

The Conclave should behave like a logistics unit under attack, not a room full of enemies waiting for combat.

1. **First response:** guards hold the gangplank and alarm the dock.
2. **Second response:** technicians accelerate launch, purge cargo, or activate the relay beacon.
3. **Third response:** the commander chooses escape with the ship, takes a hostage/cargo leverage piece, or attempts emergency transit.

For a level-10 party, start with a compact force: a named captain or Rift Magister equivalent, two technicians, and 6-10 guards divided between dock and ship. Add reinforcements only if the relay clock completes. The captain's goal is to leave with the airship, not die defending a pier.

### Interactive Airship Features

- **Mooring chains:** AC and hit points appropriate to the party, but do not make breaking them the only answer. A character can lock, jam, or seize a chain station with tools, magic, or Athletics.
- **Crane controls:** an action at the controls can swing cargo as cover, block the gangplank, expose a maintenance hatch, or destabilize the shore-power line.
- **Helm:** controlling it prevents the ship from gaining altitude or steers a failed launch into a safe crash berth rather than Wintervale.
- **Lift crystals / engine:** damaging them slows the launch; stabilizing them lets the party seize the vessel intact. Use obvious escalating visual effects before any explosion.
- **Cargo winch:** can raise a PC to the deck, lower captives, or create a swinging obstacle. Make it a choice, not a trap.
- **Railings and open sky:** falling is a threat, but place nets, cargo awnings, magical handholds, or the crane hook so a single shove is not an arbitrary character death.

## The Conclave's Dock Operation

### Why the Airship Matters When the Conclave Has Portals

The Conclave can move people, messages, and ordinary bombs through its portal network. This ship is not carrying ordinary cargo.

The **Northshore Transit Warehouse**, immediately beside the air dock, holds an **Aetheric Planar Anchor**: a massive black-iron and crystal device that locks and distorts nearby portal magic. It cannot safely pass through a portal: its active resonance would cause catastrophic feedback at both ends. The airship is docked to load the Anchor and carry it to the [[Northern Base]].

- **Portal lockdown:** the Anchor can shut down Wintervale's circle and interfere with teleportation across the dock.
- **Elemental-command amplification:** coupled to the Relay Handler's geode engine, it can turn the party's earth-elemental assault force against them.
- **Rupture threat:** if deliberately overloaded, it can destroy the air dock and damage the local portal infrastructure rather than fall into enemy hands.
- **Northern Base provenance:** it is a prototype dispatched from [[Northern Base]], where larger anchors are being installed around the comet-capture infrastructure.

The party sees a wide warehouse door thrown open, a heavy loading sled beneath a shrouded ringed machine, and crane chains already hooked to its frame. It hums whenever the portal circle or an earth elemental is nearby. The party's intervention stopped the transfer before the Anchor cleared the building.

### What the Conclave Wants Tonight

1. Activate the Anchor to lock Wintervale's portal circle and suppress party teleportation around the dock.
2. Use the Anchor to amplify the Relay Handler's elemental-command engine when the earth elementals commit to the attack.
3. Depart before the party can seize the ship or identify the Anchor's Northern Base origin.
4. If defeat is certain, overload the Anchor rather than let the party capture it.

The Conclave does **not** want to flatten Wintervale. The town, air dock, and column are useful infrastructure. Their preferred response is a controlled launch, cover from the deck, and a clean extraction.

## Encounter Package: The Anchor Run

This is a challenging objective encounter for **five 11th-level adventurers**. The numbers below are intentionally compact: the danger comes from divided objectives, vertical terrain, the launch clock, and competent retreat tactics—not from flooding the map with bodies.

### Enemy Roster

| Enemy | Number | Starting position | Job |
|---|---:|---|---|
| [[Astral Conclave Rift Magister]] (operations mage) | 1 | Upper deck, beside the helm | Controls space, protects launch, and escapes only with indispensable records |
| [[Astral Conclave Sky-Captain]] (airship commander) | 1 | Upper deck, at helm and deck batteries | Boss commander; directs batteries, keeps the party divided, and protects the ship's withdrawal |
| [[Astral Conclave Null Cantor]] | 1 | Upper deck, behind cover or at the deck stair | Counters major magic, suppresses concentration, and protects the Magister |
| [[Astral Conclave Relay Handler]] | 1 | Airship deck with a signal console linked by cable to the warehouse | Activates the Anchor, commands elementals, and runs emergency extraction |
| [[Astral Conclave Veil Escort]] | 2 | One at the gangplank, one beside the Handler | Screens, shoves, and buys time rather than seeks kills |
| [[Astral Conclave Field Technician]] | 2 | Northshore Transit Warehouse | Arms the Anchor, readies it for loading, and holds the warehouse; fights only when cornered |

Run the two technicians on one shared initiative. Their first action is always mission work, not an attack. If the party reaches the airship before the alarm, allow Celdric's information to remove one escort from its starting post or let the party begin with surprise against a technician—not the whole encounter.

### Sky-Captain: Optional Identity and Mindset

Use a temporary name such as **Captain Serane Vale** if one helps the scene. The Sky-Captain is a professional logistics officer, not a cackling villain. They believe the Anchor deployment is necessary to prevent a worse Conclave failure at the Convergence. They will sacrifice the airship or bargain for time; they will not willingly destroy Wintervale or die for a prototype that can be replaced.

Their surrender condition: the airship cannot launch, the emergency portal is disabled, and at least one ally is down or captured. A captured Sky-Captain can identify the **purpose** of the shipment—feeding a larger Conclave operation—without identifying its final location unless the party gets specific leverage or evidence.

### Setup Before Initiative

- The airship's balloon is fully inflated and its lower deck is level with one airdock platform; the upper deck is reachable by stairs or climbing.
- Two of four mooring chains are true launch locks. Celdric can identify them if he gets his full scout report to the party.
- The Aetheric Planar Anchor remains inside the Northshore Transit Warehouse, secured on its loading sled; a thick signal cable runs from it to the Relay Handler's deck console.
- Once active, the Anchor blocks teleportation and portal use across the dock zone unless a creature succeeds on a DC 16 spellcasting ability check.
- The Relay Handler can couple the Anchor to the Geode Command Engine to commandeer the earth elementals.
- The central column remains structurally stable unless the party deliberately damages it. Tell the players plainly that breaking it risks the air dock and the ship together.

### Aetheric Planar Anchor

The Anchor is an explicit, visible defense—not an invisible veto of player magic. It blocks portal travel and interferes with teleportation only while active. Its location is clear: pale light leaks through the warehouse doors and travels up the cable to the Relay Handler's console. A character adjacent to the core can use an action to attempt a **DC 16 Arcana, Nature, tinker's tools, or thieves' tools** check; two successes shut it down. *Dispel magic* requires a DC 16 check. Breaking or removing the geode core also works, but risks beginning the rupture-overload clock.

**Conclave response.** The Relay Handler can spend an action at the airship's linked signal console to reactivate the Anchor after it has been disabled, unless the geode core has been removed or broken. This is an explicit choice: restore the defense, advance an escape clock, or fight.

### Starting Clocks

Place these in view when combat begins. A technician can advance **one** clock by one segment as an action. The party can reverse or freeze a clock with appropriate actions, spells, or control of its relevant station.

| Clock | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Launch** | Ship is moored | Gangplank retracts and balloon strains upward | Ship departs unless two launch locks, the helm, or lift array are controlled |
| **Crystal denial** | Housings intact | Technicians prime one housing to be jettisoned or ruined | One housing is lost or becomes a dangerous environmental hazard; repeat for the others only if the fight runs long |
| **Emergency extraction** | Handler has not begun | Handler is concentrating at the focus | A 5-foot escape portal opens for the Handler and adjacent allies until end of next turn |

Do not advance all three clocks automatically. At the end of each Conclave round, advance the **one** that best follows their current situation. That keeps the encounter tense but fair.

### Terrain Rules That Make the Two-Deck Dock Matter

| Feature | Play effect |
|---|---|
| Lower deck | The party can board here from the gangplank or maintenance access. It contains the Handler, one housing, and hard cover from cargo. |
| Upper deck | The helm, Magister, Cantor, and a clear view of the approach. Reaching it requires stairs, a climb, flight, or clever use of the crane. |
| Central stone column | Supports both decks and supplies recharge energy. It has protected control conduits at each deck; turning a conduit off disables one Conclave advantage for a round. |
| Top boarding platform | Lets the party board at upper-deck level, but begins exposed to ranged attacks and forced movement. |
| Bottom boarding platform | Safer route to the lower hold, but gives the Conclave time to advance Launch or Extraction. |
| Balloon rigging | Can be cut or tangled to reduce the ship's launch speed, but never drops the ship instantly. Telegraph the descent and give characters time to steer or evacuate. |

### Aether Deck Batteries

Two crystal-fed batteries are recessed into the upper deck's port and starboard rails. At **initiative count 20 (losing ties)**, if at least one battery is active, the ship takes one **Deck System Action**. The Sky-Captain must be able to direct it.

| Deck System Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Aether Broadside** | Target a point within 120 feet. Conclave-chosen creatures in a 15-foot-radius sphere make a Dex save: with two batteries, **DC 16, 27 (6d8) force/lightning, half on success**; with one, **DC 15, 18 (4d8), half on success**. A failure also pushes 10 feet. Do not use this to push a character over a rail without first telegraphing that risk. |
| **Gravitic Suppression** | One 20-foot square on gantry, upper deck, or lower deck becomes difficult terrain until initiative 20 next round. Hostile creatures beginning there make DC 15 Str save or their speed is 0 until the start of their next turn. |

**Each battery:** AC 16; 40 hit points; immune poison and psychic; resistant to nonmagical bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing. A creature adjacent to one can use an action and make a **DC 16 Arcana, thieves' tools, or tinker's tools** check to disable it. A technician can reactivate one disabled battery with an action. Disabling both drops the Sky-Captain's command ward.

### The Power Triad

Put three cards or markers in front of you: **AEGIS**, **ARSENAL**, and **LIFT**. The airship and airdock cannot fully power all three systems at once.

At initiative count 20, the Sky-Captain chooses the active mode. The Captain cannot choose the same mode on two consecutive rounds. Announce the next intended mode at the end of the preceding Conclave turn so the players can react to it.

| Mode | What is powered | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Aegis** | Command ward and Anchor standby field | The Anchor blocks teleportation through the dock zone. The Sky-Captain gains +2 AC until the next initiative count 20. Batteries cannot take a Deck System Action and Lift cannot advance from ship power. |
| **Arsenal** | Deck batteries | The ship takes one Deck System Action. The Anchor's anti-teleport field is suppressed and the Sky-Captain does not receive the Aegis AC bonus. |
| **Lift** | Balloon and launch array | Advance Launch by one segment. The Anchor's anti-teleport field is suppressed and the ship cannot use a Deck System Action. |

**Player counterplay:** At the lower-deck central console, a creature can use an action to make a **DC 16 Arcana, thieves' tools, or tinker's tools** check. On a success, it chooses the mode at the next initiative count 20 and locks it for that round; the Sky-Captain cannot override it. Stony has advantage on this check. On a failure, the console emits a harmless but obvious feedback burst and the Sky-Captain knows it was tampered with.

This creates a readable decision each round: the Conclave can protect itself, shoot, or leave—but not do all three. Let the party move the physical mode marker when it wins the console.

### Conclave Tactics by Round

**Round 1 — Secure the launch.** The escorts screen the gangplank with Veil Screen. The Sky-Captain uses Command Volley or Grapnel Step to create a brutal boarding problem; the Magister uses positioning or a low-cost control spell to keep the party off the upper deck. Use Gravitic Fold to pull people toward the *center* of a deck or cargo net, not off a railing without warning. The Handler begins Emergency Extraction only if the party threatens the lower deck; otherwise, the technicians advance Launch.

**Round 2 — Divide the party.** If the ship is in Arsenal mode, it fires an Aether Broadside at a cluster or uses Gravitic Suppression to lock down the easiest boarding path. If it is in Lift mode, Launch advances instead. The Sky-Captain spends legendary actions on movement and Cutlass attacks, saving Deck Command for a high-value target. The Cantor uses *slow*, a precise counterspell, or Signal Fracture against clustered casters. The technicians either retract the gangplank / advance Launch or prime a single crystal housing for denial. The Magister uses Vector Step to move a technician or the Handler away from a grappler.

**Round 3 onward — Preserve the mission.** If launch is viable, the commander commits to it. If launch is stopped, the Handler opens extraction and the Magister covers the escape. If both are stopped, the Conclave offers a short, credible bargain: safe withdrawal in exchange for leaving the ship and town intact. A refusal leads to surrender, capture, or a final attempt to destroy only the records—not a suicidal last stand.

### Crystal-Housing Complication

When a technician primes a housing, show the danger visibly: frost or static crawls across its seals, the housing hum rises, and nearby metal pulls toward it. On the following round, the party can spend an action to stabilize it using Arcana, tinker's tools, smith's tools, or an appropriate spell; Celdric or Stony can plausibly assist. On a failure or if ignored, it releases a **contained 15-foot surge**: force/lightning damage appropriate to the table, difficult terrain of crystal fragments, and no town-level explosion.

Each saved housing is a meaningful reward: evidence, a power source, or leverage. Each destroyed housing is a Conclave loss but not a campaign-ending catastrophe.

### Difficulty Dials

| If the first round overwhelms the party | If the party is winning too easily |
|---|---|
| Have one escort abandon the fight to handle civilians or cargo; do not call reinforcements yet. | Let the relay beacon complete one signal, bringing 2-3 ordinary guards at the edge of the map after 2 rounds. |
| Have the commander choose extraction over Launch, reducing deck pressure. | Have a technician complete the first Launch segment immediately, forcing an objective choice. |
| Let Celdric disable the relay beacon or reveal the true launch locks. | Have the Magister use Vector Step to relocate the Handler and a crystal key to the upper deck. |
| Make a primed housing a rescue / stabilizing problem rather than an extra damage burst. | Trigger a second objective: a cargo cage contains people, or the final crystal packet is about to transit. |
| Have one deck battery be inoperative from prior damage. | Use both deck batteries for the first Aether Broadside, then have the Sky-Captain defend the command ward. |

Avoid adding another full caster unless the party is fresh, coordinated, and has already shown it can handle the Magister and Cantor. The existing roster is enough for a hard, cinematic fight.

The Magister and Cantor are elite deployments in this encounter. The Magister's one-time *chain lightning* is a panic button for a meaningful cluster, not surprise punishment. Use the Cantor's *wall of force* to create a tactical choice or shield an objective, never to bench a character.

### Likely Entry Plans

| Player plan | Opening benefit | Pressure to apply |
|---|---|---|
| Quiet lower-hold infiltration | Starts beside engine/cargo | Must still secure an exit and stop launch |
| Crane/gantry assault | Immediate upper-deck access | Exposed to guards and the captain |
| Earth elemental diversion | Clears the gate and pulls guards dockside | The ship starts launch procedures early |
| False cargo delivery | Gets close to warehouse or gangplank | A suspicious inspector creates a short social clock |
| Magic / flight insertion | Bypasses the dock line | The party has less control over civilians and cargo below |

### Strong Endings

- **The party captures the airship:** it becomes a mobile ally asset, but it needs a crew, repairs, and somewhere safe to berth.
- **The party grounds the airship:** they take the dock and cargo, but the Conclave captain may escape through the portal network.
- **The ship escapes damaged:** the party rescues people or steals records, gaining a concrete pursuit lead.
- **The ship is destroyed:** no Conclave air asset remains at Wintervale, but loss of evidence and damage to the dock shape the town's reaction.

### Celdric in the Fight

Celdric should be a capable ally, not a sixth player character. Give him one memorable contribution:

- use star magic to expose an invisible scout or reveal the safest boarding route;
- hold the relay beacon long enough for the party to reach the ship;
- counter an emergency transit attempt once; or
- rescue a falling character or civilian.

After that contribution, keep his turn abstract: he holds a zone, protects civilians, or duels a minor threat off-screen. If he is endangered, make it a choice the party can respond to—not a sudden death used to force emotion.

## Session Goal

If the dock raid becomes a townwide fight, use the following broader framework as a fallback. Otherwise, the airship-raid material above is the Session 14 plan.

End the session with a meaningful result rather than an endless siege. The party should either:

1. liberate Wintervale and secure or disable its portal circle;
2. win a costly partial victory, such as evacuating civilians and seizing the portal while Conclave leadership escapes; or
3. be forced to withdraw after learning a concrete truth about the Conclave's local operation.

The real win condition is **control of the portal network and the people it threatens**, not simply defeating every defender.

## Suggested Truth Behind Wintervale

Use this as the default unless the table creates a better answer during play:

Wintervale is a cold-route waystation built around a prewar transit circle. The Conclave converted its old town hall and undercroft into a relay depot. It is not a major fortress; it is a practical transfer point for personnel, signal components, and small high-value cargo moving between hidden sites. The Conclave holds it through surveillance, a compact professional guard force, and the threat that the portal can bring in reinforcements or explosives.

This makes the town strategically important without requiring hundreds of enemies or turning every resident into a collaborator.

## Assault Principles

Give the players these principles through their own planning, a Stonehearth captain, or reconnaissance:

- **Do not begin with a broad earth-elemental charge.** It warns the portal crew and invites another bomb or emergency extraction.
- **Protect civilians before the main fight.** Set a visible refuge area, evacuation route, and rally signal.
- **Seize the portal circle intact if possible.** Destroying it is a fallback, not the first plan; it may be a route to Conclave sites or proof of their network.
- **Keep the earth elementals away from unstable old foundations.** They excel at closing streets, breaching a wall, and pinning defenders, but careless earth-shaping can collapse homes or the portal undercroft.
- **Expect the Conclave to purge evidence or flee.** The party should assign someone to chase the most important runner rather than assuming every enemy will stand and fight.

## Battle Plan: Operation Stone Lantern

### Phase 0: Approach and Reconnaissance

Before initiative, let the party choose one or two preparations. Each should expose a useful fact and change the coming battle.

| Preparation | Checks / tools | Success reveals or grants | Failure / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-ground watch | Perception, Survival, familiar, flight | Guard routes, bell/alarm location, and a weakly watched approach | The party loses time or a sentry becomes suspicious |
| Earth-sense survey | Earth elemental tremorsense, Stonecunning, *locate object* | Undercroft entrances, unstable ground, and whether civilians are sheltering below | A Conclave ward detects unusual movement below ground |
| Civilian contact | Stealth, Persuasion, thieves' cant, disguise | A safe rendezvous, the local resistance contact, and a warning about hostages or curfew | Contact is watched, creating an urgent extraction scene |
| Arcane survey | Arcana, *detect magic*, Stony's device | Portal power lines, the emergency shutdown, and whether the circle can receive a payload | The ward sends a short alert to the Conclave |

**Information target:** by the time combat starts, the party should know at least two of: where civilians are concentrated, how the alarm works, where the portal is, and how the Conclave will attempt to escape.

### Phase 1: The Quiet Opening

The party chooses one opening operation. Do not make them complete all three.

1. **Cut the alarm:** disable a bell tower, signal crystal, or rooftop relay. Success delays Conclave reinforcements by 3 rounds after the first open fight.
2. **Open the refuge:** secure a shrine, inn cellar, warehouse, or old gatehouse as a civilian rally point. Success prevents civilians from becoming battlefield complications and earns local trust.
3. **Seal the escape lane:** earth elementals collapse a side street or shape a stone barricade near the service exit. Success blocks the technicians' preferred retreat and forces them toward the portal.

If the opening goes loud, move immediately to Phase 2. The consequence should be an alerted enemy, not a dead-end plan.

### Phase 2: The Outer Fight

Run a single large tactical map rather than a room-by-room clearing. A useful map is **180 feet by 120 feet**, divided into these zones:

```text
                 NORTH: ridge road / outer gate
  [A] Gatehouse ----- [B] market square ----- [C] bell / relay tower
       |                    |                         |
  [D] homes + refuge -- [E] old town hall -- [F] service lane
                              |
                    [G] undercroft stairs
                              |
                    [H] portal chamber (below ground)
                 SOUTH: frozen stream / escape culvert
```

| Zone | What it does in play | Terrain / complication |
|---|---|---|
| A. Gatehouse | Main public approach; ideal for a deliberate breach | Narrow gate, raised walkways, cover for guards |
| B. Market square | Open battle space; civilians may cross it if not evacuated | Stalls provide half cover; frozen flagstones can become difficult terrain |
| C. Bell / relay tower | Alarm and signal objective | 30-foot climb; one person can act each round to signal or sabotage |
| D. Homes / refuge | Moral objective; civilian extraction | Flammable buildings and panicked noncombatants make area attacks risky |
| E. Old town hall | Conclave command post | Reinforced doors, trapped records room, roof access |
| F. Service lane | Technician escape route | Crates, carts, and a narrow passage easy for earth elementals to seal |
| G. Undercroft stairs | Chokepoint before the portal | Old masonry; careless earth magic risks a cave-in |
| H. Portal chamber | Final objective | Circle, power pylons, cover pillars, and a control dais |

**Earth elemental assignment:** split the twenty into four five-element teams. Do not run twenty individual turns.

| Team | Default order | Mechanical abstraction |
|---|---|---|
| Gate Team | Hold A and keep outside reinforcements out | Controls the gate and absorbs a guard wave |
| Refuge Team | Guard D and escort civilians | Prevents civilian casualties unless the party abandons the area |
| Street Team | Close F and deny the culvert | Stops one escape or reinforcement route |
| Reserve Team | Supports the players' chosen objective | Arrives after 2 rounds at the party's call; can breach, pin, or carry wounded |

For each team, use one group initiative and treat it as an allied unit. On its turn it can do **one** major battlefield task: hold a zone, create or remove an obstacle, pin a group of ordinary defenders, escort civilians, or make a breach. This preserves the feeling of twenty allies without turning the session into forty turns per round.

### Phase 3: The Conclave's Response

Once the alarm sounds or the first Conclave defender falls, choose the response that best matches the party's choices.

| Response | Trigger | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence purge | Party ignores town hall | A technician burns records and arms a crystal purge; the party has 3 rounds to stop it |
| Civilian lock-in | Party attacks gatehouse first | Doors to the refuge district are sealed, making civilian rescue the immediate choice |
| Portal reinforcement | Party approaches the undercroft | The circle begins charging; it will deliver a small Conclave strike team in 3 rounds unless interrupted |
| Desperate escape | Command force is losing | The leader and a technician run for the portal with a ledger, key, or prisoner |
| Structural threat | Earth shaping goes unchecked | A damaged foundation begins to collapse, requiring a quick rescue or a change of approach |

Use only one response at first. A second response is appropriate only once the party has clearly won an objective.

### Phase 4: The Portal Chamber

The portal chamber should be an objective fight, not simply a boss room. Give it three simultaneous jobs:

1. stop the portal from receiving help, a bomb, or an extraction team;
2. prevent the destruction of the local evidence; and
3. decide the fate of the Conclave commander or technician.

**Portal controls.** Place three exposed power pylons around the circle and a control dais on the far side. A character can use an action to attempt Arcana, thieves' tools, tinker's tools, or Stony's wrist device against a DC appropriate to the group. Three successes before three failures make the circle safe to shut down, lock, or redirect. A failure creates a visible complication rather than arbitrary damage: a pylon surges, the circle begins to open, or the controls demand a different approach.

**Enemy force, scalable for a level-10 party.** Begin with a named Conclave portal commander (use an existing Rift Magister or adapt one), two technicians, and 4-6 guards. Add a small portal reinforcement only if the earlier alarm was not stopped. The commander's goal is extraction or denial, not a glorious last stand.

**Bomb safety valve.** Because the Conclave already tried a portal bomb, telegraph any repeat attempt early: a mechanical ticking, a distorted circle, technicians refusing to enter, and a glowing object visible on the far side. Give the party at least one round to react. They can block the circle, redirect the object, carry it through, or flee with a clear cost.

## Likely Player Plans and How to Support Them

| If they choose... | Let it accomplish... | Then challenge with... |
|---|---|---|
| Stealth infiltration | A clean rescue or a head start on the portal | A timed evidence purge or a suspicious lieutenant |
| Full frontal assault | Rapid control of the gate and market | Civilians and the portal becoming the actual priorities |
| Deception / false parley | Access to town hall or a name and password | A social clock before the disguise is tested |
| Earth-elemental tunneling | A surprise route to the undercroft | Old foundations, buried wards, and risk to civilians above |
| Portal sabotage from range | Delay or deny reinforcements | A technician fleeing with the portable control key |

Never make a sensible plan fail because it differs from this document. Move the encounter's pressure to whichever objective they leave exposed.

## Named Roles You Can Introduce If Needed

These are optional names, not established characters.

- **Relay Prefect Vaelor Senn:** practical Conclave commander; values the network and evidence more than the town. Offers a bargain if cornered.
- **Mara Vell, Wintervale wardkeeper:** local civic keeper forced to maintain records and curfew lists; knows where people are confined and can become the party's first town ally.
- **Basalt-Captain Oren:** leader of the Stonehearth contingent; patient and disciplined, but refuses orders that knowingly bury civilians beneath a collapse.
- **The Lantern Children:** a small civilian runner network that knows roof paths, culverts, and which homes are empty. Use as a complication-free way to reward respectful contact with the town.

## Consequences by Outcome

### Clean Liberation

The party controls the portal, civilians see that the attack was aimed at the Conclave, and Wintervale becomes a forward base. Reward them with a portal destination, Conclave records, a local guide, or a small volunteer force.

### Costly Victory

The town is free, but the commander escapes or key evidence is purged. Wintervale still provides allies and shelter, but the Conclave is warned and begins hardening the next site.

### Partial Success / Withdrawal

The party saves civilians or stops a catastrophe but cannot hold the town. They gain a specific lead, a rescued witness, or proof that makes the next strike more focused. The Convergence clock continues; do not reset the campaign because one battle went poorly.

### Conclave Holds the Town

If the party loses the field, avoid capture-as-the-only-outcome. Let them retreat with a cost: Stonehearth casualties, a lost artifact, a Conclave propaganda victory, or the portal becoming harder to reach. They should retain agency and a viable next move.

## Session Pacing

1. **15-25 minutes:** war council and choice of reconnaissance/preparation.
2. **30-45 minutes:** quiet opening plus outer-fight objectives.
3. **45-75 minutes:** town hall or portal undercroft, depending on player priorities.
4. **30-45 minutes:** portal-chamber objective fight and decision about Wintervale's future.
5. **10 minutes:** outcome, casualties, recovered intelligence, and the next Convergence-clock choice.
