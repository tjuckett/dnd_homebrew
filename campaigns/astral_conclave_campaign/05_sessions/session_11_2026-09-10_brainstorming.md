# Session 11 Brainstorming - September 10, 2026

## Table-Facing Premise

The party's recoding of the warforged has broken the Astral Conclave's control over Heliox. With the city waking up and the Conclave's command structure collapsing, the party helps secure the Central Spire, learns what became of the remaining Conclave agents, and is brought to a sealed prisoner whose elemental blood was used to develop the Conclave's partial control of the four elemental guardians.

The prisoner is the new player character: an earth genasi, primarily a sorcerer with a warlock pact. Do not make the character's backstory for the player. The session should reveal only what the Conclave knows and leave room for the player to define the rest.

## Established Starting Position

- Kira's underwater base was stripped, wrecked, and abandoned after Malrec's attack. Its submarine is not salvageable.
- The party returned to [[Asmar]] and infiltrated the [[Central Spire]] again.
- They reached the broadcast system and used code tied to the Sovereign to recode the warforged.
- The recoding made the warforged immune to the Astral Conclave's control signal; it did not erase anyone's memories, force loyalty, or solve Heliox's political problems.
- The transfer left the warforged with a new resonance connection to the Sovereign. They are not a hive mind and retain independent thought, but they can sense the Sovereign's presence and inherently trust that he speaks in good faith.
- Citizens who were directly controlled have no memory of what they did, saw, or heard while the Conclave held them. They remember only the last moment before control and the moment they woke free.
- Four powerful elementals were being controlled through a separate device above the broadcast chamber. The party disrupted that system and sent one elemental to the Feather.
- The Sovereign was not sacrificed. Their player character has now voluntarily left the adventuring party to lead the warforged into their future.
- [[High Curator Malrec Voss]], Kira's former protege and leader of the Astral Conclave, remains at large.
- The Conclave's control system was a foreign graft upon Heliox's real Civic Directive Lattice. Heliox citizens and institutions should not be treated as willing Conclave collaborators merely because the city was used.

## Session Goals

- Give the city a believable, player-driven liberation without turning the session into a long mop-up battle.
- Let the citizens regain agency and participate in reclaiming their own city.
- Establish what the Astral Conclave loses, what it escapes with, and what evidence or prisoners remain behind.
- Introduce the new earth genasi as a person, not a reward or an exposition machine.
- Reveal the direct connection between the captive group and the Conclave's ability to partially control powerful elementals.
- Leave the group with two or three clear next leads: the missing genasi companions, Conclave escape routes, the captives sent through the portal, and the wider threat to the elementals.

## The Retaking Of Heliox

### What Changed When The Signal Failed

The broadcast recoding should create an immediate, visible break rather than a vague citywide victory:

- Controlled warforged stop following Conclave directives. Some freeze in confusion, some abandon posts, and some immediately turn on Conclave guards trying to force them onward.
- Directly controlled citizens have a blank interval in their memory. They may wake holding a weapon, standing in an intake lane, beside an injured neighbor, or far from where they remember being, with no idea how they got there.
- The city's legitimate emergency systems remain intact. Doors, lifts, lights, medical stations, and civil records do not automatically fail.
- The Conclave loses its largest advantage: it can no longer use citizens as obedient guards, a crowd weapon, or a labor force.
- The elementals are not automatically freed by the warforged recoding. Their control used a related but more experimental binding system; breaking its master device ends active direction but may leave them frightened, enraged, injured, or disoriented.
- The party does not need to defeat every Conclave soldier. Once the city realizes it is free, the Conclave is outnumbered, exposed, and unable to use the population as cover.

### A Three-Beat Reclamation Sequence

Run this as a short skill-and-choice sequence, with one fight only if the table wants it.

1. **Hold the Broadcast Room.** The party prevents a final Conclave purge, sabotage pulse, or attempt to restore the signal. This validates the victory from Session 10 and gives the citizens time to organize.
2. **Open the Spire.** The party and newly freed citizens open shutters, release blocked intake lanes, restore lifts, and broadcast a clear message: the control signal is broken and Conclave orders are no longer binding.
3. **Secure the City Nodes.** Local citizens, guards, and technicians reclaim the Lattice Ring's relay nodes, transit stations, and armories. The party chooses where to lead rather than attempting all of it personally.

Suggested checks: Arcana or Tinker's Tools to stabilize the broadcast hardware; Persuasion or Intimidation to stop panic and organize defenders; Investigation to find a sabotage team; Athletics to open manual shutters; Insight to identify a Conclave agent posing as a frightened citizen.

Success should mean Heliox retakes its city. A failed check costs time, damages a district system, lets a few Conclave agents escape, or causes a tense civilian confrontation; it should not undo the warforged immunity.

### Civilian Roles

Use named or improvised citizens so the victory belongs to Heliox:

- **Tavar-9, intake supervisor:** organizes a count of the missing and clears the former portal intake lanes.
- **Nemeris-4, archive custodian:** preserves records before the Conclave can purge them and identifies concealed research rooms.
- **Lethra-2, former controlled guard:** leads loyal guards through security routes, despite shame over what they did while controlled.
- **The Sovereign:** has enough authority and trust to coordinate warforged defenses, but lets the party choose the dangerous objectives. Their departure from the party is a leadership choice, not a coronation.

## Heliox After Liberation: Interim Leadership

Heliox is free of the Conclave signal, but not suddenly free of scarcity, grief, or political disagreement. The best immediate structure is a **Civic Recovery Assembly**: a temporary emergency body that restores consent and services while the city decides what lawful government should look like. It avoids making the Sovereign the automatic ruler and keeps [[Arch-Calculant Veyrix]] accountable rather than erased.

### Kira After The Loss Of Her Lab

Kira's underwater lab is gone, but Kira is not out of the story or out of options. The reclaimed Central Spire has the tools, power access, records, and Conclave hardware she needs to begin building a replacement lab. She does so as a **closely supervised, covert technical advisor** to the Continuity Cell.

- Nemeris-4 and the Continuity Cell quietly grant her limited access to a sealed industrial repair bay, materials, and power allocation—not free control of Heliox infrastructure.
- Her presence is not public. Veyrix, Nemeris-4, Dhor-8, Tavar-9, the Sovereign, and the party know; the wider city and most warforged are told only that a restricted recovery project is operating there.
- Her immediate jobs are stabilizing the warforged recoding, helping seal the Seismic Annex breach gate, and preserving/decoding Conclave elemental-control records before they fail or are sabotaged.
- Her loss matters: her specialized equipment, safe refuge, submarine, personal notes, and ability to work alone are gone. She must now ask others for space, materials, oversight, and trust.
- Kira recognizes the resonance trammel as Conclave work derived from old calibration principles she once understood. She can help diagnose or safely extract it, but cannot demand access to Stonehearth or Eldara.

The secrecy is protective, not a claim that the warforged are irrational. Many warforged have direct or inherited reasons to distrust Kira's past work with the Conclave and elemental extraction. If her location becomes known, make it a future accountability scene: the Continuity Cell must decide whether her expertise justifies continued protection, and Kira must answer for what she did rather than hide behind necessity.

Kira remains in Heliox during the party's initial trip to Stonehearth. Her history of trapping and draining elementals would make her presence an immediate diplomatic wound and distract from the rescued genasi's introduction. She sends the party with a compact diagnostic prism that Crystalmender can use to assess the trammel. If Eldara later chooses to invite Kira, that invitation becomes evidence of earned trust rather than automatic forgiveness.

### Immediate Civil Leadership: The Continuity Cell

Before the Conclave seized the city completely, a small group of Heliox officials, guards, and citizens independently recognized that the violet directives were foreign. They traced missing people, impossible maintenance orders, and falsified civic records to a hidden Conclave graft. The Conclave imprisoned or isolated them rather than risk a public alarm.

When the signal breaks, these survivors are the first people able to act with a plan. They become the **Continuity Cell**, a provisional response group—not because an old title forces obedience, but because they had already earned trust, prepared routes, and know where the Conclave hid its control systems.

| Continuity Cell member | What they discovered before capture | What they take control of now |
|---|---|---|
| **Arch-Calculant Veyrix** | The Conclave's resource models and emergency orders contradicted Heliox's real preservation forecasts. | Civil coordination, citywide resource continuity, and public confirmation that the graft was foreign. |
| **Nemeris-4** | Repeated archive queries and altered records surrounding sovereign authority and lattice maintenance. | Archives, evidence preservation, and the public record of what happened. |
| **Severance Marshal Dhor-8** | Guard rotations and security reroutes were placing noncompliant citizens in Conclave custody. | Loyal guards, armories, pursuit routes, and civilian protection. |
| **Tavar-9** | Intake lists were selecting citizens for removal rather than legitimate dormancy or civic review. | Intake lanes, missing-person counts, transit reopening, and district volunteers. |
| **A district witness** | A civilian organizer, medic, or technician who saw controlled neighbors disappear and preserved an unaltered local log. | Public trust and the citizen response; name them at the table if useful. |

The Cell was held in separate secure rooms, administrative lockouts, or forced dormancy because the Conclave could not trust them. The party's destruction of the broadcast control frees them from those restraints at the same moment the city wakes.

### The Civic Continuity Console

Kira does not make anyone obey the Sovereign. Instead, she helps the Continuity Cell use a legitimate Heliox emergency console that had been locked behind the Conclave graft.

- The console opens public emergency channels, transit routes, medical stations, armories, evacuation shutters, and civic records.
- It suppresses remaining foreign Conclave commands and flags compromised devices for shutdown.
- It requires a local three-part confirmation: civil authority (Veyrix), archive verification (Nemeris-4), and security or district confirmation (Dhor-8 or Tavar-9).
- It cannot compel a citizen, alter memories, force a vote, or make anyone attack, arrest, or obey.
- The Sovereign's resonance may help Kira bypass damaged pre-partition hardware, but it conveys no political authority over Heliox.

This gives Heliox a credible immediate command structure. The Sovereign leads and protects the newly free warforged, while the Continuity Cell opens the route to the under-spire annex, coordinates the civilian chase, and asks the party to take the urgent rescue mission.

### The Sovereign's Call To The Warforged

The shared code produces an unintended aftereffect: every recoded warforged feels the Sovereign as a steady, familiar presence at the edge of awareness. It is connection, not possession. They do not share senses or thoughts, and they can disagree with or refuse him; they simply know that his voice is real and that he is not speaking through the Conclave's control lattice.

While the Continuity Cell restores the city systems, the Sovereign makes one short call through the reopened public channels:

> "The Conclave no longer speaks for you. Protect the wounded. Secure their agents alive where you can. Hold the city for its people. You are free to choose how."

The result is immediate and visible: warforged form protective lines around civilians, disarm Conclave personnel, block their escape routes, and bring prisoners to the Continuity Cell rather than taking revenge. This is why the Conclave abandons exposed positions and retreats to the under-spire annex so quickly.

### Immediate Activation Scene

Kira explains the technical step, but the Continuity Cell activates the console in public. The Sovereign stands with them as a warforged liaison, then makes the call that rallies the newly free warforged against the Conclave's retreat.

Read or paraphrase:

> The violet commands are gone. In their place, amber lines return to the city's displays—not an order, but a question written in every open channel: **CIVIC CONTINUITY QUORUM RECOGNIZED. EMERGENCY ACCESS REQUESTED.** Veyrix, Nemeris, and Dhor place their hands on three separate consoles. One by one, the shutters unlock. Then the message changes: **NO DIRECTIVE ISSUED. LOCAL CONSENT REQUIRED.**

The Continuity Cell can then make a short public request: secure the wounded, open the transit lanes, do not punish anyone for actions taken under control, and report Conclave movement. The city responds because these people are known local leaders making concrete requests that citizens already want to fulfill.

| Role | Default holder | Authority now | Pressure or conflict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arch-Calculant** | [[Arch-Calculant Veyrix]] | Civil continuity, citywide resource decisions, and negotiations with Calyxis | His Preservation Doctrine and prior covert sabotage remain politically explosive. He must govern with oversight, not emergency fiat. |
| **Recovery Assembly chair** | Rotating chair; begin with Nemeris-4 or a respected district representative | Sets public agenda, records decisions, and prevents one office from quietly reclaiming absolute control | The city has no habit of transparent, shared emergency power. |
| **Archive and truth custodian** | Nemeris-4 | Preserves records, audits orders issued during control, and distinguishes Conclave graft from legitimate Heliox systems | Every faction wants records that make it look better. |

| **Continuity and dormancy review** | Quietus Prefect Sol-Vane, under Assembly oversight | Keeps life-support, power rationing, and voluntary dormancy systems functioning | His office is associated with harsh policy and is an easy place for hidden collaborators to hide. |

| **External liaison** | A party-selected delegate, with Kira as technical advisor when available | Communicates with Calyxis, elemental communities, and the party | Heliox needs help, but cannot appear to have been conquered by outsiders. |

### The Assembly's First Public Principles

- No citizen is punished merely for actions taken while under confirmed Conclave control.
- A controlled citizen's missing memory is evidence of harm, not evidence of guilt. The city must reconstruct events from records, physical evidence, and voluntary witness accounts.
- Every emergency order, dormancy directive, and security detention is subject to review.
- Conclave agents, coercion victims, and willing collaborators are not treated as the same category.
- The city will preserve evidence before assigning blame.
- The Sovereign's authority does not override the right of Heliox citizens to choose their own future.

## Hidden Conclave Collaborators

The end of the signal creates a dangerous ambiguity: some citizens who were never controlled will claim that they were, hoping to avoid accountability. Use this as a mystery and a social pressure point, not an excuse for mass suspicion or collective punishment.

### Three Categories To Keep Separate

| Category | What happened | How Heliox should respond |
|---|---|---|
| **Controlled** | The Conclave directly overrode their choices through the graft; they have no memory of the controlled interval. | Medical care, testimony about the moments before and after, and no punishment for compelled acts. |
| **Coerced collaborator** | They made choices under threats to family, district power, or personal safety. | Protected confession, restitution where possible, and case-by-case accountability. |
| **Willing collaborator** | They knowingly aided the Conclave for status, ideology, profit, or influence. | Evidence-based investigation, detention only when necessary, and public due process. |

### The Quiet Violet Network

Use the **Quiet Violet Network** as the local name for the remaining voluntary Conclave cell. It is not a public organization. Its members used civic positions to pass along schedules, identify dissenters, protect Conclave equipment, and normalize the language of "necessary continuity."

They now pretend that every suspicious order was signal-driven. Their immediate goal is not to retake Heliox; it is to destroy evidence, save compromised colleagues, and escape with research on elemental control.

| NPC | Public role | Actual status | What reveals them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Relay Auditor Carth-12** | Lattice technician helping restore signal nodes | Willing collaborator; routed Conclave maintenance access through harmless-looking repair orders | His private audit stamps appear on orders issued before the control signal was active. |
| **Warden Ilex-7** | Intake-lane security officer, now loudly ashamed of "what the signal made me do" | Willing collaborator; used control chaos to identify prisoners and move them into Conclave custody | Captured intake logs show he retained independent clearance and chose which civilians disappeared. |
| **Quietus Prefect Sol-Vane** | Administrator of dormancy quotas and power rationing | Ambiguous, not a default villain; he accepted Conclave data believing it would prevent collapse, then concealed how compromised it was | Give the party a choice: he can confess and cooperate, or double down and become a political obstacle. |
| **Severance Marshal Dhor-8** | Covert defense commander | Not a Conclave collaborator by default; suspicious because of his own secret sabotage campaign against Calyxis | His secrecy is a red herring and a useful warning that secrecy alone is not proof. |

### How Collaborators Avoid Persecution

- They repeat true stories of citizens who were controlled, then blur the line between compulsion and voluntary assistance.
- They exploit the memory gaps: a genuine victim cannot confirm or deny the exact orders they carried out, while a willing collaborator can falsely claim the same blank interval.
- They destroy personal logs but cannot erase distributed maintenance stamps, energy draw records, or witness testimony.
- They encourage panicked accusations against political rivals, hoping the Assembly will discredit itself in a witch hunt.
- They target Nemeris-4's archives, the captured technician, and the Seismic Annex records because those documents can distinguish the categories above.
- If cornered, they offer useful information about Malrec's escape in exchange for protection; some of it is true, some is designed to send the party after a rival.

### Run This Fairly

- Give the party evidence before a named citizen is exposed as a willing collaborator.
- A frightened or guilty citizen is not automatically a villain.
- A missing memory cannot prove either innocence or collaboration by itself; compare it with independent records and evidence.
- Let a controlled citizen make an ugly accusation without treating it as fact.
- The party can choose transparency, a quiet investigation, or immediate detention; each approach has political consequences but none should invalidate the liberation.

## The Astral Conclave's Defeat, Escape, And Capture

### Recommended Outcome: The Cell Falls Back To The Annex

The Conclave cell in Heliox should be decisively defeated without pretending the entire organization is gone.

- **Malrec escapes the city.** He is already established as far beyond the party in direct combat. His departure is separate from the local cell's retreat, through a prepared astral extraction route or remote evacuation triggered before Heliox fully locks down.
- **The surviving local cell falls back to the Seismic Annex.** They have one priority: destroy the research, retain the earth-genasi captive, and transport him to the Conclave's main base.
- **Freed citizens and guards give chase, but cannot breach the annex's automated defenses safely.** Their pursuit reveals the hidden route and proves the threat is real; it does not replace the party's role.
- **The party's urgent objective is to breach the annex, stop the purge and extraction, and free the captive.** A few agents may surrender or escape afterward, but the local operation is broken if the prisoner and meaningful evidence are saved.

### Escape Clock

Use this as the annex countdown once the Conclave begins its retreat. It is visible: warning lights change color, the party hears shutters cycle below, and the Sovereign or a technician can report the extraction charge rising.

| Step | Conclave action | Party opportunity |
|---:|---|---|
| `1` | The Conclave seals the under-spire route and activates the outer defenses. | Follow the fleeing agents, use a security route, or cut power to one defense layer. |
| `2` | Automated turrets and Conclave-patched guard robots block the civilian pursuit. | Bypass, disable, or survive the corridor while citizens hold the upper route. |
| `3` | Evidence purge begins; physical files burn and local logs start to erase. | Preserve one evidence bank or capture the purge operator. |
| `4` | The captive's restraint frame transfers to the extraction cradle. | Free the captive or disable the transport clamps. |
| `5` | The breach gate reaches full charge. | Break the gate, seize its key, or hold the room long enough to interrupt the jump. |
| `6` | The Conclave attempts to transport the captive to its main base. | This is the final round to stop the extraction. |

Make the timer urgent but fair. Telegraphed consoles, visible conduits, and a captive in sight mean the party can spend actions on the objective instead of merely trading attacks. If they stop the transport but lose the records, the new character is still freed and introduced; if they preserve records but fail to free him, the gate is the clear rescue route rather than an arbitrary disappearance.

### Evidence Left Behind

The party should recover at least three of these:

- A prisoner ledger describing the genasi group as `elemental-interface subjects`, not enemy combatants.
- Partial resonance readings that explain why the Conclave could impose commands on the four elemental guardians only imperfectly.
- A transfer record showing that the other captives were moved before the Heliox collapse; it gives a destination, codename, or transport route for a later rescue.
- Evidence that Malrec ordered the research preserved or evacuated, proving the elemental-control project is larger than one room.
- A map of a remaining Conclave relay, safehouse, or northern transport route.

## The New Character: The Seismic Annex

### Why He Is Still Here

Place the captive in a sealed **Seismic Annex** beneath the Central Spire, accessed through a concealed maintenance descent behind the former portal infrastructure. It is a Conclave black-site chamber built into an old seismic-stabilization vault, unknown to most of Heliox.

- The Conclave used the annex to study mortals with elemental lineage because a construct-control signal could not command true elementals by itself.
- The genasi group's mixed elemental and mortal resonance gave the Conclave a bridge: a way to translate a command lattice into something an elemental could partially receive.
- The result was unstable. The four guardians could be directed in bursts, but not fully or safely controlled. Their resistance, rage, and the need for a separate device in the room above are evidence of that failure.
- The Conclave kept the earth genasi alive because his earth lineage made him exceptionally resilient to the pressure, crystal restraints, and repeated resonance tests. He is the last survivor presently in the annex, not necessarily the last survivor of his entire group, and is valuable enough that the retreating cell intends to take him with them.
- The other members of his party were recorded as transferred off-site after the experiments became too dangerous or too visible. Their fate is unknown; do not declare them dead unless the player later wants that.

This explains why the party did not find him during the earlier spire infiltration: the annex was a compartmentalized black-site lab beneath the mapped spire, concealed from normal civic records and sealed behind a Conclave-only descent.

### Discovery Scene

As the city turns against the Conclave, escaping agents retreat through a hidden under-spire route. Freed guards and citizens give chase, then are stopped by active turrets and guard robots. A damaged security map identifies the route as an unauthorized chamber still drawing immense power; its occupant is listed only as `SUBJECT: EARTHWARD / VITAL SIGNS PRESENT`.

Tavar-9 or Nemeris-4 brings the report to the Sovereign. The Sovereign asks the party to take the route immediately: the civilians can hold the upper corridor, but they cannot survive the annex defenses or let the Conclave carry a living prisoner away.

### Defense Gauntlet

Keep the approach short and tactical. The Conclave needs time more than it needs to kill the party, so its defenses delay, mark, and channel intruders toward bad positions.

| Layer | Defense | Player solutions |
|---|---|---|
| **Lockdown shutters** | Heavy doors seal the descent while the extraction lights begin cycling. | Force them, pick the manual locks, disable the actuator, or use a maintenance crawl. |
| **Sentry corridor** | Two ceiling turrets mark targets with violet lines and fire force bolts from protected alcoves. | Destroy, blind, hack, take cover, or bypass their targeting relay. |
| **Guard-robot bay** | Two dormant Heliox security constructs wake with Conclave override collars. | Fight, remove the collars, use Heliox authority to shut them down, or slip past them. |
| **Seismic seal** | Crystal pylons lock the final door and interfere with earth movement and teleportation. | Disable two pylons, ground the circuit, or risk breaking through as a cave-in hazard. |

The guard robots are machines, not controlled citizens. Removing an override collar can shut one down instead of destroying it. Do not add endless reinforcements; the extraction clock and the final room are the real pressure.

Read or paraphrase:

> Behind a wall of blackened brass, the air tastes of dust and hot metal. The ruined chamber beyond is too small to be a prison and too deliberate to be a workshop. Cracked crystal lines run from a shattered control cradle to a single restraint frame. Its labels have been burned away except for one warning: **ELEMENTAL RESONANCE: DO NOT WAKE WITHOUT GROUNDING.** Inside, someone is still breathing.

The cells are powered down enough to open safely, but not perfectly. Let the new player decide what waking looks like: consciousness, magic flaring, suspicion, restraint, anger, or quiet observation. Do not roll for whether the party trusts them; give all players room to make the scene theirs.

### What The Party Can Learn Without Taking The Character's Agency

- The captive arrived with a group of half-elemental, half-mortal travelers from the elemental continent. By default, they were sent by or sympathetic to the [[The Living Bridge Accord]], though the new player can choose an independent connection instead.
- They were traveling toward the earth elemental settlement and searching for the people opposing the Conclave. They did not know what the party looked like or exactly where to find them.
- The Conclave captured the group because it wanted to learn how elemental lineage could translate mortal commands into elemental obedience.
- The captive's earth affinity made him the most durable subject and the only person left in this annex.
- The party's disruption of the elemental controller created the opportunity to discover the annex before the Conclave could erase it.

Keep unknown: the new character's pact source, personal goals, exact history with the other travelers, whether their companions survive, and why they joined the expedition. Those belong to the player.

### Practical Starting Conditions

- His equipment is stored in a nearby evidence locker or was overlooked in a sealed specimen cabinet. Return it promptly so the player can participate normally.
- He has lingering but non-punishing signs of captivity: cracked earth-like scars, a disrupted spell focus, or a resonance brand that fades after a rest.
- The brand can provide one future story signal—such as pain near a Conclave elemental-control device—but it must not remove agency, track him flawlessly, or function as a permanent mechanical penalty.

## Suggested Session Flow

1. **Opening aftermath:** the broadcast signal is gone, citizens awaken, and the city realizes the Conclave can no longer command it.
2. **Conclave retreat:** agents flee toward the secret under-spire Seismic Annex. Citizens give chase, are blocked by the defenses, and call the party to the urgent objective.
3. **Annex breach:** circumvent turrets, guard robots, shutters, and traps while the evidence-purge/extraction countdown advances.
4. **Prisoner rescue:** stop the breach gate and free the genasi before the Conclave can take him to its main base.
5. **New-character introduction:** let the genasi enter play through roleplay, then return their equipment.
6. **Aftermath:** establish the Civic Recovery Assembly, resolve captured or escaped agents, and use recovered evidence to identify the missing companions and the wider elemental-control program.

## Likely Questions And Answers

### Why was the captive still in Heliox?

The group had already been copied, tested, and partially transferred into the elemental-control research. The earth genasi was too valuable and too difficult to move safely during the earlier chaos, so the local cell kept him in its fortified under-spire annex. When the signal failed, it immediately began the planned emergency extraction: purge the evidence, lock him into a transport cradle, and send him to the Conclave's main base. The party intervenes before that plan completes.

### Why were the four elementals still controlled if the genasi experiments were incomplete?

The Conclave did not achieve true control. It built a crude command bridge from stolen resonance data, confinement hardware, and the broadcast apparatus. The controller could force brief directives through pain, destabilization, and magical coercion. This is why a dedicated upper-room device was needed, why the elementals were powerful but unstable, and why breaking the device ended the Conclave's grip.

### Why does Heliox accept help from a half-elemental stranger?

Heliox does not automatically trust him. The party vouches that he was a captive; the Sovereign can authorize medical care and temporary protection; and recovered records corroborate the story. The new character earns wider trust through play, while the city has a moral obligation not to repeat the Conclave's treatment of him.

### What happened to the missing citizens sent through the portal?

The recovered portal records should confirm that some citizens were transferred before the party's earlier retreat. This remains an urgent rescue lead, separate from the city-retaking victory.

## Hooks After Session 11

### Final Act Of Session 11: Stonehearth

Once the annex is secured and the first Civic Recovery Assembly is functioning, send the party to [[Stonehearth]] before Session 11 ends. Handle Heliox's initial recovery and the journey as a montage; do not hold the destination for a separate session. Stonehearth is the strongest next step because it connects four active threads without requiring the party to solve them all immediately:

- The rescued earth genasi and their original group were already trying to find Stonehearth.
- [[Eldara Stonewise]] and Stonehearth belong to the [[The Returning Tide]], which can explain why the sealed portals are damaging both the elemental and Material Planes.
- Stonehearth has ties to former Reclaimer enforcers now sympathetic to the [[The Living Bridge Accord]], giving the new character a natural social connection rather than making them the sole representative of coexistence.
- The village can provide the next actionable lead: a surviving companion, a stolen resonance sample, a compromised portal site, or a Conclave route through the Forbidden Lands.

Do not make Stonehearth instantly trust the party. The group arrives as people connected to the crisis that harmed the elementals, but the rescued genasi and evidence from the annex give Eldara a reason to hear them out.

### Fast Departure Montage

Run these as a short epilogue after the annex rescue:

1. **Secure the annex:** the Continuity Cell seals the breach gate, catalogs evidence, and assigns guards to the prisoner and archive records.
2. **Stabilize the city:** the Assembly opens transit and medical routes, begins a missing-person count, and publicly confirms that controlled citizens will not be punished for the actions they cannot remember.
3. **Handle the Conclave prisoners:** exposed agents are detained under guard; captured technicians are separated from the records they could erase.
4. **Send a delegation:** Veyrix and the Sovereign remain in Heliox. They ask the party to represent the city's new willingness to cooperate, carrying proof of the Conclave's elemental-control experiments and a formal request to meet Eldara.
5. **Give the party travel support:** an emergency transit pass, supplies, a secure route out of Heliox, and one sealed evidence case for Stonehearth.

Kira remains in the sealed, covert Heliox repair bay to stabilize the breach gate and begin rebuilding a lab with city resources. She gives the party a diagnostic prism keyed to the resonance trammel, along with an honest warning: it is Conclave extraction technology built from calibration principles she once helped develop.

Do not add a travel encounter unless the group specifically wants one. The city needs the party's diplomatic and investigative skills at Stonehearth more than it needs another fight on the road.

### Stonehearth Arrival Scene

When the party arrives, Stonehearth is tense. Former Reclaimer elementals are arguing with Returning Tide elders and Bridgeborn sympathizers over whether Heliox's liberation proves mortals can change or merely creates another dangerous machine power. The rescue of an earth-genasi prisoner and proof of Conclave experimentation make the party's arrival impossible to ignore.

**Granitefist** meets the group at the outer cavern passage with defenders, recognizing the rescued genasi's elemental resonance but not immediately lowering the village's guard. **Crystalmender** notices the injuries and experimental residue. They bring the party to [[Eldara Stonewise]] at the Heartstone Altar.

Eldara is visibly weakened. A Conclave-made **resonance trammel**—a crystal-and-metal calibration rod—draws amber light from the runes in her stone body. It is bonded to her resonance and cannot be torn away safely. The rescued genasi's experimental scars and the annex evidence make the connection clear: the trammel is collecting the same kind of elemental-resonance data the Conclave used to control the four elemental guardians.

Eldara's immediate question is not "Are you heroes?" It is: **"Did you come to open the way home, to make peace here, or to ask us to fight another war for you?"**

The party does not need a perfect answer. Their choices establish what kind of alliance with the elementals they are actually trying to build. End Session 11 on Eldara examining the sealed evidence case, the trammel pulsing as it drains her, and the realization that the Conclave's work has reached deeper into elemental politics than anyone believed.

The trammel becomes an immediate Stonehearth problem: Crystalmender can stabilize Eldara for now, but the party must eventually choose between a careful extraction, a grounding ritual, tracing its stored resonance to the Conclave, or seeking Kira's technical help. Do not make careless removal safe; its stored power can backlash through Eldara or the Heartstone Altar.

### Other Leads To Keep Active

- Rescue the genasi's missing companions from the off-site elemental-interface program.
- Track Malrec's escape route and the data he extracted from Heliox.
- Use captured Conclave records to locate the citizens taken through the portal.
- Investigate what the escaped or freed elementals need after their confinement and control.
- Decide how the newly independent warforged, under the Sovereign's leadership, will relate to Heliox and the rest of Asmar.

## GM Guardrails

- The recoding victory is real and durable. Do not restore citywide control just to create another emergency.
- The citizens reclaim Heliox alongside the party; avoid making the party the city's new rulers.
- Malrec escaping preserves the campaign villain. Captured personnel, lost infrastructure, exposed records, and a liberated city make the Conclave's defeat meaningful.
- Make the new player's first scene about choice and dignity, not a forced combat test or a long interrogation.
- Treat the genasi group as victims of Conclave experimentation. Do not imply their elemental heritage made them inherently dangerous or responsible for what the Conclave did.
- Do not decide the new character's backstory, pact, personality, companions' final fate, or reason for joining the party; leave those open for the player.

## Details To Confirm With The New Player Before Play

- Character name, pronouns, appearance, and visible earth-genasi traits.
- The name and rough composition of the original traveling party.
- Whether any companions should be alive, missing, dead, or deliberately ambiguous.
- The source and tone of the warlock pact.
- What the character knows about the earth elemental village and why the group wanted to reach it.
- Whether the character wants immediate trust, wary cooperation, anger toward the party, or another opening dynamic.
