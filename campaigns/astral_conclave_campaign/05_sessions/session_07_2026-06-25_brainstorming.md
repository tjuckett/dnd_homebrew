# Session 07 Brainstorming - June 25, 2026

## Current Snapshot
- Session 06 ended with [[Mirrordune Relay]] destroyed in a catastrophic leyline-fed detonation.
- The party survived by sheltering in the drive compartment of [[The Calyxis Reclaimer]] while the blast disintegrated the inner site and gouged out a giant crater.
- There is effectively nothing worth recovering from the crater itself.
- The Reclaimer survived only in damaged sections:
  - the vehicle has `3` major parts,
  - one forward section was thrown some distance away,
  - and roughly half of that section was destroyed inside the disintegration radius.
- [[Kira]] arrived in disguise and helped the party salvage the remaining Reclaimer sections.
- The party can now reconnect the surviving sections and replace, bypass, or jury-rig the lost one well enough to keep moving.
- While those repairs were underway, a drone arrived to investigate the site.
- The party shot it down.
- That means Heliox-linked observers or a forward scout group are already operating closer than [[Heliox Prime]] itself.
- The party is likely heading toward [[Heliox Prime]], but has not fully committed on-screen yet.

## Core Tension
- The party no longer has a live desert relay to chase.
- Mirrordune was not merely a trap. It was proof that the [[Astral Conclave]] would rather erase a whole charging site than let the party investigate it.
- The Reclaimer is still usable, but damaged enough that every decision about travel, appearance, and risk now matters more.
- It is not safe to enter [[Heliox Prime]] openly.
- The Heliox warforged with the party should know enough civic culture to warn them that open arrival means detention, seizure of evidence, and likely disappearance into procedure.
- The party may not get a clean, uninterrupted road to Heliox.
- Someone nearby already checked the crater and may now know:
  - survivors escaped the blast,
  - the Reclaimer is damaged but not gone,
  - and unusual actors are moving with technical help.
- Heliox is the next real arc:
  - the command signal is hidden inside ordinary civic systems,
  - the Conclave presence is administrative and embedded,
  - and the party is arriving wounded, short on allies, and with less hard evidence than they hoped.
- Kira's involvement changes the texture of the next step:
  - she can explain why Mirrordune became a leyline-fed disaster,
  - she can help keep the Reclaimer functional,
  - and she has strong reasons to remain hidden.

## Recommended Session Spine
### 1. Open With The Crater And The Cost
- Start with the wrecked Mirrordune basin, heat still rising from fused glass and blackened sand.
- The party has already survived the immediate catastrophe; now let them take in what it cost.
- Make the important truth clear:
  - Mirrordune cannot be searched like a normal dungeon,
  - there is no intact bunker reward waiting in the crater,
  - and the Conclave successfully denied almost all direct site evidence.
- Let Kira, Selix, Varyn, and Captain Talis react from different angles:
  - Kira sees a technical horror,
  - Selix sees proof of industrial scale,
  - Varyn sees Heliox implication,
  - and Talis sees a mission that has turned into an execution attempt.

### 2. Repair What Can Be Saved
- The next active scene can be salvage triage around the damaged Reclaimer sections.
- Focus questions:
  - what can still move,
  - what has to be cut away,
  - what can be replaced with field engineering,
  - and whether the Reclaimer can still enter Heliox as a vehicle instead of a wreck.
- Kira should matter here:
  - she can disguise herself,
  - diagnose structural damage,
  - improvise a bypass or replacement strategy,
  - and possibly notice details in the blast pattern the others would miss.
- Good practical outcome:
  - the Reclaimer becomes ugly but mobile,
  - one section is clearly improvised or missing,
  - and the party understands they are driving a wounded war machine into a city built on procedure.

### 3. Decide How To Approach Heliox
- The session's main choice should become not "where next?" but "how do we arrive?"
- Main options:
  - attempt to enter openly with the damaged Reclaimer,
  - hide the Reclaimer outside the city and go in quietly,
  - send a small contact group first,
  - accept outside help from someone inside Heliox who reaches them first,
  - or split technical salvage from social approach.
- Each option should shift what kind of trouble finds them first:
  - open arrival brings immediate detention, impound threats, and political pressure,
  - quiet arrival brings observation, doubt, and fewer obvious protections,
  - accepting help means trusting someone the party has never met under active surveillance pressure,
  - and splitting the group risks isolation if Heliox procedure starts closing around them.

### 3A. Scout Group Pressure
- Before the city proper, the party may run into the people who sent or followed the drone.
- This should feel like Heliox reaching outward in a controlled, deniable way rather than launching a military assault.
- Best version:
  - a small scout team,
  - one technical observer,
  - one patrol lead,
  - and perhaps a light crawler, dispatch skiff, or portable relay mast.
- Their job is not necessarily to kill the party immediately.
- Their job is to assess:
  - whether Mirrordune truly failed,
  - who survived,
  - whether the Reclaimer is still mobile,
  - and whether this can still be handled as a procedural containment problem.
- Good outcomes from this scene:
  - the party captures one scout,
  - the scouts retreat and warn Heliox,
  - the party learns there is a nearby outpost or waystation,
  - or one scout turns out to be frightened rather than fully loyal.

### 3B. Sevra's Intercept
- Best version for this session: [[Sevra-6]] reaches the party before official Heliox procedure does.
- She intercepted a higher-order dispatch telling observers, patrols, and selected civic officials to exterminate everyone aboard the Reclaimer under preservation authority.
- That order tells her:
  - the higher-ups already know the party survived Mirrordune,
  - the city is treating them as compromised assets to be erased rather than witnesses to be processed,
  - and even the Heliox citizens traveling with the party have been marked for death.
- Sevra cannot stop the system.
- What she can do is warn the party away from the public approach and redirect them to a meeting point where she can get them into the city another way.
- This should be the first strong proof that resistance inside Heliox is becoming active, not just suspicious.

### 4. Let The Heliox Signal Change Shape
- The Heliox-tuned crystal should now matter more than the destroyed Mirrordune lead.

  - civic dispatch tones,

  - checkpoint signaling,

- Best effect:
  - the party realizes Mirrordune was only a desert arm of a much larger civic machine,

### 5. First Heliox Foothold

## Heliox Arrival Tone
- [[Heliox Prime]] should feel calm, orderly, and severe.

- Public dispatches continue at normal intervals.

- No one in authority admits there is a crisis, but citizens are beginning to question the strange activities of their leaders.
- The Heliox-tuned crystal does not point to one villain tower. It pulls toward a command district, then begins catching the same cadence in multiple official signals.

## Is There A Heliox Resistance?
- Yes, but not a clean underground army with a banner and a headquarters.
- Heliox is too procedural, too surveilled, and too self-disciplined for an open resistance to have formed yet.
- What exists is a loose, frightened pattern of dissent:
  - workers who know schedules have become wrong,
  - archivists who saw sealed orders appear without proper chain,
  - patrol officers disturbed by repeated commands nobody voiced,
  - and engineers who know the [[Civic Directive Lattice]] has been altered.
- Most of them do not think of themselves as "the resistance."
- They think of themselves as citizens trying to keep the city sane, protect coworkers, or preserve records before they disappear.
- If the party brings proof, restraint, and a real plan, those people can start becoming a real resistance.

## The Intercepted Order
Use this as the hidden dispatch Sevra catches inside ordinary civic traffic.

- Surface framing:
  - routine preservation-routing update,
  - outer approach observation notice,
  - and intake preparedness language that sounds bureaucratic if read casually.
- Hidden meaning:
  - watch for the party,
  - identify the damaged Reclaimer,
  - classify everyone aboard as compromised,
  - deny them all normal intake,
  - eliminate survivors and recover only useful hardware,
  - and keep the operation deniable under preservation language.
- What makes it chilling is not open villain language.
- It should sound like the city has already decided what the party is and built procedure around that assumption.

### Suggested Dispatch Text
`Outer preservation revision: mark damaged foreign crawler or segmented war asset approaching from Mirrordune vector as total contamination event. All aboard are to be classified compromised, including Heliox nationals, technical witnesses, and recovery personnel exposed to command residue or relay-tainted hardware. Deny public intake. Recover crystals, tuning instruments, command remnants, and surviving machine assets only if secure containment is possible. If containment is uncertain, initiate full extermination protocol and erase mobile witnesses before arrival at civic review. Public explanation remains salvage quarantine and cross-border hazard neutralization. Route exceptions require upper cadence confirmation only.`

### What Sevra Understands From It
- `damaged foreign crawler or segmented war asset` means the Reclaimer has been identified well enough to watch for its silhouette.
- `all aboard are to be classified compromised` means no one riding with the party is meant to survive intake.
- `including Heliox nationals` means Selix, Varyn, and any other Heliox citizens with them have already been written off.
- `command remnants` means the city knows or suspects the party has relay crystals, detector gear, or related hardware.
- `full extermination protocol` means this is not arrest authority with lethal backup; it is a kill order dressed up as procedure.
- `upper cadence confirmation only` means the order came through a protected layer ordinary clerks are not supposed to question.

## The Warehouse Pattern
- Sevra's group has noticed unusual work in several warehouses across Heliox.
- These sites share the same warning signs:
  - sealed access under preservation authority,
  - irregular deliveries at off-hours,
  - strange power draw,
  - workers reassigned and then unavailable for questioning,
  - and patrol patterns that look administrative rather than military.
- The group does not yet know what the warehouses are for.
- Their best guess is that they are tied to hidden command infrastructure, crystal routing, or emergency civic control.
- The truth:
  - at least one warehouse contains a major broadcast device tied to population control,
  - there are `5` such broadcast nodes or related control stations distributed through the city,
  - and the Conclave can use them together to help project a district-wide or city-wide obedience field.
- Important reveal pacing:
  - the first warehouse should prove the device is real,
  - the players should learn there are `5`,
  - but they should not be able to solve all `5` in one session.

## The Mole
Use this to turn the fragile Sevra contact network into a real risk.

- One member of Sevra's small circle is a mole.
- The mole does not need to be openly villainous.
- Best version:
  - they believe they are preserving Heliox through ugly necessity,
  - or they are compromised and rationalizing their obedience,
  - or they are frightened enough to trade the party for personal survival.
- Their function in Session 07:
  - help sell the warehouse lead as plausible,
  - feed true information mixed with one fatal omission,
  - and lead the party into a kill box that also contains a real broadcast node.
- This keeps the trap believable:
  - the site matters,
  - the clue is not fake,
  - and the Conclave is using the players' own success to spring the attack.

## Suggested Mole: Merix-7
- Role: logistics reviewer or warehouse allocation clerk tied to sealed preservation transfers.
- Demeanor: measured, useful, apparently relieved to find someone else taking the danger seriously.
- Why Sevra trusts him at first:
  - he has real records,
  - he noticed the same warehouse anomalies,
  - and he has never pushed for reckless action before.
- What is actually true:
  - he has either been quietly compromised through command insertion,
  - or he consciously decided the city must survive even if a few dangerous outsiders disappear.

  - he gives the party real evidence,

- What he hides:
  - the warehouse is already prepared for intrusion,
  - the node inside can be remotely hardened or activated,
  - and a kill team is waiting for the party to commit.

## Sevra's Mistake
- Sevra is not foolish; she is early.
- Her mistake is that she thinks shared fear equals shared loyalty.
- She trusts the cell enough to bring the warehouse pattern to the party before she knows which member is leaking information.
- That makes the betrayal painful and believable instead of making her seem careless for no reason.

- Why it looks right:
  - unusual night activity,
  - sealed preservation access,
  - guarded but not like a fortress,

  - and crate traffic that implies major hardware inside.
- Why the players go:
  - it is exactly the kind of place Sevra's group has been worrying about,
  - it can yield proof,
  - and the party wants something concrete before moving deeper into Heliox.
- What is true:
  - the warehouse does contain a massive broadcast device used for population control,
  - the device is one of `5` related nodes,
  - and disabling or reading it would matter.
- What is false:
  - it is not unguarded,
  - it is not merely a discovery scene,

## What The Warehouse Actually Is
- Outward cover:
  - sealed civic storage,
  - dispatch harmonization depot,
  - or crystal logistics overflow under preservation authority.
- Real function:
  - hidden broadcast node tied to the obedience architecture in Heliox,
  - local signal amplifier,
  - command-route stabilizer,
  - and district relay for population control.
- Visual identity:
  - rows of legitimate city crates and work gear,
  - one deep interior chamber with insulated walls,
  - heavy crystal conduits,
  - brass relay vanes,
  - and a central broadcast spine or dish assembly far too large to be normal warehouse equipment.
- Core reveal:
  - the players are not chasing rumors anymore,
  - they have found part of the machine that can control the city.

## The Five Broadcast Nodes
- The first warehouse should let the players learn there are `5` key broadcast nodes in the system.
- Suggested structure:
  - `1` warehouse node in the logistics district,
  - `1` dispatch-tower subnode,
  - `1` refinement-annex node,
  - `1` reserve-power or recharge-vault node,
  - `1` deeper Lattice-linked master node or command insert.
- What the players should learn here:
  - the warehouse they found is only one segment,
  - the whole system is redundant enough that smashing one node helps but does not end the threat,
  - and the Conclave built the network for city-scale control rather than one-off local abuse.

## Warehouse Kill Box
This should be one of the deadliest encounters of the Heliox arc so far.

- Purpose:
  - kill the party outright if possible,
  - seize or destroy their hardware if possible,
  - and leave behind a plausible civic explanation if the Conclave wins.
- Framing:
  - once the party is committed deep enough into the warehouse,
  - doors seal,
  - signal dampers engage,
  - the broadcast node powers up,
  - and the strike team attacks under cover of Heliox infrastructure.
- Important fairness rule:
  - the warehouse must contain a real target and real evidence,
  - the trap should be foreshadowed by odd guard behavior, overclean lanes, insulated walls, or too-convenient access,
  - and the party should have at least a slim path to detect something is wrong before the box shuts.

## Kill Box Components
- Sealed blast doors or sliding civic shutters divide the warehouse into kill zones.
- The broadcast node emits a control-pressure field that punishes lingering in exposed lanes.
- Hidden crystal repeaters create overlapping suppression zones.
- Security constructs or compromised Heliox heavy patrol units engage once the doors lock.
- Conclave technicians or handlers remain behind hardened glass, catwalk cover, or a shielded control booth if you want an active operator presence.
- The node room itself is dangerous terrain:
  - arcing crystal lines,
  - hard cover pillars,
  - elevated maintenance walks,
  - grounding trenches,
  - and exposed relay housings that can be sabotaged or overloaded.

## Deadly Encounter Package
Use this as a boss-scale encounter for `5` level `10` characters. Tune up or down based on party strength and whether they still have the Reclaimer available nearby.

- Primary threats:
  - `2` Heliox control brutes or security constructs holding the floor,
  - `4` to `6` disciplined support enemies such as shock troopers, command-wardens, or rifle constructs,
  - `1` node operator, relay handler, or hidden controller,
  - plus the broadcast field as a lair-style hazard.
- Why this is deadly:
  - action economy,
  - forced movement,
  - terrain control,
  - control effects from the field,
  - and the possibility that the node itself worsens every round unless interrupted.
- Strong version:
  - the party fights enemies and the room at the same time.

## Broadcast Field Hazard
- At initiative count `20` or on a fixed round cadence, the warehouse node emits one effect:
  - obedience pulse,
  - suppression hum,
  - directional stun burst,
  - or target-lock directive.
- Suggested effects:
  - DC 17 Wisdom save or lose reactions and have speed halved until next round,
  - DC 17 Constitution save or take `4d10` psychic/thunder and be unable to speak above a few words,
  - DC 17 Strength or Dexterity save or be pulled/pushed into exposed lanes by magnetic or command surge,
  - or one construct/warforged target is singled out for a harder effect.
- The Sovereign or special party tech should matter here:
  - they can resist better,
  - identify the pulse rhythm,
  - or disrupt one emission cycle with the right action.

## Kill Box Objective Pressure
- If the party only fights enemies, the warehouse keeps getting worse.
- Give them at least `2` simultaneous priorities:
  - survive the kill team,
  - stop or damage the broadcast node,
  - rescue Sevra or another ally if present,
  - prevent the mole from escaping,
  - or stop the node from sending a wider alarm.
- This keeps the scene from becoming a static slugfest.

## Good Kill Box Beats
- The doors slam and the players realize too late that the lead was leaked.
- The mole's betrayal becomes obvious only after the lock-in starts.
- The players see the full broadcast device powering up and realize the warehouse rumor was true.
- Mid-fight, they recover proof that there are `5` nodes total.
- The Conclave attempts to kill them with both violence and civic systems.
- If they survive, they leave with proof, a new enemy shape, and the knowledge that Heliox is already a battlefield.

## Ways The Trap Can Still Be Survivable
- Sevra gives them partial warning signs without realizing what they mean.
- The mole omits the kill-box details but cannot perfectly hide all the setup.
- The players can spot:
  - insulated walls where there should be none,
  - unusually empty work lanes,
  - too few visible workers,
  - hidden firing slits or sealed catwalk doors,
  - or relay hum stronger than normal logistics equipment should produce.
- Strong play before the trap fully closes can:
  - stop one blast door from sealing,
  - isolate part of the enemy force,
  - damage the broadcast node early,
  - or preserve an escape line.

## What They Can Discover In Heliox
- The Heliox-side frequency is being blended into normal [[Civic Directive Lattice]] dispatch cadence.

- Some Heliox personnel are victims, some are unaware functionaries, and a smaller number are active collaborators or compromised supervisors.
- Sleeper devices may exist in Heliox, but the more immediate danger is city-scale suggestibility through official systems.
- The city contains the architecture for something worse: if enough energy is forced through the Lattice, the Conclave can emit a citywide control field.
