# Session 08 Brainstorming - July 9, 2026

## Current Snapshot
- The party infiltrated [[Heliox Prime]] at night after scanning the city from above.
- They discovered `6` major control hubs capable of broadcasting or stabilizing the citizen-control signal.
- They entered a warehouse containing one of those hubs.
- Stony created a feedback device that could send a disruption wave through the network from within `30` feet of a hub.
- The feedback test destroyed the warehouse hub, but it also killed roughly `50%` of the citizens affected through that part of the network.
- Heliox now has `5` known control hubs remaining.
- The party has learned the core horror:
  - the citizens are not merely receiving commands,
  - they are integrated into the control architecture,
  - and brute-force disruption can kill them.
- The next question is no longer "can the hubs be destroyed?"
- The next question is "how can the party free Heliox without committing genocide?"

## Campaign Need
- The warforged player needs a satisfying exit from the party in `2` to `3` sessions.
- The exit should feel like the natural culmination of their character's hidden backstory rather than a sudden retirement.
- The player character once wiped their own memory years ago in an attempt to reset themselves so they could not be controlled.
- That reset worked in one important way:
  - it severed or rewrote the obedience hooks that would let a command network overwrite them.
- The cost was severe:
  - the reset disabled them,
  - stripped away identity, stored skill, and accumulated capability,
  - and effectively forced them to start again from level `0`.
- The same anti-command architecture may be transferable to the citizens of Heliox.
- The terrible catch:
  - broadcasting it citywide requires the warforged PC to use themself as the living template,
  - overwrite their own restored selfhood into a distributable refusal pattern,
  - and sacrifice themself so the city can keep the part of them that knows how to say no.

## Core Emotional Idea
- The first feedback wave killed people because it fought the control signal from the outside.
- The warforged PC's reset is different because it creates an internal refusal structure.
- It does not simply break the chain.
- It teaches the controlled mind how to reject the chain without shattering.
- The sacrifice should not be framed as "die to turn off the machine."
- It should be framed as:
  - the PC once saved themself by becoming unreachable,
  - now they can save Heliox by making everyone else unreachable too,
  - but the pattern cannot be copied without giving up the original.

## Conclave Panic Protocol
- After the warehouse hub is destroyed, the [[Astral Conclave]] stops treating Heliox as a long-term infiltration project.
- They decide the party is too dangerous to contain through subtle procedure.
- Their new strategy:
  - activate the remaining control hubs,
  - turn controlled citizens into weapons against the party,
  - herd citizens toward harvesting sites,
  - strip power cores, charged components, and useful civic hardware,
  - destroy or abandon whatever they cannot carry,
  - and leave Heliox crippled before the party can expose or reverse the operation.
- This is a drastic measure, but the Conclave is cutting losses.
- They are no longer trying to preserve the city as cover.
- They are trying to extract value, erase witnesses, and escape with enough power to continue the larger plan.

## Time Crunch
- The party should feel hunted from Session 08 onward.
- The city itself becomes the danger:
  - controlled citizens block streets,
  - work crews become search parties,
  - patrol units coordinate without speaking,
  - dispatch towers issue kill-directives under civic language,
  - and civilians begin walking toward harvesting stations unless interrupted.
- The party is pressured by two simultaneous needs:
  - survive the controlled populace and Conclave strike teams,
  - and save as many citizens as possible before their power cores are harvested.
- This makes the warforged PC's eventual solution urgent.
- The sacrifice is not just a clean way to disable the hubs.
- It may be the only way to stop a citywide forced march before the Conclave guts Heliox and leaves.

## Important Table Principle
- Do not force the sacrifice as the only possible action until the player has had space to choose it.
- Give the party other partial solutions:
  - disable one hub safely but slowly,
  - shield a district,
  - evacuate a limited number of citizens,
  - isolate one signal branch,
  - or use Stony's work to build a safer local disruptor.
- Those options should help, but they should not solve the whole city quickly enough.
- The warforged sacrifice becomes the fastest, cleanest, and most complete solution, not the only imaginable action.
- If the player resists the sacrifice, the story can pivot toward:
  - a longer liberation arc,
  - a partial save with major costs,
  - or the warforged PC leaving the party alive to pursue the dangerous cure alone.

## Session 08 Recommended Spine
### 1. Open With The Cost
- Start immediately after the warehouse hub is destroyed.
- The city does not explode into chaos.
- It becomes worse in a quieter way:
  - dispatch bells stutter,
  - work crews stop mid-task,
  - nearby citizens collapse,
  - and survivors repeat broken phrases before waking in terror or confusion.
- The party should understand that their test did something real and awful.
- Stony should get a clear read:
  - the device worked exactly as designed,
  - but the network used the citizens as part of the circuit,
  - so the feedback burned through living minds before it reached the machinery.

### 1A. The City Turns
- Before the party can fully process the cost, the remaining hubs activate together.
- Public dispatch towers shift from civic order to command cadence.
- Controlled citizens turn toward the warehouse district.
- Some attack directly.
- Others ignore the party and begin walking toward designated preservation intake points.
- The phrase "preservation intake" should sound bureaucratic at first.
- The reveal:
  - these are not shelters,
  - they are harvesting sites,
  - and the Conclave is gathering citizens for power-core extraction.
- Good dispatch language:
  - `Preservation intake is now mandatory. Viable cores will be secured for continuity of purpose.`
  - `Unauthorized actors are to be restrained, dismantled, or delivered for review.`
  - `Civic assets will proceed to designated recovery stations. Resistance wastes the future.`

### 2. Make The Warforged PC The Exception
- During the aftershock, the control signal tries to correct everyone nearby.
- Rehm, Selix, or other Heliox citizens feel it.
- The warforged PC feels something different:
  - a command searches for a path that is no longer there,
  - old memory locks warm,
  - and some internal structure answers with refusal rather than obedience.
- The signal may briefly identify them as:
  - `UNASSIGNED`,
  - `ROOT MISSING`,
  - `EXECUTIVE CHANNEL SEALED`,
  - or `SUBJECT OUTSIDE CADENCE`.
- This is the first clue that the PC is not merely lucky or strange.

### 3. Escape The Warehouse District
- The hub going dark triggers a containment response.
- Use this as a chase, stealth scene, or pressure sequence rather than another full battle.

  - patrols converging,

  - compromised citizens moving like searchlights,
  - controlled citizens forced to fight the party,

  - grab hub fragments,

### 4. The Safehouse Diagnosis
- Sevra, Selix, Stony, or a Heliox engineer studies the hub fragments and the party's condition.
- Compare three signatures:
  - citizens killed by feedback,

  - the citizens have command hooks braided through identity, motor response, and civic directive channels.
  - the feedback burned through those hooks violently.
  - the warforged PC has scars where the hooks should be.
  - those scars are organized, not random.
  - the PC's old reset left behind a stable refusal pattern.
- This gives the party a new research objective:
  - find out what created that pattern,
  - and whether it can be taught to Heliox safely.

- The safehouse group finds one lead tied to the PC's forgotten past.
- Possible leads:

## The Warforged Backstory Reveal
- Years ago, the PC realized they could be controlled or repurposed by a command system.
- They attempted a total memory wipe to reset themself before anyone else could overwrite them.
- The plan was not just amnesia.
- It was a deliberate severing of command compatibility:
  - delete known hooks,
  - burn out obedience receptors,
  - scramble identity routes the command signal could use,
  - and rebuild from a blank self that had no authorized master.
- Side effect:
  - their body survived,
  - their old self was mostly erased,
  - and their capability collapsed so completely that they effectively had to level up again from nothing.
- The tragedy:
  - they saved their freedom by destroying their continuity.
- The triumph:
  - the thing that made them "broken" is the exact thing Heliox needs.

## How The Pattern Works
- The control network kills people when disrupted because their command hooks are braided into core function.
- A safe cure needs three steps:
  - identify the command hooks,
  - give the citizen's mind a replacement route before the hooks are severed,
  - then let the control signal burn itself out against a refusal structure.
- The warforged PC already contains a working example.
- Their reset did not remove obedience cleanly.
- It replaced obedience with a self-protecting null answer:
  - no master accepted,
  - no command channel trusted,
  - no external directive allowed to outrank the self.
- This can be adapted for others, but only if the network sees the PC's whole active self as the original template.

## Why The Sacrifice Is Necessary
- A partial scan can copy the shape of the pattern but not the living priority that makes it work.
- A dead or inert sample is not enough.
- A normal broadcast would flatten the pattern into another command, which would repeat the same violence as Stony's feedback.
- The PC has to actively choose refusal while connected to the Lattice.
- That choice is the template.
- The city has to receive not just data, but an enacted act of self-ownership.
- Once the Lattice copies that act at city scale, the PC's own identity becomes the carrier wave and cannot remain intact.

## Make It Feel Fair
- Foreshadow the cost before the final moment.
- Let Stony or Selix say the awful part plainly:
  - "We can copy the map, but not the fire that walks it."
  - "A dead pattern becomes a command. A living pattern becomes permission."
  - "If we make the city obey freedom, we have failed. It has to learn refusal from someone doing it."
- Let the PC ask questions.
- Let the party search for alternatives.
- Let alternatives accomplish something meaningful even if they cannot save everyone quickly.
- The final sacrifice should feel chosen under pressure, not author-mandated.

## Alternatives And Partial Solutions
- Stony can build a safer local dampener:
  - protects one room, one street, or one group of citizens.
  - useful for rescues and final approach.
- Selix can tune hub fragments:
  - identifies which citizens are most at risk from feedback.
  - helps prevent another accidental massacre.
- Rehm can recognize living symptoms:
  - tells the party who is still reachable and who is being pushed toward full synchronization.
- Sevra can create dispatch blind spots:
  - hides party movement and protects witnesses.
- The intact stupor device from the mountains can become a test rig:
  - dangerous but useful for proving the PC resists command differently.
- The mountain-cave corpse from Heliox can carry an old clue:
  - a diagnostic tag,
  - a failed reset mark,
  - or evidence that someone else tried the same cure and did not survive.

## Harvest Protocol
- The Conclave does not want to hold Heliox anymore if the party keeps breaking the network.
- Their fallback plan is to turn the city into fuel.
- Controlled citizens are ordered to report to:
  - preservation intake halls,
  - repair annexes,
  - energy-core refinement stations,
  - mobile extraction rigs,
  - and sealed civic warehouses that were already part of the control network.
- Public explanation:
  - emergency stabilization,
  - quarantine review,
  - contamination screening,
  - or power-core preservation.
- Real purpose:
  - remove citizens from public view,
  - extract usable power cores,
  - strip memory, identity, or command-relevant components,
  - and ship the cores out through Conclave-controlled routes.
- The horror should be procedural:
  - citizens lining up calmly because they have been ordered to,
  - intake clerks repeating comforting phrases,
  - extraction rigs warming behind sealed doors,
  - and resistance members realizing the city is being harvested under its own laws.

## Controlled Populace Encounters
- Use the citizens as a moral and tactical problem, not just enemies.
- Most controlled citizens are victims.
- They can:
  - grapple,
  - block routes,
  - carry signal repeaters,
  - point out the party's location,
  - swarm escape paths,
  - or attack with tools, civic arms, or integrated systems.
- The party should be able to choose nonlethal options, but nonlethal choices cost time.
- Time matters because every delayed round, scene, or failed route can move more citizens into harvest lines.
- Good encounter pressure:
  - a controlled crowd blocks the only direct route to the memory vault,
  - a friend or named NPC is in a harvest line,
  - the party can rescue one intake hall or chase the Conclave transport, not both,
  - and controlled citizens keep repeating city slogans while being marched to dismantling.

## Two Heavy Guard Constructs
Use these when the Conclave stops relying on ordinary controlled citizens and sends real hardware after the party. They should feel in line with the earlier Heliox robot enemies: heavy plating, direct impact, forced movement, line denial, and brutal construct discipline.

### Heliox Lockstep Bulwark
*Medium construct, lawful neutral*

- **CR:** `8`
- **AC:** `19` heavy civic plating
- **HP:** `152` (`16d8 + 80`)
- **Speed:** `30 ft.`
- **STR** `22` **DEX** `10` **CON** `20` **INT** `8` **WIS** `12` **CHA** `6`
- **Saving Throws:** Str `+10`, Con `+9`, Wis `+5`
- **Skills:** Athletics `+10`, Perception `+5`
- **Damage Resistances:** cold; lightning; bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical attacks
- **Damage Immunities:** poison
- **Condition Immunities:** charmed, exhaustion, frightened, paralyzed, poisoned
- **Senses:** darkvision `60 ft.`, passive Perception `15`
- **Languages:** understands Common and Construct Cant but speaks only through dispatch bursts

#### Traits
- **Lockstep Formation.** The bulwark has advantage on attack rolls against a creature if at least one controlled citizen, allied construct, or Conclave creature is within `5` feet of the target and not incapacitated.
- **Citizens As Cover.** While a controlled citizen is within `5` feet of the bulwark, attack rolls against the bulwark have disadvantage unless the attacker is within `5` feet of it.
- **Command-Hardened Frame.** The bulwark has advantage on saving throws against effects that would move it, knock it prone, or impose the stunned condition.

#### Actions
- **Multiattack.** The bulwark makes two Arc Maul attacks and one Shield Ram attack.
- **Arc Maul.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* `+10` to hit, reach `5 ft.`, one target. Hit: `17` (`2d10 + 6`) bludgeoning damage plus `7` (`2d6`) lightning damage.
- **Shield Ram.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* `+10` to hit, reach `5 ft.`, one target. Hit: `13` (`2d6 + 6`) bludgeoning damage, and the target must succeed on a DC `18` Strength saving throw or be pushed `15` feet and knocked prone.
- **Containment Slam (Recharge 5-6).** The bulwark slams its shield into the ground. Each creature of the bulwark's choice within `15` feet must make a DC `17` Dexterity saving throw, taking `27` (`6d8`) thunder damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success. On a failed save, a creature is also pushed `10` feet away from the bulwark.

#### Reaction
- **Interpose.** When a creature the bulwark can see hits an allied construct, Conclave creature, or controlled citizen within `5` feet of it, the bulwark reduces the damage by `18` (`4d8`). If the attacker is within `5` feet of the bulwark, the attacker must succeed on a DC `18` Strength saving throw or be knocked prone.

#### Tactics
- Use the Lockstep Bulwark to pin the party in a street, doorway, intake hall, or warehouse lane.
- It should shove characters into controlled crowds, away from civilians they are trying to save, or back toward a harvest line.
- It is strongest when the party is trying to avoid hurting citizens.

### Heliox Core-Reaper Mauler
*Large construct, lawful evil*

- **CR:** `10`
- **AC:** `18` reinforced extraction chassis
- **HP:** `189` (`18d10 + 90`)
- **Speed:** `30 ft.`
- **STR** `24` **DEX** `10` **CON** `21` **INT** `10` **WIS** `13` **CHA** `8`
- **Saving Throws:** Str `+11`, Con `+9`, Wis `+5`
- **Skills:** Athletics `+11`, Perception `+5`
- **Damage Resistances:** cold, fire, lightning; bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical attacks
- **Damage Immunities:** poison
- **Condition Immunities:** charmed, exhaustion, frightened, paralyzed, poisoned
- **Senses:** darkvision `60 ft.`, passive Perception `15`
- **Languages:** Common, Construct Cant

#### Traits
- **Harvest Chassis.** The mauler has advantage on Strength checks and Strength saving throws. It can drag or carry one grappled Medium or smaller creature without reducing its speed.
- **Powered By Intake.** When a construct or warforged creature dies within `30` feet of the mauler, the mauler gains `15` temporary hit points.
- **Brutal Impact.** Once per turn, when the mauler hits a creature with a Slam or Reaper Claw attack, it can deal an extra `9` (`2d8`) force damage.

#### Actions
- **Multiattack.** The mauler makes two Reaper Claw attacks. It can replace one attack with Hydraulic Slam.
- **Reaper Claw.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* `+11` to hit, reach `10 ft.`, one target. Hit: `18` (`2d10 + 7`) slashing damage plus `9` (`2d8`) force damage. If the target is Large or smaller, it is grappled escape DC `19`. The mauler can grapple up to two targets at a time.
- **Hydraulic Slam.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* `+11` to hit, reach `5 ft.`, one target. Hit: `20` (`3d8 + 7`) bludgeoning damage, and the target must succeed on a DC `19` Strength saving throw or be knocked prone.
- **Core-Draw Spike (Recharge 5-6).** One creature grappled by the mauler must make a DC `17` Constitution saving throw. On a failure, the target takes `36` (`8d8`) lightning damage and is stunned until the end of its next turn. On a success, the target takes half damage and is not stunned. A construct or warforged target has disadvantage on this saving throw.
- **Extraction Pulse (1/Day).** The mauler emits a violent magnetic pulse in a `20-foot` radius. Each creature of the mauler's choice in that area must make a DC `17` Constitution saving throw, taking `33` (`6d10`) thunder damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success. Constructs and creatures wearing metal armor that fail the save are also pulled up to `15` feet toward the mauler.

#### Bonus Actions
- **Drag To Intake.** The mauler moves up to half its speed, dragging one creature it has grappled. This movement does not provoke opportunity attacks from the grappled creature.

#### Reaction
- **Punishing Servo.** When a creature within `10` feet of the mauler hits it with a melee attack, the mauler makes one Reaper Claw attack against that creature.

#### Tactics
- Use the Core-Reaper Mauler when the party sees citizens being harvested or moved toward extraction rigs.
- It should grab one PC or named NPC and start dragging them toward machinery.
- Against the warforged PC, it should feel especially threatening because its tools are designed for power-core removal.
- Pair one Core-Reaper Mauler with controlled citizens or one Lockstep Bulwark for a hard encounter.

### Encounter Use
- **Moderate-hard pressure:** `1` Lockstep Bulwark plus controlled citizens as nonlethal obstacles.
- **Hard fight:** `1` Core-Reaper Mauler plus `2` to `4` weaker Heliox constructs.
- **Deadly chase-stop:** `1` Lockstep Bulwark and `1` Core-Reaper Mauler guarding a harvest intake route.
- If the party is level `10`, the two together are a serious threat but still more interesting if the goal is not simply "kill them."
- Better objectives:
  - stop the mauler dragging citizens into an extraction chamber,
  - disable an intake rig,
  - get past the bulwark before the pressure clock advances,
  - or save a named NPC without killing controlled citizens.

## Pressure Clock
Use a simple visible or hidden countdown.

- `0`: One hub destroyed. Conclave begins containment.
- `1`: Patrols and dispatch systems search for the party.
- `2`: Remaining hubs harden against physical sabotage.
- `3`: Selected districts enter deeper compliance and begin hunting the party.
- `4`: Preservation intake orders begin moving citizens toward harvest sites.
- `5`: The Conclave identifies the warforged PC as a refusal anomaly.
- `6`: Full synchronization begins and mobile extraction rigs activate.
- `7`: The city becomes much harder to save without catastrophic losses.

  - and the person who authorizes the citywide harvest protocol.
- Identity:
  - Veyr-Kas presents themself as a calm preservation auditor assigned to prevent civic collapse.
  - In truth, they are the Conclave's field authority for converting controlled societies into portable resources.

- Demeanor:
  - quiet,
  - exacting,
  - courteous,
  - completely merciless,
  - and offended by waste more than cruelty.
- Philosophy:
  - people are "continuity assets,"
  - power cores are "recoverable civic value,"
  - memory is "useful residue,"
  - and survival belongs to whoever can be repurposed efficiently.
- Why they are scary:
  - they do not hate Heliox.
  - They value Heliox precisely enough to dismantle it cleanly.
  - They do not want revenge on the party.
  - They want to remove a disruption, harvest the city, and leave on schedule.

- Boss traits:

  - reaction-based countercommands,
