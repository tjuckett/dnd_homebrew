# Session 06 Brainstorming - June 4, 2026

## Current Snapshot
- The party is finished with the Sandglass cave situation and is back in the aftermath at or near [[Sandglass Relay]] and [[The Calyxis Reclaimer]].
- The expedition has been gutted:
  - `15` soldiers are dead,
  - `4` soldiers survived,
  - almost all accompanying NPCs are dead,
  - the only named NPC survivors are [[Selix]], [[Varyn]], and the captain.
- The party recovered three tuned crystals from the bodies at Sandglass:
  - one crystal is tuned to the second relay site, likely [[Cinderhook Relay]], but it has no ping,
  - one crystal is tuned to the leyline charging site, [[Mirrordune Relay]] or Mirrordune Station,
  - one crystal is tuned toward the main city, [[Heliox Prime]], but the signal is faint.
- The party is probably skipping Cinderhook and going straight to Mirrordune, then Heliox.
- Stony still has the relay retuning device recovered from Sandglass.
- The tuned crystals can help Stony and Selix retune the scanner toward Mirrordune and later identify Heliox-side infrastructure.

## Core Tension
- The route is no longer "which relay next?" It is "can they catch the active charging operation before it disappears?"
- Cinderhook's silence is a clue, not a destination focus:
  - the second relay has been cleaned out,
  - the Conclave has already reacted to Sandglass,
  - and the chain is collapsing backward toward Mirrordune and Heliox.
- Mirrordune is the live operation:
  - charged crystals,
  - controlled or abandoned Heliox workers,
  - evacuation,
  - purge charges,
  - and tracks or transit records leading to Heliox.
- Heliox is the next arc:
  - the signal is faint because it is hidden inside ordinary civic systems,
  - the Conclave presence is administrative and embedded,
  - and the party will arrive with proof, grief, and very few survivors.

## Recommended Session Spine
### 1. Open With The Cost And The Route
- Start with the quiet Reclaimer and the tiny surviving expedition.
- Let the captain name the losses plainly: `15` soldiers dead, `4` still standing.
- Selix and Stony can work with the crystals and retuning device.
- The Cinderhook crystal gives a clean frequency but no answer.
- The Mirrordune crystal is warm, active, and charge-warped.
- The Heliox crystal is faint and layered under civic signal noise.
- Push the practical conclusion: if they want a live trail, Mirrordune is the trail.

### 2. Travel Toward Mirrordune
- The scanner locks onto the Mirrordune crystal.
- The pulse should feel less like a beacon and more like a countdown.
- Travel signs:
  - glassy sand ridges,
  - heat shimmer with rhythmic pulses,
  - dust from a distant crawler or transport,
  - faint crystal overtones through the Reclaimer frame,
  - and the Mirrordune crystal warming whenever the vehicle points true.
- Cinderhook can be implied in passing:
  - the Cinderhook crystal stays cold,
  - the scanner briefly tries to triangulate a dead side-bearing,
  - but the active pulse pulls harder toward Mirrordune.

### 3. Mirrordune Countdown Scene
- Mirrordune should not start as a static dungeon.
- It is an operation in the middle of shutdown.
- On arrival, the party sees:
  - a mirrored antenna frame folding inward,
  - charge cradles around a shallow leyline vent,
  - a charged crystal crate being moved toward a Heliox-bound transport or portal route,
  - purge crystals being armed around the relay table,
  - a command bolt or stupor-field device being destroyed,
  - and one or more Heliox workers abandoned, controlled, unconscious, or caught in the collapse of the field.
- The players should immediately understand that acting fast matters.

### 4. What They Can Interrupt
- Stop the purge of the command bolt or stupor-field device.
- Damage, steal, or mark the charged crystal crate.
- Prevent the blast failsafe from destroying all Mirrordune evidence.
- Capture a relay technician before the escape route closes.
- Free or stabilize controlled Heliox workers.
- Salvage cleaner tuning hardware than anything left at Sandglass.
- Learn how the charged crystals are being routed into Heliox.

### 5. Point To Heliox
- Mirrordune evidence should point hard toward [[Heliox Prime]].
- The Heliox crystal should sync with:
  - transport route tags,
  - official-looking work orders,
  - dispatch cadence,
  - charge-crystal logistics records,
  - and the direction of the final transport.
- End the session, if pacing fits, with the party either:
  - chasing the charged shipment,
  - standing over the damaged Mirrordune relay with proof,
  - or watching the Heliox crystal pulse in time with a distant civic dispatch.

## Crystal Mechanics
- Stony can use the relay retuning device with any of the three crystals, but the scanner can only cleanly track one frequency at a time unless upgraded.
- Basic read:
  - DC 12 Arcana, Investigation, or Tinker's Tools.
  - Reveals the crystal's category: silent relay, active charging site, faint city signal.
- Stable Mirrordune tune:
  - DC 14 Arcana, Investigation, or Tinker's Tools.
  - Takes 10 minutes.
  - On success, the scanner follows Mirrordune reliably.
  - On failure, it still points toward Mirrordune, but the signal surges, drifts, or briefly confuses the dead Cinderhook side-bearing.
- Careful comparison:
  - DC 16.
  - Takes 1 hour and benefits from Selix's help.
  - Reveals that Cinderhook was cut clean, Mirrordune is actively resonating, and Heliox is buried under civic signal noise.
- Advantage sources:
  - Selix helping,
  - using Sandglass relay hardware as a grounding point,
  - mounting the damaged receiving antenna on [[The Calyxis Reclaimer]],
  - using the resin-sealed relay shard,
  - or comparing all three crystals together.

## The Three Crystals
### Cinderhook Crystal
- Signal feel: clean, cold, and absent.
- It still has a frequency, but no live ping answers.
- Keep it brief unless the players ask:
  - Cinderhook has likely been shut down, scrubbed, moved, or placed behind a silent receiver state.
  - The party does not need to visit it to understand the urgent route.

### Mirrordune Crystal
- Signal feel: warm, rhythmic, overcharged.
- It points toward the leyline charging site.
- It is the strongest active lead.
- On a good check:
  - Mirrordune is not just relaying commands; it is tied to power movement,
  - charged crystals are being gathered, stabilized, or routed,
  - workers or technicians may still be present,
  - and the signal is growing unstable because the site is preparing to evacuate.
- Table line: `This one is alive. Not loud, exactly, but fed.`

### Heliox Crystal
- Signal feel: faint, layered, municipal.
- It points toward [[Heliox Prime]] and disappears into civic signal noise.
- On a good check:
  - Heliox infrastructure is part of the command architecture,
  - the signal is being hidden under normal city cadence,
  - Mirrordune likely feeds or supports the Heliox-side plan,
  - and going to Heliox with Mirrordune proof will be much stronger than going with Sandglass clues alone.
- Table line: `The pulse is there, but it is wearing the city like a mask.`

## What They Find At Mirrordune
- Mirrordune is the leyline charging station and the final relay in the desert chain.
- It sits on mirrored dunes and low glassy ridges above a shallow leyline vent, with charge cradles sunk into glassy sand.
- Collapsible antenna frames crown the site, angled toward Cinderhook and Heliox while the charging equipment draws power below.
- Its job is to charge refined crystals, mask the operation, and keep controlled workers synchronized until the final shipment clears.
- The Conclave presence is small, technical, and urgent:
  - one relay handler in command,
  - two or three field technicians,
  - one convoy-load coordinator,
  - one portal or escape specialist if you want a named noncombatant,
  - and possibly two controlled Heliox laborers or drivers being used as tools rather than soldiers.
- There should not be a large combat squad unless the party makes a loud approach or you want escalation.

### First View
- A mirrored antenna frame is half-folded, catching sunlight or starlight in hard flashes.
- Three tuning masts point in different directions:
  - back toward the silent Cinderhook frequency,
  - down into the leyline charge field,
  - and toward Heliox-side infrastructure.
- A low relay table is covered in tuning crystals, wrist-device sync clamps, grounding braids, and purge crystals.
- Several charge cradles glow around a shallow leyline vent, each holding or recently holding refined crystals.
- A small transport or crawler sled is loaded with a crate of charged crystals or relay components.
- A portal frame, emergency transit circle, or folded escape arch is being prepared nearby.
- The site sounds wrong: not like conversation, but like a countdown made of crystal hums, relay clicks, and clipped wrist-device gestures.

### Astral Conclave Activity
- They are not trying to hold territory.
- They are trying to finish three things:
  - keep the charge cycle stable until the last crystal crate leaves,
  - purge the command hardware controlling the workers,
  - and destroy Mirrordune's relay hardware before the party can use it.
- The relay crew already knows Sandglass failed and Cinderhook is gone.
- Their default plan is escape and evidence denial, not a stand-up fight.

### If The Party Watches Before Acting
- A relay handler checks a wrist device, then adjusts the mirrored antenna by a few degrees.
- A technician removes a tuning crystal, compares it to the Heliox-side bearing, and packs it into a padded case.
- Another technician arms purge crystals around the relay table.
- A controlled Heliox worker or driver stands too still until given a wrist-device instruction.
- The charged crate is moved toward the transport or portal route.
- The command bolt and stupor-field device are being prepared for destruction once the final crate clears.

### What They Can Interrupt
- Stop the purge of the command bolt or stupor-field device.
- Damage or steal a tuned crystal that cleanly identifies the charge cycle.
- Prevent the blast failsafe from destroying all Mirrordune evidence.
- Knock loose the charged crystal crate before it leaves.
- Capture a relay technician before the escape portal closes.
- Free or stabilize a controlled Heliox worker before the purge sequence catches them.

### If They Arrive Too Late
- The transport has left or the portal has closed.
- The mirrored antenna frame is warped from its own failsafe blast.
- The relay table is slagged, but not as completely as Sandglass because the crew was rushing.
- The party can still find:
  - evidence of a charged-crystal transfer,
  - a half-melted tuning crystal that matches the Mirrordune crystal they recovered,
  - signs that Heliox workers were controlled here,
  - and damaged charge cradles around the leyline vent.

### Evidence At Mirrordune
- Tuning crystals prove Mirrordune was actively linking Cinderhook, Heliox, and its own charge field.
- The antenna geometry shows Mirrordune was masking and stabilizing the charging operation, not merely forwarding military commands.
- Charged crystal residue and leyline stress patterns prove refined crystals were charged here.
- Wrist-device sync clamps show Conclave leaders controlled the site through addressed devices, not open broadcasts the party could casually read.
- Controlled-worker marks show Heliox personnel were being used by the Conclave rather than all willingly collaborating.
- Purge crystal placement proves the crew intended to destroy the relay after the final transfer.
- The work orders, route tags, or dispatch cadence look official enough to pass Heliox systems, implying the Conclave has compromised the city's command structure rather than merely hiding in the desert.

### Mirrordune Choices
- Chase the charged-crystal transport toward Heliox.
- Capture a technician or stabilize a controlled worker.
- Use the Feedback Spike against Mirrordune, risking anyone attached to its active branch.
- Salvage Mirrordune's cleaner tuning hardware before it burns.
- Turn toward Heliox with proof that city-linked infrastructure is being used by the [[Astral Conclave]].

## Heliox Arrival Tone
- [[Heliox Prime]] should feel calm, orderly, and severe.
- The disturbing part is not open battle. It is that the city seems normal while the detector hears the same command cadence inside ordinary civic systems.
- Public dispatches continue at normal intervals.
- Patrols are polite but rigid.
- Workers move on exact schedules.
- No one in authority admits there is a crisis, but citizens are beginning to question the strange activities of their leaders.
- The Heliox-tuned crystal does not point to one villain tower. It pulls toward a command district, then begins catching the same cadence in multiple official signals.

## What They Can Discover In Heliox
- The Heliox-side frequency is being blended into normal [[Civic Directive Lattice]] dispatch cadence.
- The Conclave does not need to command everyone directly. It hides command inserts inside procedures Heliox citizens already obey.
- The Conclave has compromised part of Heliox's command structure. That is how it can authorize relay work, redirect workers, move charged crystals, and make the recharge station look like legitimate Heliox activity.
- Some Heliox personnel are victims, some are unaware functionaries, and a smaller number are active collaborators or compromised supervisors.
- The charged crystals from Mirrordune are likely routed toward a logistics annex, refinement district, or sealed civic reserve.
- Sleeper devices may exist in Heliox, but the more immediate danger is city-scale suggestibility through official systems.
- The city contains the architecture for something worse: if enough energy is forced through the Lattice, the Conclave can emit a citywide control field.

## Evidence In Heliox
- Signal-smoothing hardware installed shortly before the Reclaimer incident.
- Maintenance records for "dispatch harmonization" or "resonance correction."
- A calibration shim with the same tuning geometry as Cinderhook and Mirrordune.
- Reassignment records for refinement workers, including routes that could connect to Selix's memory.

- Official-looking work orders that should not exist:

## What The Compromised Command Structure Explains
- Sandglass Relay could operate as a "survey camp" because Heliox paperwork or dispatch cadence gave it cover.
- Cinderhook could receive retuning hardware and evacuation authority because the compromised system treated the relay chain as legitimate emergency infrastructure.
- Mirrordune could run a leyline charging station because official routes reassigned Heliox workers and moved refined crystals there.
- Charged crystals could move back toward Heliox without triggering normal alarms because logistics systems saw the transfer as authorized.
- Wrist-device orders from Conclave leaders work because they are backed by compromised civic permissions, not because every worker personally trusts the Conclave.
- This means the core mystery in Heliox is not "which villain snuck into the city?" but "which layer of official command has been made to speak with the Conclave's voice?"

## Astral Conclave Presence In Heliox
- The Conclave presence should feel embedded and administrative, not like robed villains in a basement.
- Likely forms:
  - one or two disguised technical advisors attached to maintenance or dispatch,
  - compromised Heliox supervisors with wrist-device instructions,
  - hidden calibration shims installed in civic equipment,
  - sleeper agents positioned near dispatch or refinement access,
  - and a remote handler who can redirect patrol attention without exposing themselves.
- They use Heliox discipline as cover. The city already values order, conservation, and obedience to procedure; the Conclave hides inside that shape.
- The Conclave does not need full political control of Heliox. It needs enough command authority to make illegal activity look like urgent preservation work.

## What The Conclave Is Preparing For The Party
- The [[Astral Conclave]] assumes the party will eventually come to [[Heliox Prime]].
- They do not know exactly when, what evidence the party will bring, or whether the party arrives with Mirrordune proof, the Reclaimer, survivors, or a captured technician.
- At this point, their goal is to kill the party by any means necessary.
- Containment, discrediting, separation, and evidence seizure are still useful, but only as steps toward luring the party somewhere controlled and killing them.
- The Conclave wants the deaths to look like civic defense, Calyxis sabotage, pre-war hardware failure, or tragic preservation necessity.

### Arrival Framing
- If the party arrives with [[The Calyxis Reclaimer]], the Conclave pushes the narrative that they are foreign saboteurs bringing a captured war machine into Heliox territory.
- If the party arrives with Calyxis soldiers, the Conclave frames them as agents provocateurs trying to collapse the truce.
- If they arrive quietly, the Conclave uses checkpoints and administrative delays to learn who is with them and what proof they carry.

  - counterfeit Calyxis provocation,

  - unstable pre-war contamination,

### Controlled Safe Route And Kill Box
- The Conclave prepares an "approved" investigation route that leads the party through monitored spaces.
- This route may include:
  - a civic intake hall,
  - a sanitized maintenance office,
  - a false Lattice access panel,

- Bait:
  - "the real Lattice access point,"
  - a supposed witness in protective dormancy,
  - seized relay hardware from Mirrordune,
  - a way to clear Selix or Varyn,

  - seal exits with civic lockdown doors,
  - flood the chamber with a localized stupor/control field,

## Citywide Takeover Field
- The [[Astral Conclave]] can, if desperate, use the [[Civic Directive Lattice]] to broadcast a citywide control field across [[Heliox Prime]].
- This is not active when the party first enters the city.
- Heliox does not know the city can be taken over this way.
- The Conclave treats it as a last resort because it requires a massive burst of stored energy, likely from charged crystals routed through civic reserve systems.
- It also risks damaging the Lattice, exposing the Conclave's access, and burning through resources they need for larger plans.
- If activated, the field does not need to issue detailed commands to every person. It makes affected warforged/construct citizens receptive, slows independent resistance, and lets addressed wrist-device orders or Lattice commands take priority.
- The horror should be gradual at first:
  - public dispatch bells ring out of sequence,
  - workers pause mid-task,
  - patrols stop speaking and turn together,
  - maintenance doors unlock or seal in waves,
  - and citizens begin obeying directives they do not remember hearing.

### Takeover Field Triggers
- The party exposes the Heliox-side command source in public.
- The party moves to seize or destroy the Lattice access point.
- The party brings proof to an authority who is not compromised.
- The party interrupts the charged-crystal routing from Mirrordune.
- The Sovereign interfaces with the Lattice and the Conclave panics.
- A captured technician or compromised supervisor reveals too much.

### How The Players Can Learn It Exists
- Energy routing records show impossible reserve draw capacity labeled as emergency preservation load.
- A maintenance schematic shows the Lattice can emit through every dispatch tower at once, even though normal dispatches do not require that.
- Mirrordune charge ledgers show too many charged crystals being moved into civic systems for ordinary rationing.
- A sympathetic Heliox technician notices "signal smoothing" upgrades are wired to broadcast, not just receive.
- Selix recognizes a stupor-field geometry scaled up across civic relay towers.
- Varyn remembers a repeated phrase or dispatch cadence that was not meant for ordinary workers.
- The Sovereign can feel an old authority layer in the city refusing the Conclave's newer obedience logic.

## Sovereign Citywide Hijack Option
- The Sovereign can potentially hook into the citywide takeover field if it is active and try to take it over.
- This should be possible only when the field is active or when the party reaches a major [[Civic Directive Lattice]] access point with enough charged-crystal power to open a citywide channel.
- The Sovereign is not issuing ordinary commands. He is uploading an alternate command structure that contradicts the Conclave obedience layer and gives Heliox citizens permission to reject forced directives.
- Best framing: he does not become king of Heliox. He gives the city back its own will by installing a Sovereign-grade refusal structure over the compromised obedience channel.
- Main cost: the Sovereign has to expose his command grammar to the Lattice.
- The Conclave cannot simply control him afterward, but they can study the uploaded structure and use pieces of it.
- The danger is not "they can use the Sovereign." The danger is "they can use anything he taught the city how to do."
- Suggested check structure:
  - DC 18 Sovereign resonance to seize a district-scale active field,
  - DC 20 to overwrite the active citywide obedience layer long enough to free everyone currently affected,
  - DC 22+ to do it cleanly while preventing the Conclave from preserving a usable copy of his command structure.
- Good reveal line: `EXECUTIVE PROFILE ACCEPTED. OBEDIENCE CHANNEL SUPERSEDED. CIVIC WILL RESTORED.`
- Dark follow-up line: `WARNING: EXECUTIVE STRUCTURE MIRRORED IN UNAUTHORIZED NODE.`

## What Not To Reveal Yet
- Do not make Cinderhook the focus unless the players insist; it is a silent proof-node, not tonight's likely destination.
- Do not reveal the full identity of the Heliox access-provider unless you want to accelerate the arc.
- Do not make all Heliox citizens collaborators.
- Do not fully solve the [[Civic Directive Lattice]] in one scene.
- Do not reveal the final destination of all charged crystals unless the party commits hard to the Heliox branch.
- Do not activate the citywide takeover field immediately on arrival. Let the party discover the possibility first, then trigger it only if the Conclave chooses last-resort escalation.

## Good Ending Beats
- The party commits to Mirrordune and reaches the live charging operation.
- They interrupt at least one purge, escape, shipment, or worker-control action.
- They recover proof that Mirrordune is tied to Heliox logistics.
- Selix or Varyn confirms that Heliox infrastructure is implicated.
- The Heliox crystal grows clearer after Mirrordune evidence is recovered.
- End on the road to Heliox, the charged-crystal tracks ahead of them, and the faint city signal becoming a little less faint.
