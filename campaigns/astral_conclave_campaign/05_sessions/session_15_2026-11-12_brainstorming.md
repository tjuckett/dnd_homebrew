# Session 15 Brainstorming - Prize of Wintervale

> **GM preparation, not established canon.** Session 15 begins in the unresolved airship fight. Choose outcomes in response to play; do not assume that the party captures the ship or that every Conclave defender is defeated.

## Session Shape

1. **Finish the airship fight.** Give the party a decisive chance to capture, ground, destroy, or pursue the vessel.
2. **Secure Wintervale.** Let the consequences of the battle land: injured elementals, frightened civilians, a compromised portal circle, and scattered Conclave personnel.
3. **Reveal a concrete next target.** The airship or its commander supplies a route, timetable, and reason to act before the Convergence.
4. **End on a choice.** The party should choose between a short preparation job in Wintervale and taking the new lead toward [[Aetheris Island]].

The recommended next arc is an infiltration of the [[Northern Base]] on Aetheris Island. The Wintervale airship is the bridge: it is waiting to load a physically transported planar-anchor device from the nearby warehouse, and its records reveal the Northern Base is preparing the comet-capture array.

## Opening: Resume the Fight Exactly Where It Stopped

Start with a one-sentence recap, then return immediately to initiative:

> The gangplank bucks beneath you as the Conclave airship strains against its moorings. Below, earth elementals grind through the last resistance at the base; above, the captain's crew fights not to win Wintervale, but to escape it.

Keep the fight focused on two visible stakes:

- **Launch:** if the ship gets free, the party must decide whether to board, ground it, or pursue it.
- **Cargo / command:** the captain, flight log, and sealed cargo are the sources of the next lead.

If the previous session ended on a cliffhanger, do not introduce fresh reinforcements in round one. Let the party resolve the position they earned.

## Mid-Fight Turn: The Geode Command Engine

Use this when the Conclave is losing the dock fight or when the earth elementals are about to overwhelm the remaining crew. It is a desperate countermeasure carried by the [[Astral Conclave Relay Handler]], not a permanent new power that invalidates the party's alliance.

The **Geode Command Engine** is a waist-high brass relay frame containing a cracked, smoky elemental geode and a ring of Conclave signal forks. It was meant to redirect elemental labor at the Northern Base; the Handler can overcharge it into a rough, painful control broadcast.

### Reveal

Give the party one full round of warning before it takes effect:

> The Relay Handler slams a dark geode into the dock console. Every copper fork along its rim begins to hum in the same low note. Across the yard, the earth elementals freeze mid-stride. Dust spills from their joints as they turn, one by one, toward the nearest living creatures.

On the Handler's next turn, if no one interrupts them, the engine activates. The Sky-Captain immediately changes tactics: their crew stops trying to beat the elementals and uses the confusion to launch, escape with the records, or seize the helm.

### What the Engine Does

When activated, each allied earth elemental within 300 feet makes a **DC 16 Charisma saving throw**. An elemental that has a personal tie to the party, has been directly aided during this battle, or is within 30 feet of a party character makes the save with advantage.

- **Success:** It resists the first pulse. It loses its reaction until the start of its next turn as it fights the signal internally.
- **Failure:** It is **commandeered** until the engine is disabled. It is not charmed; this is a hostile resonance command. It treats Conclave personnel and the engine as allies and follows a simple order: *contain the intruders; do not destroy the dock.*

The order matters. Commandeered elementals grapple, restrain, body-block, shove PCs away from the ship, and bury approaches in rubble before they use lethal damage. They protect the device and its line of sight instead of becoming ten extra monsters trying to kill the party.

At the end of each commandeered elemental's turn, it repeats the save, ending the effect on itself on a success. A party character can use an action adjacent to an elemental to appeal to it, ground it with suitable elemental magic, or use a relevant bond; grant advantage on that repeat save or allow an immediate repeat save. Reward any established relationship with the elementals.

### Run It as a Third Side, Not Ten Full Stat Blocks

Divide the ten elementals into two abstract groups of five. Each group acts on one initiative count after the Relay Handler.

| Elemental group action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Seal the route** | Creates difficult terrain in a 20-foot square, blocks the gangplank/gantry, or provides half cover to the Conclave. |
| **Contain intruders** | One creature near the group makes a Strength or Dexterity save against the elemental's normal save DC or is grappled / pushed 10 feet; use this only where the movement has a clear, non-lethal destination. |
| **Guard the engine** | The group blocks access to the dock console and absorbs one hit or spell that would strike the engine this round. |
| **Break free** | If three or more elementals in the group saved, were aided, or succeeded on repeat saves, the entire group shakes off the command and becomes an allied screen again. |

This preserves the feeling of an elemental onslaught without making ten individual turns slow the battle to a crawl.

### The Device Is a Real Objective

Place the engine at the Relay Handler's deck console. The Anchor itself remains in the warehouse, visibly reachable through the open crane door but protected by the two technicians and any commandeered earth elementals that can reach it.

- **AC 15; 45 hit points;** immunity to poison and psychic damage; resistance to nonmagical weapon damage.
- A creature adjacent to it can use an action to attempt a **DC 16 Arcana, Nature, tinker's tools, or thieves' tools** check to disable it. Two successes disable it; a failure by 5 or more causes a harmless force pulse that pushes the creature 10 feet.
- A character can instead use an action to **ground the signal** with *dispel magic* (DC 16), a suitable lightning/thunder/earth magic effect, or a clever physical solution such as severing the copper forks and anchoring the geode. Let a good plan work in one decisive action.
- The Relay Handler can spend an action to repair one failed disable attempt or reactivate the device after it has been disabled, unless the geode itself has been removed or broken.

When the device is disabled, every commandeered elemental immediately gets a new saving throw with advantage. On success, it is free and may use its reaction to move up to half its speed toward the nearest Conclave target or to protect a party member. On failure, it is stunned until the end of its next turn rather than continuing to fight the party.

### Conclave Tactics During the Turn

| Enemy | Dynamic role |
|---|---|
| Sky-Captain | Uses the confusion to advance Launch, order a retreat, or threaten to cut the moorings. They do not waste turns attacking elementals. |
| Relay Handler | Activates and maintains the engine. Once it is threatened, they choose between protecting it and completing emergency extraction. |
| Null Cantor | Uses counterspell, silence, or forced movement to prevent the party from reaching or dispelling the engine; they are the device's bodyguard. |
| Crew | Screens the console, readies nets/manacles, and tries to make the party spend actions rescuing each other. |

### Escalation and Safety Valve

If the party ignores the device for two full rounds, have the engine begin to crack and overload. Announce that it is becoming unstable: it will either collapse the gangway / cargo yard or burn out, ending the control effect, at the end of the following round. This stops the twist from becoming a long punitive fight and forces a decision.

If the party is already struggling, only one group of five elementals fails the initial save. If they are dominating the fight, both groups fail and the Sky-Captain immediately advances the Launch situation. Scale the pressure through objectives and positioning, not by giving the elementals extra damage.

### The Real Cargo: Planar-Anchor Device

The Geode Command Engine is only a field control unit. The airship's intended cargo is the **Aetheric Planar Anchor**, a crane-mounted black-iron and crystal machine still inside the nearby Northshore Transit Warehouse. It is too large and unstable to move by portal—and it cannot safely pass through one because its purpose is to seize and distort planar transit. Attempting to portal it causes destructive feedback at both ends.

The transfer has been interrupted: the Anchor is on a loading sled beneath the warehouse's open crane door, with its hoist chains attached but its weight not yet off the floor. A thick copper-and-crystal cable runs from the Anchor to the Relay Handler's deck console. When the Handler connects the Geode Command Engine to that cable, it amplifies the elemental-command signal and creates a visible pale field over the dock.

- A creature cannot use teleportation or planar travel to enter or leave the dock/airship zone without first succeeding on a DC 16 spellcasting ability check; on a failure, the magic is wasted but the creature remains safe in its original space.
- The Wintervale portal circle is locked in place: it cannot be used for emergency escape, reinforcements, or a bomb trick while the anchor runs.
- The anchor makes the earth-elemental control pulse possible at this scale. Disabling the Geode Command Engine ends the takeover, but the anchor still blocks portal transit until separately shut down.

The anchor is not a bomb by default. If the Conclave is about to lose, the Sky-Captain can order a **rupture overload**: a three-round clock that will destroy the anchor, collapse the dock, and damage the local portal circle. Tell the players plainly that this will be catastrophic for the waterfront, but give them time and multiple ways to stop it: disable the anchor, remove its core, ground it with the earth elementals, or get it clear of the town.

### Payoff

The freed elementals can spend the remainder of the fight holding the dock, pinning the crew, bracing the airship, or preventing a destructive crash. The cracked geode and planar anchor bear Northern Base inventory marks, confirming that the Conclave is developing anti-portal and elemental-command infrastructure for its comet-array operation. This feeds directly into **Operation Starfall** without replacing the party's more immediate airship objectives.

## Airship Endings

| Outcome | Immediate consequence | What the party learns / gains |
|---|---|---|
| **Captured intact** | The crew surrenders, flees, or is restrained; the party has a damaged but flyable vessel. | Flight log, Conclave signal codes, physical cargo, and a route to the Northern Base. |
| **Grounded or crippled** | The ship remains at Wintervale and needs repairs; its crew may escape through the town or portal network. | Cargo and records remain available, but the party needs another ride or a repair scene. |
| **Escapes damaged** | The party saves people, steals a manifest, or marks the vessel; the ship flees north. | A pursuit lead and a narrow window before it reaches the Northern Base. |
| **Destroyed** | Wintervale's dock is damaged and valuable evidence is lost. | A surviving officer, a protected log crystal, or cargo tag still identifies the next target. |

Never make the next adventure depend on one specific outcome. Put the same core truth in at least two sources: the captain, the helm's flight log, a cargo ledger, and a surviving crew member can all point to the Northern Base.

## The Core Discovery: Operation Starfall

The ship is transporting an **Aetheric Planar Anchor** from the [[Northern Base]] for deployment at Wintervale. The Conclave sent it by air because the anchor is too large, too resonant, and inherently unsafe to move through a portal. Its arrival lets the Conclave lock Wintervale's portal circle, suppress escape magic at the dock, and turn the earth elementals against the assault.

The device also reveals the larger plan: the Northern Base is producing and deploying planar-anchor technology to protect the comet-capture array from exactly the sort of interference the party has been causing. The party has stopped a field deployment—but must now decide whether to exploit the airship's route to strike the source.

The party can learn the following facts in layers:

| Evidence | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| Cargo seals or manifest | The anchor was dispatched from **Aetherion Outpost / Northern Base, Aetheris Island**. It is marked as a prototype for anti-portal and elemental-control operations. |
| Helm flight log | The ship has a storm-safe route and a scheduled berth at the Northern Base Sky Dock within a few days. |
| Captain or relay officer | The Northern Base is installing larger anchors around its observatory and Heart Array; without them, the Conclave fears the party can disrupt its comet-capture operation. |
| Encrypted command slate | A small group of living captives / interface subjects has already been transferred to the Northern Base for the Heart Array Vault. |

Do not name every Conclave secret at once. The important player-facing conclusion is simple: **the Northern Base is close to activating something during the Convergence, and this airship gives the party a way to get there before it does.**

### Captives or Moral Pressure

Choose one, based on the party's preferred stakes:

- **Prisoners in the lower hold:** three to six coerced engineers or elemental-interface subjects were being taken north. Rescuing them creates witnesses and an optional insider, but makes a quiet impersonation harder.
- **No prisoners aboard:** the manifest confirms that earlier prisoners have already reached the Heart Array Vault. This keeps the pace fast and gives the island infiltration a rescue objective.
- **A willing defector:** the relay officer knows the Northern Base's sky-dock procedures and agrees to cooperate in exchange for protection from Conclave reprisals. They are useful but not perfectly trustworthy.

## Securing Wintervale: A Short, Meaningful Aftermath

Do not turn the rest of the town into a second siege. Run this as two or three scenes after the airship battle.

### Scene 1: The Earth Elementals Report

The elementals have cleared the remaining base rooms but do not claim that the town is safe. Their report establishes both victory and loose ends:

- The main Conclave garrison is broken or has withdrawn.
- The old town-hall undercroft contains the portal circle, now unattended but still active enough to be dangerous.
- One Conclave team escaped into town carrying a small relay case; it can purge records or call extraction if left alone.
- One or two earth elementals are injured, exhausted, or trapped by a collapse, giving the party a chance to repay their aid.

### Scene 2: Choose One Wintervale Problem

Present one immediate problem, not all of them.

| Problem | Scene | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| **Relay runner** | A short chase through snowy market streets to stop the escaping Conclave team. | Intact command slate or portal codes. |
| **Unstable portal** | The party must secure, disable, or watch the circle while it flashes with an incoming emergency signal. | A usable intelligence asset or a delayed enemy response. |
| **Civilian refuge** | The town's people need protection while the Conclave's remaining guards melt away. | Wintervale accepts the party as liberators and offers supplies, guides, and a safe berth. |
| **Elemental rescue** | A damaged base wing pins allies beneath a collapse near valuable records. | Preserved alliance with Stonehearth and recovered Conclave files. |

### Scene 3: Wintervale's New Status

The town need not become perfectly stable overnight. End with a practical provisional arrangement:

- [[Brenn Ironfist]] organizes repairs, weapons, and dock work.
- [[Ithilwen Moonshadow]] tends to the wounded and speaks for frightened civilians.
- [[Torel Greenthumb]] examines the airship regulator, relay hardware, or portal components.
- Celdric coordinates watches and asks the party to decide what the town does with the portal circle.

This gives Wintervale a face and lets it function as a forward base without consuming the next session.

## The Decision Board

After the aftermath, put three options in front of the players. All are valid; each gives a tangible advantage for Aetheris Island.

| Option | What it is | Benefit for the Northern Base arc | Cost of delay |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fly now** | Take the captured ship or chase its damaged escape route toward Aetheris Island. | Surprise, fresh flight codes, and arrival before the Conclave knows the full scope of the Wintervale defeat. | Wintervale's portal and repairs remain vulnerable. |
| **Secure the portal** | Spend a day decoding, warding, or disabling the town-hall circle. | Prevents an immediate counterattack and may reveal a safe route or other Conclave destinations. | The Northern Base changes its sky-dock codes and tightens inspection. |
| **Prepare a countermeasure** | Work with Torel, the elementals, and any rescued engineer to stabilize the regulator or build a ward-disrupting device. | Counts as one prepared advantage for the Aetheris infiltration, alongside Kira's eventual Blackout Pulse. | The Conclave gains time to replace the lost shipment or move prisoners. |

Make the tradeoff visible, but do not punish the party for protecting Wintervale. A day of preparation should change the island's posture, not erase the lead.

## How the Airship Creates an Entry Plan

If the party captures the ship, it is not an automatic victory over the Northern Base. It gives them one strong approach and forces a choice about disguise, risk, and extraction.

| Asset recovered | Entry advantage | Complication |
|---|---|---|
| Captured airship | May approach the Sky Dock with a legitimate hull, cargo profile, and route. | The base expects a captain and crew; any alert turns the dock into a trap. |
| Flight codes / manifest | Supports forged cargo or a false emergency landing. | Codes expire or are replaced after a delay. |
| Planar anchor | The party can study it, dismantle it for a countermeasure, or exploit its known command signature. | It is dangerous, blocks friendly portal magic, and attracts every Conclave survivor. |
| Defector / captive engineer | Identifies an inspection blind spot, service culvert, or observatory maintenance window. | The NPC has limited access and an understandable fear of being recognized. |
| Damaged escaped ship | Gives a trail to follow through northern weather. | The party must find transport and arrives after the base has some warning. |

## Recommended Default: The Stolen-Ship Deception

Use this if the party captures or grounds the vessel. It is the cleanest continuation because it rewards the Session 14 victory without skipping the challenge of the Northern Base.

The party learns that the airship must return to the Sky Dock in **four days** for refit and debrief. The base expects the prototype anchor's crew but has not yet received a reliable distress report because the Wintervale relay was disabled or jammed during the assault.

The party can fly the ship north under its existing identity. Their initial objective is not to conquer the base; it is to enter, confirm the array's activation timetable, and choose one high-value target:

1. rescue the interface prisoners in the Heart Array Vault;
2. steal the observatory's comet timetable and array plans; or
3. sabotage the phase-regulator installation before it powers the capture array.

At the Sky Dock, give them a **social / infiltration clock** rather than immediate combat:

| Progress | Inspection response |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Routine docking signal and cargo crew request. |
| 1 | An officer asks for captain confirmation and cargo seal. |
| 2 | The ship is directed to an isolated inspection berth. |
| 3 | Captain Frostwing suspects a compromise; the party must escape the dock, bluff hard, or trigger their chosen diversion. |

Advance the clock only for contradictions, delays, or visible magic—not for every imperfect answer. The captured ship creates a real opening, not a disguised scripted failure.

## If the Ship Is Not Captured

Keep the same destination while changing the method.

- **Ship escapes:** A damaged lift crystal sheds a visible astral wake. Celdric or a recovered navigation sextant can follow it to Aetheris Island. The party gets a pursuit / interception scene before choosing another entry route.
- **Ship is destroyed:** A surviving command crystal preserves the route, cargo schedule, and Northern Base berth. Torel can repair it in a day; the party travels by a Stonehearth-guided mountain route, smuggler vessel, or portal-adjacent lead.
- **Ship is grounded but unusable:** Its cargo is still the prize. The planar anchor, manifest, and dock codes enable a forged-cargo or service-culvert plan, while Wintervale's workers repair the ship as a future extraction asset.

## Convergence Clock

Keep the deadline tangible without declaring exact dates the table has not established. At the end of each significant travel day, preparation day, or failed large-scale operation, advance the Convergence by one step.

| Step | Conclave escalation |
|---:|---|
| 0 | The Northern Base expects the shipment and continues normal preparation. |
| 1 | Wintervale's failure is suspected; sky-dock inspections tighten. |
| 2 | The base starts substituting a less stable regulator; the array becomes more dangerous. |
| 3 | Captives are moved closer to the Heart Array Vault and the observatory begins full calibration. |
| 4 | The Convergence is imminent; the party must act with whatever preparation it has. |

Use the clock to change circumstances, never to announce that the campaign's endgame happened off-screen.

## Session 15 End States

A satisfying Session 15 should end with one of these, not a new unfocused list of clues:

- The party is airborne in the captured ship, choosing how to bluff its way into the Northern Base.
- The party has secured Wintervale and selected one preparation job before departing for Aetheris Island.
- The party has a pursuit route to the escaping vessel and a clear reason to reach the Northern Base first.

The final image should point north: a stolen Conclave chart, the planar anchor's pale field snapping shut around the Wintervale portal, or the airship disappearing into an aurora over Aetheris Island.
