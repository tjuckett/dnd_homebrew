# Session 12 Brainstorming - September 17, 2026

## Table-Facing Premise

The party has won a decisive but unstable victory beneath the [[Central Spire]]. Heliox is temporarily helpless while its warforged reboot, the local Astral Conclave cell has been broken but not fully accounted for, and a newly freed companion carries the clearest link yet to the Conclave's elemental-control program.

Session 12 should let the party decide what their victory means: secure Heliox and preserve its evidence, pursue the people trying to erase the truth, or bring the rescued survivor and the Conclave's crimes before the elemental communities at [[Stonehearth]].

## Confirmed Starting Position

- The party is in the Central Spire sub-basement after defeating the immediate Conclave forces in the asset chamber.
- The new player character has been freed from Conclave custody and has joined the group. Their name, history, pact, companions, and goals belong to that player.
- The broadcast signal permanently made all warforged immune to Astral Conclave control.
- The warforged are currently inactive during an approximately eight-hour reboot. The characters may not yet know its duration.
- Heliox's population was incapacitated when last seen from the upper spire. Do not assume their condition or recovery without investigating it at the table.
- Six stations used for the takeover signal burned. A group of roughly six to twenty humans was seen running toward the spire at the same time.
- Stony stopped a newly forming portal with his wrist device.
- The party interrupted technicians repairing the main spire portal for a Conclave escape attempt.
- [[High Curator Malrec Voss]] remains at large. The local cell's immediate forces in the annex have been defeated, but some personnel may have escaped.

## What Session 11 Prep Left Unresolved

The following material from the Session 11 prep was not established in play and should be treated as options to introduce, not automatic facts:

- How Heliox organizes a legitimate response once people wake.
- Whether a Continuity Cell exists, who belongs to it, and whether its members can prove they opposed the Conclave graft.
- The status of Conclave prisoners, technicians, records, and the annex breach gate.
- The identity and purpose of the humans approaching the spire and the people who burned the six stations.
- What the Conclave learned from the elemental-lineage captives, and what happened to the new character's companions.
- Whether the party recovered a prisoner ledger, resonance data, transfer records, a Malrec order, or a route to another Conclave site.
- The next destination: [[Stonehearth]], a portal-rescue trail, or a Conclave escape route.
- Kira's role after the loss of her underwater lab and whether Heliox will permit supervised use of its facilities.

## Session Goals

- Give the new player character a dignified first roleplay scene and prompt return of their equipment.
- Make the warforged immunity a lasting victory while the temporary reboot creates immediate, grounded pressure.
- Let Heliox begin reclaiming itself through local people and institutions rather than installing the party as rulers.
- Turn the burned stations, portal repair, and annex into actionable evidence rather than background mysteries.
- End with a player-chosen next mission and a clear reason to take it.

## Opening: The Room After the Fight

Start quietly. The battle is over, the group is depleted, and the new character has just awakened in the place where the Conclave held them. Give the new player control over their first impression: pain, anger, silence, spellcasting, distrust, recognition, or simple relief.

Nearby, place an evidence locker or sealed specimen cabinet holding the captive's equipment. Return it promptly. Any marks of captivity should be narrative only: cracked stone-like scars, a faded resonance brand, or a damaged focus. Do not impose a lasting mechanical penalty or decide their backstory.

The room can offer three visible exits or objectives:

1. **The breach-gate console:** still warm from the attempted extraction and capable of revealing the escape route or being safely disabled.
2. **The purge archive:** damaged records that can be saved, copied, or allowed to burn while the party prioritizes recovery.
3. **The emergency lift:** a route back toward the waking city, where the party can assess the broader crisis.

This lets the party choose the first consequence of the victory instead of being assigned one.

## Evidence in the Seismic Annex

Award at least one meaningful piece of evidence even if the party did not explicitly protect records during Session 11. The rest can be damaged, missing, or recoverable through play.

Choose one or more:

| Evidence | What it establishes | Lead it creates |
|---|---|---|
| Prisoner ledger | The Conclave classified the captives as `elemental-interface subjects`, not combatants. | Names or descriptions of missing companions; a moral and political case against the Conclave. |
| Resonance readings | Elemental lineage was used as a bridge to force only partial commands onto the four guardians. | A connection to the unstable elemental bindings and to [[Stonehearth]]. |
| Transfer fragment | Other captives left Heliox before the collapse under a codename, route, or facility number. | A future rescue mission; do not establish the companions' fate. |
| Malrec evacuation order | The elemental-control research mattered beyond this local cell. | A safehouse, relay, northern transport route, or name of a higher handler. |
| Portal repair schematic | The main portal needs particular parts, a keyed technician, or an external power route to operate. | A way to prevent another escape or trace people taken previously. |

Keep the recovered material incomplete. It should establish the Conclave's crime and offer a direction, not answer every campaign mystery at once.

## Heliox During the Reboot

The city does not need another citywide control crisis. The recoding is real and permanent. The pressure comes from the gap it has created: the warforged who normally keep systems running are down, civilians are waking with missing time, and the Conclave will exploit confusion to escape or destroy proof.

### Immediate Problems

- Medical stations, lifts, shutters, and power systems need manual crews while warforged operators reboot.
- Controlled citizens wake without memories of the period in which the Conclave used them. Treat that absence as evidence of harm, not guilt.
- The six burned stations may have destroyed vital traces of the takeover signal, but their fires may also expose who had access and why they acted now.
- The human group approaching the spire may be rescuers, a resistance cell, Conclave collaborators, frightened civilians, or a mixture. Give evidence before revealing their allegiance.
- Any surviving Conclave personnel want to escape, erase records, and recover portable research—not to retake the city by brute force.

### A Short Reclamation Sequence

Run two or three scenes, not a prolonged sweep of every district.

1. **Open the route.** The party chooses whether to restore an emergency lift, force a shutter, or use the annex controls to reach the city quickly.
2. **Protect the vulnerable.** A civilian medical station, trapped intake lane, or failing infrastructure needs help before it becomes a casualty count.
3. **Secure the proof.** The party chooses between guarding a prisoner, preserving records, investigating the burned stations, or pursuing an escape route.

Failure should cost time, property, a lead, or an escaping agent. It must not reverse the warforged immunity or declare ordinary citizens complicit.

## Heliox's First Response

If the party wants to stabilize the city, introduce a provisional **Civic Recovery Assembly**. It is an emergency body, not a new permanent government and not the Sovereign's coronation.

Potential members from the Session 11 prep:

| Person | Useful role | Complication |
|---|---|---|
| [[Arch-Calculant Veyrix]] | Civil coordination and resource continuity | His prior policies and secrecy make oversight essential. |
| Nemeris-4 | Archive recovery, evidence, and authentic civic records | Everyone wants the records to favor their version of events. |
| Tavar-9 | Missing-person count, transit, and intake-lane recovery | They may be blamed for acts performed while controlled. |
| Lethra-2 | Guard routes and civilian protection | They may carry guilt over attacks committed under control. |
| A district medic, organizer, or technician | Public trust and a civilian voice | Name this person only when useful at the table. |

The Assembly's first principles should be simple:

- No punishment solely for actions taken under confirmed Conclave control.
- Evidence distinguishes controlled victims, coerced collaborators, and willing collaborators.
- Emergency powers, detentions, and dormancy orders are recorded and reviewable.
- Heliox's people decide their future; the party and newly immune warforged do not rule by default.

### The Human Group and the Burned Stations

Use the group and station fires as the Session's investigation, but do not predetermine their answer. Three viable versions:

- **Resistance responders:** they destroyed the stations to prevent the Conclave from restoring the takeover signal, but their haste damaged evidence and caused the fires.
- **Quiet Violet collaborators:** they burned the stations to erase maintenance logs and ran to the spire to recover agents or research.
- **Mixed group:** several frightened civilians or coerced workers were led by one willing collaborator. This supports a fair evidence-based investigation rather than a simple reveal.

Clues can include a station-access seal, an unaltered neighborhood log, a survivor's testimony, the timing of the fires, and annex records. Do not treat missing memories as proof of guilt.

## Kira and the Annex

Kira remains a useful breach-technology expert, but the loss of her base means she needs access, material, oversight, and trust. If the party asks for her help, a reclaimed Heliox industrial bay can become a **supervised, covert repair site**.

Her limited jobs are:

- Stabilize the permanent warforged recoding without claiming control over them.
- Seal or render safe the annex breach gate and its portal hardware.
- Preserve and decode elemental-control records.

This should be a political choice for Heliox, not a free restoration of Kira's power. If her presence later becomes public, the city can demand accountability for her former Conclave work.

## The New Character's Lead

Use only what the player approves. The Session 11 prep proposed that the captive might be an earth genasi and that their group was taken while seeking elemental allies, but neither point should override the character sheet or player decisions.

If the player adopts the elemental-lineage connection, the annex evidence can establish that:

- The Conclave sought a way to translate its command lattice into partial coercion of powerful elementals.
- The new character was retained because their elemental resonance survived the experiments better than the others.
- Other companions were transferred off-site; they are missing, not automatically dead.
- The Conclave's experiments relate to the disrupted control of the four elemental guardians.

Questions to confirm before play:

- Character name, pronouns, appearance, and any visible elemental traits.
- What the new character remembers of captivity and what they prefer to keep unknown.
- Whether companions exist, and whether any should be alive, missing, dead, or ambiguous.
- Their pact source and whether it should be relevant now.
- Whether they want an immediate connection to [[Stonehearth]] or a different first lead.

## Three Strong Next-Mission Choices

Present these as choices after the immediate Heliox scenes. Keep all unchosen hooks alive.

### 1. Stonehearth: The Resonance Trammel

The party brings the rescued character and annex evidence to [[Stonehearth]]. [[Eldara Stonewise]] is weakened by a Conclave-made resonance trammel drawing energy from her stone runes. [[Crystalmender]] can stabilize her but cannot remove it carelessly.

The trammel connects the annex research to a living elemental leader. The party can choose among a careful extraction, grounding ritual, tracing its stored resonance, or seeking Kira's help. Stonehearth offers diplomacy, consequences for elemental exploitation, and a natural place for the new character to define their connection.

### 2. The Portal Trail: Missing Citizens and Companions

The party follows transfer fragments and portal records before the Conclave destroys the route. This can lead to citizens taken before the portal failure, the new character's missing companions, or a Conclave staging site.

Use this if the table wants a direct rescue and pursuit. Make the first step concrete: a location code, supply convoy, portal component supplier, or captured technician—not a vague promise of a distant facility.

### 3. Heliox: The Quiet Violet Investigation

The party stays long enough to identify who destroyed the stations, protect the annex evidence, and stop the remaining voluntary Conclave network from escaping through the confusion.

Use this if the group wants consequences, investigation, and civic politics. The mystery must be resolved through records, access, and behavior, never by punishing people for missing memories.

### 4. Dapplefern Refuge: The Tabaxi Captive Trail

A surviving Shadow Fang transfer record, a familiar village token, testimony from [[Kasha "Whisperclaw"]], or Sylas's owl leads to [[Echoes of Dapplefern]]. The party can investigate what became of the Tabaxi player character's family, find the hidden Dapplefern Refuge, and earn allies through a concrete act of protection rather than a request for soldiers.

The refuge will not help at first. Chief Sable Reedwhisper asks the party to protect Dapplefern from the Unpaid Hunt of [[The Thorn-Crowned Stag]] and establish what became of the Tabaxi character's family before the council considers any support. Success provides one Aetheris advantage: a Storm Harbor route, forged cargo identities, reconnaissance, a safe extraction point, or a prisoner-rescue objective. Confirm the family outcome with the player before revealing it.

## Sylas: The Door That Is Not Yet Open

Use [[Sylas]] when the party begins seriously considering an immediate strike on [[Aetheris Island]]. He should not physically prevent the journey, declare it impossible, or tell the party what they must do. His role is to reveal the cost of going unprepared and point toward the missing piece without explaining the whole answer.

If the party chooses the Dapplefern hook first, use the owl-and-Tabaxi-token trail in [[Echoes of Dapplefern]] as this intervention's first step. Sylas need not appear twice: the later vision can occur after the refuge reveals the Unpaid Hunt and the party has a reason to understand what preparation for Aetheris requires.

### The Omen

An owl familiar appears in the damaged annex or on an exterior spire rail. It waits until the party names the island, then leads them to a darkened transit platform, a quiet repair bay, or another moment away from the immediate crisis. Sylas is already there.

> "You have broken one chain. Do not mistake the broken link for the hand that holds it."

He shows them three brief, ambiguous images:

1. A frozen island beneath a comet-bright sky; the party reaches its walls, and every automaton turns toward them before they cross the gate.
2. An amber light draining from a stone figure while a black metal rod hums in time with the island's wards.
3. The same island, but its defenses falter for a single breath as a pulse of grounded amber resonance passes through its foundations.

The vision establishes an inference, not a command: the island can be attacked now, but its elemental-detection wards and automaton response make that route likely disastrous. The resonance trammel at [[Stonehearth]] and Kira's proposed Blackout Pulse may be connected to the advantage the party lacks.

### What Sylas Can Say

Sylas may say:

> "The north is not the wrong road. It is the road that kills you if you take it carrying only victory."

> "There is a wound in stone that remembers the shape of their lock. Heal it, study it, or carry its lesson north. But do not ask me which choice costs least. I have seen too many answers become cages."

> "If you go now, you may still win something. I have not seen you bring it home."

He cannot confirm a single future, name the best route into Aetheris, reveal Malrec's full plan, or guarantee that Stonehearth will solve the problem. The players may still choose the island immediately; honor that decision and run it as a high-risk infiltration with fewer advantages.

### Why This Works

- It preserves the party's victory and momentum instead of inventing a new barrier to block them.
- It gives an in-world reason for the existing heist trigger: a precise target, entry plan, and prepared advantage.
- It makes [[Stonehearth]] relevant as preparation, not as a detour imposed by the GM.
- It keeps Sylas unsettling and useful without turning him into a quest giver or prophecy vending machine.

## Suggested Ending Beats

End the session on a committed direction rather than a complete resolution:

- The last warforged reboot indicator changes from critical to stable, confirming the immunity held.
- The Assembly publicly adopts a first principle protecting control victims from automatic blame.
- An annex record yields the next destination or the identity of a surviving accomplice.
- The new player character chooses whether to share a personal lead, remain guarded, or simply travel with the party.

## GM Guardrails

- The recoding victory is real and durable. Do not restore Conclave control to manufacture another emergency.
- Heliox belongs to its residents. Let them reclaim it alongside the party.
- Do not make the new player character an exposition device, a reward, or a permanent experiment victim.
- Do not decide their backstory, pact, companions' fate, or reason for staying with the party.
- Keep the Astral Conclave's local defeat meaningful: it loses personnel, infrastructure, secrecy, and leverage even though Malrec remains free.
- Use evidence before exposing a named Heliox resident as a willing collaborator.
